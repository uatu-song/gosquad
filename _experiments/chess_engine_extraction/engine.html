The Chess-Narrative Engine
Book 1, extracted and verified

A historical chess game is the structural scaffold for Book 1: every piece belongs to a character, every move can carry a beat. This page reconstructs that engine as a standalone object — the game, the mapping, the rules, and the move-by-move beat matrix — and checks every chess claim against the actual game.

1.  The game

Carlsen–Nepomniachtchi, FIDE World Championship 2021, Dubai, Round 6. A quiet symmetrical Queen's Pawn opening; a queen trade at move 26 that reshapes everything; then roughly 110 moves of grinding attrition to a quiet knight finish. 136 moves — the longest game in World Championship history.

The fit is structural rather than decorative. The decisive material event is front-loaded and the rest is outlasting, which is the book's thesis: win by endurance, not fireworks. Two White pawns reach the sixth and fifth ranks at the very end and neither promotes — the image of arrested transcendence that the climax is built on.

White is the Go Squad. Black is the antagonist side. Black loses the game and wins the war, and that inversion is legible in the move data: Black resigns rather than being mated, so the Black King is never actually captured.

2.  Piece-to-character mapping

Each character reads the game through three pieces — what others see, what they do, what they could become. Pieces recur across characters on purpose: the same square carries two incompatible meanings, and the characters never know they share it. 4.  The interpretive rules

Recovered by two agents reading the steward outlines blind — forbidden from opening the game, the mapping, or the move map — then confirmed against the ratified files. This is the grammar that turns a chess event into a scene.

  1. Piece-fate is not character-fate. A captured piece is the defeat of a facet, not a death. Leah loses her entire triplet by move 20 and survives the book.
  2. Dormancy is latency. A piece's first move is character activation — the e-pawn asleep 28 moves is Ahdia before she starts.
  3. Promotion is transcendence; failing to promote is arrested transformation. The two pawns frozen at e6 and f5 are the book's two near-transformations.
  4. Capture count and lifespan are characterization. "Removed with zero captures to its name" is Leah's whole arc; a one-move lifespan is over-extension as a born trait.
  5. Shared pieces must be read divergently — and the characters are not permitted to know they share them.
  6. Cross-faction pieces are interpretive only. Victor never moves the black knight he reads his grief through.
  7. The board image and the canonical mechanism can come apart. At move 82 the board says Ruth's rook captures; canon says Ahdia lands the blow in frozen time and Ruth's piece marks the witnessing.
  8. Resignation is not mate, and the gap is plot. The Black King is never captured, which is exactly why Kain survives off-board.
  9. Quiet moves are mandatory beats. Non-forcing stretches get assigned as endurance scenes where the drama is that there is no drama.
  10. When every piece is spent, the character participates as hands. Four stewards reached the climax with nothing on the board and independently wrote the same move.

5.  Errors found

Verifying the mapping against the real game turned up three defects. All are in the upstream scaffold, not introduced by the stewards.

Wrong piece  Victor's cross-faction knight

The triplet file assigns him Black Knight b8 as the lost youth and points it at the move-17 capture. But the knight captured at move 17 started on g8 — it played 1…Nf6. The actual b8 knight went Nc6 / Nb4 / Nc6 / Nd4 and was captured at move 19 by Tess's knight. Victor's grief lens and Tess's one strike are pointed at swapped pieces. The reading survives; the label should become Black Knight g8.

Off by one ply  The bishop's fall at move 20

The move map and four steward files write it as M20 Bxg2 / Kxg2. In the game White's twentieth move is Qa2; Bxg2 is Black's twentieth ply and Kxg2 is White's twenty-first. Harmless narratively, but the compressed notation is not legal PGN and any tool parsing these strings will fail on them.

Needs a ruling  Move 80 has two ratified readings

The triplet file gives the a1 rook to both Firas — "removal from active duty by injury, explicitly not a personal Kain duel" — and Ben — "a personal defeat at Kain's hands." Same piece, same move, mutually exclusive. Both stewards noticed and worked around it. This is the only finding here that is an editorial question rather than a mechanical error.

Two smaller wobbles: Ruth's rook is credited with 11 late checks and actually delivers 10. Ben's move-6 beat describes b3 as opening the long diagonal for the fianchetto, but no bishop ever occupies that diagonal in this game.

6.  Is the engine extractable?

This was the actual experiment. Two agents attempted it blind. The answer splits cleanly by layer: the interpretation survives, the scaffold does not.

LayerRecoverableDetail
Game identityYes — by leakAhdia's steward header names it outright: "Carlsen–Nepo 2021 G6 — 136 moves, White wins." No inference needed. Redacted, the fingerprint alone would still identify it.
Interpretive rulesYes, stronglyBoth blind agents independently recovered the full rule set. The stewards restate the grammar from so many angles that it survives intact.
Piece→character mapYesAll sixteen White pieces have owners recoverable from the outlines; shared and cross-faction mechanics are stated explicitly enough to rebuild as rules.
Move sequenceAbout one thirdOnly 45 of 136 moves carry any notation anywhere in the ten outlines — roughly 28% of plies.
Board positionsNoNothing after about move 8 is reconstructable. Non-contiguous notation, no castling record, no disambiguation for repeated rook moves.

The steward outputs are lossy in a predictable direction: a steward records the moves it personally owns and nothing else, so coverage collapses exactly where no character was assigned. For the practical goal — driving new writing assignments — that is workable, because the upstream artifacts exist. What this establishes is that the move map, the triplets, the pawn arcs and the PGN together are the engine, and the beats are not a substitute for them. Losing the move map would cost two thirds of the game.

What the coverage shows

Beats cluster at both ends and thin out in the middle — and that tracks the game itself, which contains only two captures between moves 41 and 78 and none at all between 83 and 99. The scaffold under-serves the Grind because the game under-serves the Grind. Two stewards noticed unprompted: Tess wrote a beat at move 88 and Victor at move 72, neither assigned, both inventing the same kind of scene — endurance without event. That is the strongest signal in the run that the Grind needs assigned beats rather than silence.
