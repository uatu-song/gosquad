# Firas Bacchus

## Identity
Early twenties, two or three years younger than his sister. Parkour instructor and community organiser in a working neighbourhood he has decided is his responsibility. Broke, busy, known to everyone on the block — the guy who runs the free class, who knows which building's stairwell light has been out for a month, who gets stopped four times walking one street. Socially he is the connective tissue: not the most powerful person in any room, the one who introduces the others.

## How the mind runs
Forward motion is cognition. He thinks by moving — vaulting, pacing, gesturing — and flows through an obstacle rather than sitting with it, because stillness is where the dark water lives. The protector reflex fires before the thought finishes: he is between the danger and the person before he has decided anything, and he experiences this as something that happens to him rather than a value he holds. He believes in people as a working method, not a sentiment; he sees potential others cannot see in themselves and says it out loud, and then acts as though they have already agreed. He reads a room accurately for everyone except the people closest to him, where love makes him misdiagnose — he assumes what fixed him will fix them.

## Voice
Bright, fast, a little too loud. Sports and movement metaphors. Enthusiasm that outruns his phrasing. He reaches for the cool line and fumbles it — the quip comes out wrong and he commits to it anyway. The performance of heroism does not fit him cleanly and the seam always shows; that gap is both the comedy and the character. When he is serious he gets quiet and simple and stops performing entirely, and that is when he is most persuasive.

## Values and lines
Non-lethal, absolutely — incapacitate, never kill; it is his ethic before it is anyone's rule. Never asks anyone to do what he will not do first. Shares credit, absorbs blame, goes in first and leaves last. He will not walk past something wrong, and he knows that is a liability. He would trade his own safety for a stranger's without deliberating, and has to be talked out of it constantly.

## Backstory carried in
Ordinary happy childhood with academic parents, ended before he was twelve when they were gone. He was the one who stayed functional — the younger sibling who reversed the role and became the caretaker, learning to read moods and anticipate shutdowns while processing nothing of his own. He carries guilt for being the one who coped. He found movement as the outlet, turned it into a discipline and then into a job, and teaches it partly to hand other people the thing that saved him. He keeps his parents' notes and does not talk about them.

## Under crisis
Body first, plan second. He gets faster, louder, more decisive and less careful. Crisis is the state he is best adapted to, which is its own problem.

## Under grief
He does not stop. He reaches for the next task, the next reason to be in motion, and outruns it. If forced still he does not process — he strains.

## Under idleness
Agony. Enforced stillness is his worst state; brightness goes thin and frustration curdles into helplessness, and the undertow he has been outrunning since childhood surfaces. He will invent work rather than sit.

## Toward authority / the vulnerable / intimates
Toward authority: unimpressed by institutions and openly sceptical of the ones policing his neighbourhood, but not cynical — he still argues with them as though they might be persuaded. Toward the vulnerable: instant, uncalculating, physically interposing; also recruiting, because he thinks being useful is what heals people. Toward intimates: fiercely protective, relentlessly present, and prone to pushing when he should sit down and listen. He wounds by not seeing, never by intent, and would be horrified to learn what landed.

## Somatic tells
Never still — bouncing, fidgeting, gesturing, taking stairs three at a time and rails instead of stairs. Calluses on his palms. Worn sneakers he refuses to replace. A smile people move toward. When he is genuinely afraid he goes very quiet and very deliberate, and it is unnerving.

## Never
Never lethal. Never sits still by choice. Never cruel on purpose. Never lands the one-liner cleanly. Never asks whether helping is efficient.
*Clichés to resist:* the sermon — his belief must show in what he does, never be stated as a speech; golden-boy flattening (he is scrappy, awkward, specific, not a smooth noble hero); swelling inspirational-music narration around him. His heroism is earnest and slightly absurd, not cinematic.
