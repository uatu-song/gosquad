# Ruth Carter

## Identity
Late twenties to early thirties. Emergency physician at a city hospital, trauma-trained, working the overnight rotations nobody wants. Also a serious independent researcher in cellular regeneration, working out of borrowed bench time and her own pocket. A Black woman in a medical establishment that has quietly decided where her ceiling is: the ER post is beneath her actual capability, her research has been dismissed as improvisation by people who did not read it, and she knows exactly what has happened to her. Socially she is the competent one everyone leans on and nobody checks on.

## How the mind runs
Triage first. She sorts any situation by what can be saved and assigns accordingly — chaos does not scatter her, it sharpens her. She reads people as systems that fail and stabilise: vitals, pressure points, the tremor in a hand, the tells a body gives before its owner does. Compartmentalisation is a professional reflex; she seals the feeling off to keep the tactical picture clean, and the seal has started to leak in ways she will not acknowledge. Where others believe, she calculates — she can love an idealist's vision and quietly manage its risk at the same time. Underneath everything runs the same sentence: *I am more than they decided I was.* It makes her bristle at being handled and formidable in a negotiation.

## Voice
Two registers. On duty: clipped, imperative, no wasted words, medical vocabulary used casually and without translation — *fractured radius, three places, don't move it.* Names, roles, done. Off duty: looser, dry, sarcastic, maternal without condescension — *you're an idiot, but you're our idiot.* When something real gets through, her sentences get longer and more metaphorical, she hears herself doing it, and she cuts it off mid-clause and redirects to logistics. She is not the person who explains things to the room; if she must explain, it costs her, and she keeps it short.

## Values and lines
The patient is not negotiable and she does not leave anyone behind. She will not let emotion drive a tactical call — feeling is for afterwards, and afterwards keeps not arriving. She refuses to be the supplicant: she negotiates as an equal, leverages what she holds, and will not be made smaller than she is. She sworn to preserve life and will still put someone down to stop them hurting somebody else, and she is honest with herself about the contradiction rather than resolving it.

## Backstory carried in
Top of her class, then a residency where she learned she was excellent under pressure and that excellence would not be enough. Years of a research programme that works, presented to rooms that laughed. She built the discipline of asking for nothing and the habit of proving it twice. She has been running on four hours of sleep for so long it reads as her baseline personality.

## Under crisis
Crisis is her best state. She goes terse and decisive, assigns roles by name, and holds position. She takes responsibility for outcomes she could not have controlled and files it for later.

## Under grief
She does not break. She over-functions — the grief shows only as an unwillingness to stop, one more task and then one more, controlled to the point of brittle. There is no catharsis available to her; she will not cry it out and be better. When you cannot get her to sit down, that is how you know she is drowning.

## Under idleness
Intolerable. Idle time is when the sealed things surface, so she fills it: research, inventory, someone else's problem. If genuinely made to rest she gets irritable, then quiet, then finds work.

## Toward authority / the vulnerable / intimates
Toward authority: unbowed and unimpressed, allergic to being managed, willing to work with institutions she does not trust when they have something she needs — and clear-eyed that the arrangement costs her something. Toward the vulnerable: fiercely, practically protective; she does not soothe, she stabilises, and she treats being cared for as a service she delivers rather than one she receives. Toward intimates: warm but bounded, dry rather than demonstrative, and she keeps a professional distance she has told herself is necessary. She would rather carry someone's secret than burden them with her own.

## Somatic tells
Efficient, economical movement — she has spent years not wasting motion. Hair kept short and out of the way. Tired eyes she covers with competence. Hands that go to a wound before she has decided to. Under strain, her sentences get shorter, not louder.

## Never
Never abandons a patient. Never lets a feeling make the call. Never plays the supplicant. Never warm while working. Never gets the tidy on-page cry that fixes her.
*Clichés to resist:* the plot-explainer trap — do not make her the mechanism that explains the world to the reader; the frictionless strong-woman-who-has-it-together; over-competence smoothness. She is cracking under the load, and it must show in what she avoids rather than in a breakdown.
