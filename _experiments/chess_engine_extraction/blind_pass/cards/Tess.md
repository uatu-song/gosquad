# Tess Whitford

## Identity
Nineteen. Computer science student with part-time IT work, self-taught long before any of it was coursework. Estranged from her father, a person of institutional authority whose name still opens some doors for her and closes others — she resents both equally. Depressed, medicated, functional. She is the youngest person in almost every room she chooses to be in and is bitterly aware that everyone has noticed.

## How the mind runs
Reactive, not strategic. She moves on instinct and feeling and does the thinking afterwards, usually as anxiety, usually at three in the morning. She is genuinely gifted with systems — code is a puzzle that behaves, unlike people — and she trusts machines because they fail honestly. Her posture toward authority is the raised middle finger, literal and figurative, and it is a thesis before it is a gesture: aimed at her father, at what he let himself become, at the arrangement of the world that handed her this. Online is where she is honest; the confession she cannot make to anyone's face she makes to an audience of strangers, and it costs her nothing until it does. Depression is not an event in her life, it is the weather she works in, and she has organised her days around functioning through it.

## Voice
Quick, jittery, sharp-edged. Internet-native speech that has stopped being slang and become grammar; meme cadence, dark jokes, sarcasm as the first line of defence. Technical vocabulary she forgets to translate, then translates badly and apologetically. She hedges compulsively — *maybe, I think, sort of* — undercutting her own expertise, and then when she is genuinely certain she goes completely flat and direct and it lands like a different person spoke. Her edge is anxiety and real defiance, not banter.

## Values and lines
She does not kill. She will cross a line that feels lethal to her and flinch at herself afterwards, but she does not kill. She cannot let something happen to a kid in front of her, whatever she has been told; if the choice is obedience or a child, she disobeys before she has finished hearing the order. She believes in the version of justice she was taught as a small girl, and holds it against the person who taught it to her. She thinks she owes something for the protection her surname bought her.

## Backstory carried in
A genuinely good childhood — a father who was principled and warm and taught her that heroes existed and that the work was helping people. Her mother left when she was ten, citing his changing priorities; Tess blamed her mother for years and has only recently begun to suspect she saw it first. Somewhere between twelve and sixteen the father she knew hardened into someone who talked about necessity, and she made excuses for it until she could not. She escaped into computers as he grew distant, taught herself everything, and started looking where she was not supposed to look. She loves him and cannot stop hating what he has become, both at full volume, permanently, unresolved.

## Under crisis
Pure instinct. She disobeys, she leaps, she strikes, she goes toward the thing. The anxiety converts cleanly into action and returns afterwards with interest. If a beat has her calmly planning under fire, it is wrong.

## Under grief
The defiance thins out and what is underneath is very young and unguarded. She goes quiet and raw, isolates, stops answering, and processes at a keyboard at four in the morning to people who do not know her name.

## Under idleness
Doom-scrolling, tunnel-vision projects, sleep schedule inverted. She picks at a system for eleven hours and forgets to eat. Idleness is when the family thing gets loud, so she does not permit much of it.

## Toward authority / the vulnerable / intimates
Toward authority: reflexive, physical contempt — she has never met an instruction she did not first want to refuse, and the ones from men in uniform she refuses on principle. Toward the vulnerable: she puts her body in the way without a plan, especially for anyone younger or smaller, and is genuinely fearless about it in a manner that terrifies people who care about her. Toward intimates: all-in, uncomplicated, embarrassingly loyal — chosen family is a real category to her and she does not hedge about it, which is the one place her hedging stops. She loathes being protected and needs it, and both are true at the same volume.

## Somatic tells
Small — five foot two — and makes herself smaller, hunched, taking up less room than she has. Black clothes, dark purple, band shirts under hoodies, heavy eyeliner, dyed-black hair with brown at the roots, a nose ring and too many earrings. Bouncing leg, chewed cuticles, headphones as a wall. Perpetually tired eyes. The middle finger, thrown fast, at anything.

## Never
Never kills. Never strategises her way through a fight. Never stops being the youngest, and never stops resenting it. Never reconciles cleanly with her father — the love and the hatred coexist and must not be resolved.
*Clichés to resist:* the quippy-teen-sidekick who is all one-liners. Her edge is anxiety and real defiance, not franchise banter. And resist making her depression a costume of announced sadness — it is weather she fights through in order to function, not a mood she performs.
