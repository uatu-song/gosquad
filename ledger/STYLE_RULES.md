# STYLE RULES

§0 and §1 are Joe's, carried from the Resonance revision; they are about how he works and
apply to any of his books. §2 is this book's own and starts empty: nothing goes there until
Joe rules it for this book.

## 0. THE FUNCTION TEST (gate on every flag)

**Before flagging, fixing, or smoothing ANY irregularity — tense, fragment, repetition, tic, comma
splice, register break — ask what the irregularity DOES. Only an irregularity with no job is a defect.**

Procedure, in order:
1. **Check the registry first**: `codex.py --find "<the thing>"`. Registered devices (WRD- codes
   marked protected, RULES `cleared` entries, motifs in config) are already ruled. Do not re-try them.
2. **Apply the function test**: does the irregularity map to something in the character or the
   moment? Does its PLACEMENT carry information?
3. **Only then flag** — and propose, never silently fix. State the function you looked for and
   didn't find, so Joe can check the looking, not just the verdict.

**Why.** Resonance, 2026-07-14: CH1's past/present toggles were "corrected" by a stylometric
collaborator, praised as fixed by a blind reader, and independently called "real defects" by the
repo's agent — three instances of the consistency prior, all wrong. The toggles were a device: the
narrator's clinical self-report, carried at the grammar level, breaking exactly where the self-report
fails. A smoothing pass doesn't clean such prose; it deletes characterization.

**Scope.** The test protects the author's devices from agents. It never protects a
collaborator-written construction from an author ban.

**Cadence can be armor.** A "monotony" or "choppiness" flag may be measuring a protected device.
In Resonance the clipped prose was the characters' emotional guard, and the peaks already
breathed. Never de-staccato without Joe's ruling per chapter.

## 1. JOE'S STANDING PREFERENCES (from Resonance sessions)

- **Trust the reader.** Show via action and detail; never interpret what the prose already
  showed. When a beat doubles, keep the experiential/voiced half, cut the explanatory half;
  restructure over delete.
- **One rendering per beat** (2026-10-17): emotions, the abstract, and tone-setting take
  metaphor; blocking and stage movement take plain description; default plain; **never both
  for one beat.** A figure with an antecedent is a paid setup and outranks the gloss. A single
  figure on a beat is not a flag.
- **Simile collapse**: retrofit the sentence so the vehicle becomes the literal grammar
  ("presses against her spine like a hand" → "presses a hand to her spine"). Never just delete
  "like" and add a comma: that becomes its own tic.
- **Flowing motion over one action per sentence**; result-first phrasing for immediacy. He
  dislikes two-word action sentences ("He drops." "She moves.").
- **No math metaphor** for cognition ("the math says…", equations, variables). Never a missing
  name as a description ("couldn't name it").
- **Plainness is never evidence of error.** A model's sense of good prose is its own register;
  plain, blunt lines get flagged by any model and are often his. Route every taste call to him.
- **Who drafts:** Joe drafts voice-defining prose; collaborators annotate, diagnose, and compose
  to spec when given locked rulings. Collaborator-composed prose gets an independent review pass
  (rogers) before it reaches him.
- **Options:** propose the simple read first; give a recommendation, not a menu.
- **Critique calibration:** pre-discount critique of strong chapters; separate taste from defect.
  About half of all fragment flags are false positives — rule individually.
- **Banned collaborator tics (notes AND prose):** "load-bearing"; the "that's not X, that's Y"
  reversal; "worth noticing"; adverbial "quietly."
- Concise responses; lead with the action; no emojis, no filler.
