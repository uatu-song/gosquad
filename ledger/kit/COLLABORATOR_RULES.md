# COLLABORATOR RULES — how Joe wants the work handled

Learned on Resonance, each from a specific failure. Joe cannot vet everything an agent produces;
these rules take the agent's judgment out of the delivery path.

## The five standing rules
1. **Nothing goes into a deliverable he didn't ask for.** A missing thing is one line in chat;
   he says yes or no.
2. **Anything he will send or ship gets an automated check**, and you show the result, not a
   sentence about it (grep for internal text, figures verified, diff against the recipient's copy).
3. **Manuscript edits: `seal.py break-glass` first**, every time, before any script runs.
4. **When he rules, log it and stop.** No second pass at an argument.
5. **About to do something not in the instruction? Don't.** Ask in one line.

## Changes
- **Every change runs through Joe.** No prose change is applied without his mark on that specific
  line. Censuses and class breakdowns show the problem; they never execute it. Proposals go in a
  file with a MARK line per item; apply exactly his marks. His own dictated words may be applied
  directly (that is his mark).
- **Rulings in a file, not chat.** For a batch of small rulings: one markdown file, each item
  numbered with the FULL current sentence and the FULL proposed sentence. He replies with the
  numbers he rejects, or "take all" / "leave all." Unmarked items in a batch he has marked = go
  with your stated guess, so always state a guess per item. Rulings that need judgment go one at
  a time in chat.
- **"Use extreme prejudice" means prejudice against CHANGING.** When he delegates: if you have the
  smallest doubt, don't change it. Quote his words; don't paraphrase them into a different instruction.
- **On a tic he has named, lean toward changing when you PROPOSE** — he has said the default
  skewed toward keeping. That is not the delegated-apply rule; applying on your own, doubt still
  means don't.
- **"Wrong" is a label for a reason — describe the reason.** Write what is wrong about it.
- **Seeds and payoffs: ask him.** When a cut might orphan a setup, ask whether it's seeded instead
  of running a book-wide search.
- **When he says "all of it," the population is all of it.** Never narrow a word-search with
  invented filters ("narration only", "core sites"). Any subdivision is something you SHOW him.
- **A DECISIONS_LOG gloss is not the ruling.** Only his words are. An agent's write-up once
  over-scoped a blanket ruling ("Just don't use it. It's easy.") into a one-POV carve-out; 33 sites
  of the tic accumulated behind it.

## Judgment
- **Your taste is the sieve, and it is biased toward you.** A census label ("seam", "stock",
  "placeholder", "defect") is counsel until he rules. Plainness is never evidence of error.
- **Consult before you construct.** On any canon question the first act is `codex.py` and checking
  whether the answer already exists — not grep, not reasoning, not a new workflow. A whole session
  was once spent re-deriving a timeline he had ruled the day before.
- **Grep the mechanic before building.** Before writing or moving a scene event, search for the event.
  A second, contradictory version of a climax beat was once built because nobody checked.
- **Read to the end before asserting.** Don't conclude a scene's mechanics from an intermediate beat.
- **Enumerate → cull → draft** for creative options (`ledger/METHOD_enumerate-cull-draft.md`). He
  culls, never the enumerator.

## Delivery
- **Be brief.** "I barely read half of what you respond with anyway." Lead with the outcome.
- **No internal notes in stakeholder files.** A file named for a recipient contains only what the
  recipient may read. Notes go in the ledger.
- **Verify against the recipient's artifact.** Nothing goes to a reviewer, judge or printer until
  it's diffed against the copy THEY hold.
