Em-dash after "Thank you" → comma. Your version reads as a continuous beat ("Thank you, both for X and for Y") rather than an interruption. Smoother in correspondence voice.
Em-dashes around "I've been reading you as an author, not just a scholar" → period split. Breaking it into two sentences gives the assertion its own beat instead of treating it as a parenthetical aside. The claim earns standalone weight.
Em-dash before "what remains" → semicolon. The semicolon does the "and here's the definition" work without the dramatic pause an em-dash creates. More clinical, more confident.
Em-dashes around "the politics of what gets erased and what refuses" → parentheses. Visually quieter. Reads as a clarifying gloss rather than an emphatic break.
Pattern: you're swapping em-dashes for punctuation that's either smoother (commas, semicolons) or visually quieter (parentheses). The cumulative effect is the email reads less performative and more like one writer talking to another at the same level.
