# STEWARD OUTLINE: Ruth Carter + Tess Whitford
## ♘ Knight (b1) — Paired Unit — Moves 3–24

---

## PRE-GAME — THE LAST NORMAL MORNING

**What happens:** Ruth is at the hospital. Twelve-hour shift ending. She's checking her phone between patients — three missed calls from Firas, a text from Ahdia she hasn't opened. Tess is across town, sitting in her car outside the police precinct where her father works, engine running, not going in. She's been parked there for eleven minutes. Her Sertraline is in the glovebox. She took it this morning. She thinks about whether it's working. She decides that's not a question with an answer.

**What it means:** These two don't know they're about to become a unit. Ruth is still operating as field leader — hierarchy, triage, responsibility flowing downward. Tess is still the youngest member, the one people check on. They exist in separate orbits with separate gravities. Ruth's gravity is obligation. Tess's gravity is the precinct she can't enter and can't leave.

**Connection forward → M3:** The call that pulls them both in comes from the same source. Whatever it is, it ends Ruth's shift and starts Tess's engine. They arrive at the same place at the same time, and from this point forward, they don't separate.

---

## M3 — Nc3: b1 → c3
### DEPLOYMENT — "We're Here"

**What happens:** Ruth and Tess deploy together to an operational position. Ruth takes field command — establishes comms, runs a quick assessment. Tess is at her shoulder, quiet, watching the field. The team is spread across the board. Leah and Victor are pushing forward aggressively. Ben is anchored. Ahdia is nowhere visible yet. Ruth checks the positioning and doesn't like how far forward Leah is.

**What it means:** Ruth defaults to control. She reads the field the way she reads a trauma bay — who's bleeding, who's stable, who's about to code. Tess is processing something different. She's watching the *structure*, not the people. She sees the geometry of the thing before Ruth does. But she doesn't say anything. She's not sure her read matters when Ruth's in command.

This is the version of their partnership that needs to break: Ruth leads, Tess follows. Leader and sidekick. Doctor and patient. The hierarchy is comfortable and it's going to get people hurt.

**Connection back ← Pre-game:** Ruth brought her hospital instincts. Tess brought her silence. Both are liabilities they don't know about yet.
**Connection forward → M4:** Ruth's read was right — Leah is too far forward. But being right doesn't help.

---

## M4 — WITNESSED: Leah + Victor's Incapacitation
### JUNCTURE 1 — "The Trap at c4"

**What happens:** Leah and Victor push into a prepared position and it collapses on them. Ruth sees it from c3 — close enough to witness, not close enough to intervene. Leah goes down. Victor survives but is separated, migrating toward Ben's position. Ruth's comms pick up the sound of the incapacitation. Tess is beside her and sees Ruth's face change.

**What it means for Ruth:** She knew. She saw the positioning and she *knew* Leah was overextended, and she didn't pull rank, didn't order a retreat, didn't say "fall back" in time. The ER surgeon who triages in seconds hesitated on the field. Ruth will carry this. Not as guilt — Ruth doesn't do guilt, she does *responsibility*. She files it: I saw it coming and I let it happen. That ledger doesn't close.

**What it means for Tess:** Tess also saw it. She saw the structural weakness before the trap sprung — the geometry was wrong, the position was exposed. She didn't say anything because Ruth was in command. Tess's silence cost Leah. That realization doesn't hit now. It hits at M10, when she finally opens her mouth.

**Connection back ← M3:** The hierarchy held. Ruth led, Tess followed. It failed.
**Connection forward → M10:** Tess will remember this moment as the reason she stops waiting for permission.

---

## M5 — WITNESSED: White Pawn Captured

**What happens:** Another loss. A support resource stripped from the board. Ruth logs it clinically. Tess watches Ruth log it clinically and wonders if that's strength or dissociation.

**What it means:** The field is degrading. Resources are being stripped around them while they hold position at c3. Ruth is still trying to manage the whole board from one square. She's field-leading a team that's getting smaller. The ER surgeon in a mass casualty event — too many patients, not enough hands, and the triage protocol says *focus on the ones you can save*.

Tess feels the losses differently. Each one is a person she trained with, ate breakfast across from, argued about TV shows with. She's on Sertraline because the world is too heavy without chemical intervention. The losses are weight. She carries weight.

**Connection back ← M4:** Second loss in two moves. Pattern establishing.
**Connection forward → M6:** The pattern continues but reverses direction.

---

## M6 — WITNESSED: Black Pawn Captured

**What happens:** An enemy resource is removed. Something on Bellatrix's side breaks. Ruth and Tess witness their first indication that the other side isn't invulnerable — the structure has gaps.

**What it means:** Ruth's triage brain notices: they bleed too. The losses aren't one-directional. She starts looking at the board differently — not just *who are we losing* but *where are they weak*. She scans the queenside. She sees something at c6 but doesn't trust it yet.

Tess sees it too. She trusts it immediately.

**Connection back ← M5:** Losses on both sides. The board is thinning.
**Connection forward → M10:** The c6 weakness Tess clocked here becomes the target she calls out four moves later.

---

## MOVES 7–9 — HOLDING POSITION AT c3
### THE WEIGHT OF WAITING

**What happens:** Ruth and Tess don't move for several turns. The board shifts around them. Threats develop and dissolve. They are threatened at least twice during this window — probing attacks, positional pressure. Ruth runs field triage remotely. Tess sits with her silence getting louder.

**What it means:** The knight is a piece that has to move to be useful. Holding position is death for a knight — it doesn't project power by sitting still. Ruth and Tess are built for movement, for striking and repositioning, and they're stuck. Ruth justifies it as strategic patience. Tess experiences it as cowardice.

The threats are real. Danger is becoming their ambient condition. The first time a threat vector crosses their position, Ruth's hands shake for exactly one second and then stop. The second time, they don't shake. Tess notices both.

**Connection back ← M6:** They saw the weakness. They're not acting on it.
**Connection forward → M10:** Tess breaks the stasis.

---

## M10 — Nxc6: CAPTURE BELLATRIX'S PAWN
### JUNCTURE 2 — "Tess Makes the Call"

**What happens:** Tess identifies the c6 weakness before Ruth does. Tess makes the call to strike. Not a suggestion — a call. Ruth follows. They remove Bellatrix's defensive pawn, breaking Black's queenside coordination. From their new position, they can see Prime Kain at d7. First visual confirmation of his location.

**What it means for Tess:** This is the moment Tess stops being the youngest. She saw the geometry at M4 and said nothing and Leah fell. She saw the weakness at M6 and held her tongue. She's been holding her tongue her entire life — daughter of the Police Chief, girl on Sertraline, the one people check in on. *Are you okay? Do you need anything? We're here for you.* She's been treated as someone to protect, not someone to follow.

She calls the strike because the alternative is watching another person fall while she stays quiet. The call comes out clean. No hesitation. No upward inflection. No "should we maybe—". She says it and moves.

**What it means for Ruth:** Ruth follows. *Ruth follows.* The field leader takes direction from Gloom Girl. And the disorienting thing is — it's right. Tess's call is better than what Ruth was planning. Ruth's judgment has been compromised since M4. She's been seeing Leah's incapacitation on the inside of her eyelids every time she blinks. Her triage has been reactive, not proactive. Tess is reading the board cleaner because Tess processes grief as weight, not interference. Ruth processes it as noise in the signal.

Ruth following Tess is the first honest thing their partnership has done.

**What it means for both:** This is the structural redefinition. Not leader and sidekick. Partners. The knight is one piece with two people inside it, and it works because both of them are driving.

**Connection back ← M4/M6:** The silence that cost Leah is broken. The weakness Tess spotted becomes the target.
**Connection forward → M12:** The partnership, now redefined, pushes deeper. Every move from here is *theirs*, not Ruth's-with-Tess-attached.

---

## M12 — Nd5: c6 → d5
### DEEP PENETRATION — "Further In"

**What happens:** Ruth and Tess advance to d5, deep into enemy territory. They're past the pawn line. They can see the back rank. The board from here looks completely different than from c3. They're behind the defense. Threats increase — they're exposed and surrounded by enemy structures.

**What it means:** Ruth's ER instinct says *this is too deep, we can't extract from here*. She says it out loud. Tess says, "Good." And Ruth hears it — Tess isn't reckless, she's correct. The only position worth holding is the one that threatens the king. Everything else is waiting to lose.

This is the first time Ruth thinks: *Tess might be the better field leader.* She doesn't say it. She files it where she filed the M4 failure — in the ledger that tracks how her control instinct serves the team versus serves her anxiety.

Ruth and Tess are developing a shorthand. Tess reads geometry, Ruth reads bodies. Tess sees where to go, Ruth sees what it'll cost. Between them they have full vision. Neither one alone has enough.

**Connection back ← M10:** The redefined partnership drives them forward.
**Connection forward → M16:** The advance continues, the threats compound, and the question becomes whether they can sustain this pace without breaking.

---

## M13–M15 — SUSTAINED THREAT WINDOW
### "COUNTING HEARTBEATS"

**What happens:** Three consecutive moves of threat exposure. Enemy pieces rotate through their zone. At least two or three of their eight total threats accumulate here. Ruth and Tess hold position at d5 while pressure mounts from multiple directions. They can't advance, can't retreat without giving up their penetration depth.

**What it means:** Sustained threat does something specific to each of them.

**Ruth** enters a state she recognizes from the ER — the focused calm that happens when there are too many emergencies to panic about any single one. Her field medicine comes alive. She's tracking vitals, calculating vectors, running triage on threats the way she runs triage on patients. Three threats? Prioritize, address, move on. She's not leading anymore — she's *functioning*. There's a difference. Leading is a choice. Functioning is a survival state.

**Tess** discovers something about her depression during sustained danger: it doesn't go away, but it *recalibrates*. The baseline heaviness that makes getting out of bed an achievement becomes a strange kind of armor. She's already carrying maximum weight. The threats don't add to it — they're just different weight. Her Sertraline isn't making her feel good, it's keeping the floor from dropping out, and right now the floor is all she needs. She tells Ruth: "I've been living in a burning building for years. This is just a different fire."

Ruth looks at her. Ruth has never heard Tess talk about her depression as a *resource* before. It recalibrates something in Ruth, too.

**Connection back ← M12:** Deep position means deep exposure. The cost of good positioning.
**Connection forward → M16:** They survive. They move. The partnership holds.

## M16 — Ndb4: d5 → b4
### QUEENSIDE STRIKE — "The Angle Changes"

**What happens:** Ruth and Tess reposition to b4, shifting from the center to the queenside. This is aggressive flanking — attacking the enemy's support structure from an unexpected angle. They are threatening new targets. The threats against them don't decrease; the threat *texture* changes. Fewer direct attacks, more structural pressure.

**What it means:** Tess chose this angle. Ruth executed the movement. The shorthand is working — Tess reads the board, Ruth moves them through it. But something in the movement feels different now. They're not responding to threats anymore. They're *hunting*.

Ruth recognizes the shift. In the ER, there's a moment when you stop treating symptoms and start treating the disease. You go from managing what's in front of you to finding the source. That's what this feels like. They've stopped triaging the board and started targeting the cause.

Tess feels it as momentum. For someone who fights gravity every morning just to sit up in bed, momentum is sacred. She protects it.

**Connection back ← M13–15:** Surviving the threat window gave them something. Not confidence — *information*. They know what sustained pressure feels like. It's survivable.
**Connection forward → M17:** They're close enough now to see what Kain Clone is doing. What they see changes the mission.

---

## M17 — Nd3: b4 → d3
### WITNESSED: Kain Clone's Pattern — "He Doesn't Fight. He Strips."

**What happens:** Ruth and Tess move to d3 and witness the aftermath of Kain Clone's systematic resource elimination. This follows the same pattern as his previous kill — not combat, not engagement. Infrastructure removal. He doesn't attack people. He removes what they stand on.

**What it means for Ruth:** She's seen this before. In hospitals. In systems. The administrator who doesn't fire the surgeon — he cuts the budget for the surgical ward. The official who doesn't shut down the clinic — he rezones the building. Kain Clone doesn't do violence. He does *erosion*. Ruth recognizes it because she's lived inside institutions her entire career. She knows what it looks like when someone kills you without touching you.

**What it means for Tess:** Tess recognizes it from her father's precinct. The way Chief Whitford doesn't order brutality — he creates conditions where brutality becomes the path of least resistance. He doesn't cover up Isaiah Bennett's killing — he structures the investigation so the cover-up covers itself. Kain Clone operates like her father. Systemically. Deniably. The realization makes her jaw lock.

**What it means for both:** They now have a target. Not a person to fight but a *pattern* to break. The Kain Clone kills by removing infrastructure. They're going to remove *him*. The pursuit begins here — three moves out from the capture at M21. They're committed. And they don't know yet that committing to this pursuit is exactly what will put them in the position where Bellatrix removes them at M24.

**Connection back ← M16:** The hunt finds its quarry. The angle shift gave them eyes on the pattern.
**Connection forward → M20:** Three moves to close the distance. The clock is running.

---

## M18–M19 — CLOSING THE GAP
### JUNCTURE 3 PROXIMITY — "The Second Kill"

**What happens:** At M19, Kain Clone strips Ben and Victor's defensive resource (Juncture 3). Ruth and Tess are close enough to witness the structural aftermath — not the act itself, but the damage. Ben and Victor's position erodes. The king line opens. Kain Clone is between them and the edge of the board, moving toward h1.

Ruth and Tess are taking fire. Two more threats accumulate in this window. The pressure is directional now — someone knows they're pursuing the clone. The threats are designed to slow them, not stop them. Delay tactics. Speed bumps.

**What it means:** Ruth is doing combat math. If they pursue the clone to h1, they'll be at the extreme edge of the board. Maximum distance from the center. Maximum distance from Ahdia. Maximum isolation. She runs the numbers and doesn't like them.

Tess is doing different math. She's counting the clone's kills. M17 — first kill. M19 — second kill. Pattern of two. If they don't stop him, there will be a third. And a fourth. The structure will keep eroding until there's nothing left to stand on. Tess's math is simpler than Ruth's and more urgent.

Ruth says: "If we go to h1, we can't get back."

Tess says: "If we don't, he keeps stripping."

Both statements are true. Both/and.

**Connection back ← M17:** The pattern they identified is continuing. The cost of stopping it is becoming clear.
**Connection forward → M20:** They choose to pursue. They know the cost.

## M20 — Nf2: d3 → f2
### COMMITTED — "No Extraction"

**What happens:** Ruth and Tess move to f2, one move from the Kain Clone's position at h1. This is the point of no return. They're deep on the kingside, far from the center, far from support. The board behind them is open — no friendly pieces covering their retreat. If they take the clone, they'll be at h1. Corner of the board. Nowhere to go.

**What it means:** Ruth feels the geometry of the trap before Tess does. This is ironic — Tess is the geometry reader, Ruth is the body reader. But this trap isn't positional. It's medical. Ruth recognizes terminal positioning the way she recognizes terminal patients. The vitals look fine right now. The trajectory is fatal.

She doesn't say "we should stop." She's past that. The ledger from M4 — *I saw it coming and let it happen* — won't allow another entry. If the Kain Clone keeps stripping infrastructure, more people fall. Ruth's triage brain does the calculation: stop one threat, sacrifice one unit. The unit is them.

Tess feels something she hasn't felt since before the Sertraline. Not happiness — *clarity*. The fog that lives behind her eyes, the one the medication manages but never clears, lifts for exactly the duration of this decision. She knows what to do. She knows what it costs. The knowing is clean.

"Together," Ruth says.

Tess nods.

**Connection back ← M18–19:** The math is done. Both answers were true. They chose.
**Connection forward → M21:** One move. Then the consequences.

## M21 — Nxh1: CAPTURE KAIN CLONE
### JUNCTURE 4 — "The Kill That Costs Everything"

**What happens:** Ruth and Tess remove the Kain Clone. The form of the removal: it's surgical. Ruth's medical precision and Tess's positional instinct combine into a single action. They don't overpower him — they *dismantle* the system he's operating within. They strip his infrastructure the way he stripped everyone else's. Poetic. Deliberate. Ruth's hands are steady. Tess's eyes are clear.

The clone's consciousness transfers. The body was always expendable. He isn't surprised. He isn't distressed. He looks at them and they see it — this was accounted for. His removal was part of the design. Three kills, then one final function: pulling Ruth and Tess to h1. Corner of the board. Maximum isolation. The clone was bait and weapon simultaneously. He accomplished his mission by dying.

Ruth and Tess stand at h1 and look back at the board. They are further from the center than any piece has been. There is no extraction route. There is no support within reach. The board between them and Ahdia is open, exposed, hostile territory.

**What it means for Ruth:** She feels the clone's smile like a diagnosis she missed. She's an ER surgeon. She knows what it means when a patient stops fighting — it means the disease already won and the body is catching up. The clone stopped fighting because his death *was* the weapon. Ruth looks at the board from h1 and sees what he saw: she's out of position. She did exactly what the trap at c4 did to Leah. She walked into the geometry and the geometry closed.

The difference — and this is the both/and that will define her — is that the clone is gone. His pattern is broken. No fourth kill. No fifth. The infrastructure he was stripping is damaged but standing. She traded her position for the team's structure. The math works. The math also means she might not survive it.

**What it means for Tess:** Tess feels the transfer happen. The clone's consciousness leaving the body like a breath. She's seen her father do this — shed accountability, leave the husk of a decision behind, appear clean somewhere else. Kain Clone is Chief Whitford is every system that kills you and walks away untouched.

But Tess didn't walk away. She stood here and broke the pattern with her hands. She initiated at M10. She chose the angle at M16. She drove the pursuit from M17. This kill — this dismantling — is hers as much as Ruth's. More. Tess was the geometry. Ruth was the hands. Both were necessary.

Tess looks at h1 and doesn't feel trapped. She feels *spent*. Like the Sertraline morning when you've done everything the day required and there's nothing left and that's not failure, that's *completion*. She did what she could from where she was with what she had.

**What it means for both:** They achieved their objective AND compromised their position. Both things are true. The capture worked and the capture doomed them. This is the both/and that runs through the entire series — you can win and lose in the same action, and both outcomes are real, and neither one cancels the other.

Three moves until Bellatrix.

**Connection back ← M20:** They committed. They followed through.
**Connection forward → M23:** Two moves of trying to get back. It won't be enough.

## M22 — HOLDING AT h1
### "THE CORNER"

**What happens:** One move of absolute stillness. Ruth and Tess at h1 with no viable immediate movement. The board continues around them. They can hear comms. They can see the field. They can't reach it.

**What it means:** Ruth does something she has never done in the field: she sits down. Not collapse — deliberate sitting. She puts her back against whatever surface h1 offers and she breathes. Not tactical breathing. Just breathing.

Tess sits beside her. Their shoulders touch. Tess says: "Do you think about Firas?"

Ruth doesn't answer for a long time. Then: "Every time I close my eyes."

Tess: "I think about Leta. Every time I open mine."

This is the moment. Not the capture, not the deployment, not the redefinition of their partnership. This. Two people who have been running since M3, who have been threatened eight times, who have broken one pattern and walked into another, sitting in a corner of the board with their shoulders touching, talking about the people they're carrying.

Ruth grieves Firas in motion — she keeps moving so the grief can't settle. Tess grieves Leta in stillness — she stops moving so the grief has somewhere to land. In this corner, on this square, with nowhere to go and nothing to do, they meet in the middle. Ruth stops. Tess holds still. The grief has one shape between them.

**Connection back ← M21:** The kill is done. The cost is arriving.
**Connection forward → M23:** They have to move. The board won't let them rest.

## M23 — Ng3: h1 → g3
### WITNESSED: White Pawn (h) Captured — "Almost Home"

**What happens:** Ruth and Tess move from h1 to g3. One square closer to the center. One square closer to the team. As they reposition, they witness another ally fall — White Pawn at h captured. Another loss. The board is thinning. They're moving through wreckage now.

**What it means:** The witness at M23 mirrors the witness at M4. They watched Leah fall from c3 at the beginning. They watch another piece fall from g3 near the end. The difference is who they are when they see it. At M4, Ruth's hands shook for one second and Tess stayed silent. At M23, Ruth's hands are steady and Tess says, out loud: "We need to keep moving."

Tess is leading. It's not a dramatic shift — it's the natural state of their partnership now. Ruth isn't diminished. Ruth is *freed*. The hierarchy that defined them at M3 is gone. What's left is two people who trust each other's vision, moving through a board that's trying to kill them, heading toward a center they may not reach.

They are one move from Bellatrix.

They don't know that. But they feel it — the way you feel pressure change before a storm. Something enormous is paying attention to their square. Something old.

**Connection back ← M22:** The corner gave them a moment. The moment is over.
**Connection forward → M24:** Bellatrix.

## M24 — REMOVED BY BELLATRIX
### JUNCTURE 5 — "Weight"

**What happens:** Bellatrix removes Ruth and Tess from the field. The removal is not violent. It is not brutal. It is not contemptuous. It is not casual. It has weight.

Bellatrix is 3.7 billion years old. She has seen species rise and fall. She has watched civilizations build cathedrals and burn them. She has existed longer than multicellular life on Earth. When she removes Ruth and Tess, she does it the way a glacier removes a mountain — not with malice, not with effort, but with the kind of inevitability that makes resistance a category error.

Ruth and Tess are together when it happens. They entered the board as a pair at M3. They leave as a pair at M24. Twenty-one moves of engagement. One capture. Eight threats survived. Two partnerships forged — with each other, and with the version of themselves that emerged from the pursuit.

**What it means for Ruth:** Ruth's last field-leader thought is about Ahdia. She calculates: with us gone, she's alone for the checkmate. She runs the triage — can Ahdia do it solo? The answer is yes. The answer is also: it will cost her everything. Ruth's final act of field leadership is trusting someone she can't protect to do something she can't help with.

The ER surgeon releases the patient. The triage is complete. There is nothing more she can do from where she is. This is the hardest thing Ruth Carter has ever done — not the fighting, not the pursuit, not the kill. Letting go.

**What it means for Tess:** Tess feels Bellatrix's removal the way she feels her depression — as gravity. An increase in the weight of existing. The floor doesn't drop out. The floor gets heavier. She's been carrying maximum weight since before the Sertraline. This is just more.

But she carried it. She carried it from M3 to M24. She initiated at M10. She drove the pursuit. She sat in the corner with Ruth and said Leta's name out loud. She led when leading was needed. She held still when stillness was needed. The depression didn't disqualify her from any of it. The depression was *in* her while she did all of it.

She doesn't fight the removal. Not because she's surrendering — because she's finished. She did what she could. The board has her move record. Twenty-one moves. One capture. One partnership redefined.

**What it means for both:** They entered as leader and sidekick. They leave as equals. They entered in hierarchy. They leave in partnership. They entered with Ruth's hands shaking and Tess's mouth closed. They leave with Ruth's hands steady and Tess's voice clear.

Their absence is what makes Ahdia's solo checkmate both necessary and devastating. Ahdia delivers the final blow alone because Ruth and Tess pursued the clone to h1 because Tess called the strike at M10 because she stayed silent at M4 because Leah fell. The chain is unbroken. Every choice connects. The both/and runs all the way through: their pursuit of the clone saved the team's infrastructure AND removed them from the endgame. Both true. Both permanent.

Bellatrix removes them with weight. Not the weight of violence. The weight of 3.7 billion years looking at two humans who did everything they could and finding that — in the geometry of this board, in the structure of this game — everything they could was not enough to be here at the end.

They survive. They are off the board, not off the earth. They'll carry this — Ruth in her ledger of responsibilities, Tess in the weight she adds to her morning inventory. But they'll carry it *together*, which is something neither of them had at M3.

**Connection back ← Everything:** Twenty-one moves. One arc. Two people. Complete.
**Connection forward → M26:** Ahdia moves to d7 alone. She captures Prime Kain. Checkmate. Ruth and Tess aren't there to see it. That's the cost. That's the both/and. They made it possible by being removed. Their absence is their final contribution to the win.

## ARC SUMMARY

**Ruth Carter** enters the board as a field leader who controls through triage and exits as a partner who trusts through release. Her grief for Firas drives her forward. Her failure at M4 haunts her ledger. Her recognition of Tess as an equal at M10 is the most important tactical decision she makes — more important than the clone kill, more important than the pursuit. She learns that leadership is not hierarchy. Leadership is knowing when someone else's vision is clearer than yours and following it.

**Tess Whitford** enters the board as the youngest, the quietest, the one on medication, the one people protect. She exits as the geometric mind that drove the knight's most critical movements. Her silence at M4 costs Leah. Her voice at M10 redefines the partnership. Her depression is not overcome — it is *present* for every single move, and she performs at the highest level while carrying it. The Sertraline keeps the floor from dropping out. The floor is all she needs.

**Together** they are the knight — the piece that moves in angles no other piece can reach, that jumps over obstacles, that threatens from directions the enemy doesn't expect. They are removed two moves before checkmate. They never see the ending they made possible. That's the cost. They paid it together.

**Theme:** You don't have to be fixed to be worthy. Tess is on Sertraline for the entire game. She never stops being depressed. She is worthy of every move she makes.
