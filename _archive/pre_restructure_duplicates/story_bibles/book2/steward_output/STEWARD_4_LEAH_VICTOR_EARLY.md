# STEWARD 4 — NARRATIVE OUTLINE
## Leah Turner/Battlea + Victor Hernandez (Early Pairing)
### Bishop (f1) | Moves 1–4 | Exposition Phase

## PRE-GAME: THE VERSION OF NORMAL THAT'S ABOUT TO END

### Leah

Leah is working a closing shift at the coffee shop. Her hands know the motions — tamp, pull, pour — but her mind is somewhere else. It's been somewhere else since the red dress. Since Kain's fingers lingered on her hand at the gala and she smiled through it because the mission required smiling through it. She hasn't told anyone that she still feels the pressure of his grip when she's falling asleep. She told herself it was nothing. She told herself she handled it. She has the recording on her phone. She hasn't listened to it since the night they made it.

What she *has* done: started pulling threads. Quietly. A name here, a pattern there. Other women. NDAs. Settlements buried under shell companies. She doesn't have a plan yet. She has a folder on her laptop labeled "K-research" and a growing conviction that what happened to her wasn't isolated. This is not activism. This is not solidarity. This is Leah trying to prove to herself that it mattered — that the dress, the smile, the held hand meant something beyond a mission objective that got filed and forgotten.

She's training harder than ever. Roller derby three nights a week. MMA four mornings. Her body is the thing she trusts. Her body did what was asked at the gala, and her body will do what's asked next. The anger has always been there — anger management books on her shelf, a punching bag in her apartment — but it's finding new shapes now. Less explosive, more directional. She doesn't know what she's pointing it at yet.

She has not thought about Leta Bennett in any meaningful way. Leta is Tess's girlfriend. Leta is a trans activist with a loud Twitter presence. Leta is adjacent. Leah has no reason to think about her, and so she doesn't. This will matter later. This will matter enormously.

### Victor

Victor is at the community center, same as every night. The after-school program ended at six. He stayed to fix the water heater, then to reorganize the supply closet, then to sit in his office with the lights off because the center is quieter than his apartment and his apartment has the wedding photo he keeps meaning to take down but hasn't because taking it down means deciding what goes in its place.

His wife's face is scratched out in the frame. He did that. He doesn't remember when. He remembers the marker in his hand and the sound it made on the glass and then it was done and he put the photo back and went to bed. He has not scratched it further. He has not repaired it. The photo sits in a state of arrested damage, and this is a metaphor he would recognize if he allowed himself to look at it directly, which he does not.

Victor thinks in systems. Always has. Community organizing is systems work — who needs what, where resources flow, which nodes connect to which networks. Since the warehouse incident, since learning about TRIOMF, his systems thinking has taken on a sharper edge. He's mapping. Not formally — he doesn't have Ben's investigator training or Leta's digital forensics. But he sees how things connect. The riots. The mining operations. The bot networks. The fusion reactor announcement. He sees the shape of a network even when he can't name all its nodes.

He's been teaching Leah. Not formally — Victor doesn't lecture. But in conversations after missions, in the van, waiting for Ruth's signal. He's been introducing the "But vs And" framework. Leah says things like "I know it was just a mission, *but* it felt wrong." Victor says: "What if it was a mission *and* it felt wrong? What if both are true?" Leah stares at him. Leah changes the subject. But she's listening. He can tell because the next time she talks about the gala, she says "and" instead of "but" — once, quickly, then corrects herself back. Progress is not always linear.

### Leah + Victor Together

They are not natural partners. Leah is kinetic, instinctive, loud in her body if not her convictions. Victor is still, analytical, quiet in a way that people mistake for passivity. But they work. Leah pushes forward; Victor reads the terrain she's pushing into. Leah fights; Victor understands what the fight is actually about. In roller derby terms: Leah is the jammer, Victor is the pivot who reads the pack.

They've developed a shorthand. Victor touches his left ear: *slow down, something's wrong.* Leah cracks her knuckles: *I hear you, but I'm going anyway.* Victor sighs. Leah goes. It works more often than it should.

Neither of them has said out loud that they're scared. The investigations are converging. The Go Squad is running hotter than ever. Ahdia is disappearing for hours, days, coming back with circles under her eyes and deflecting every question. Something is building. Leah channels the dread into training. Victor channels it into mapping. They are both doing the same thing — converting fear into motion — and neither recognizes it in the other.

## MOVE 1 (e4): THE OPENING SALVO

*White's e-pawn advances to e4. Leah and Victor's pawn companion — the central pawn — pushes forward aggressively, claiming the center.*

### What Happens (External)

The Go Squad commits to engagement. This is the decision to act — to stop reacting to Kain's escalations and push into contested territory. The e-pawn is the first piece to move in the entire game. It claims the center of the board. It says: *we are here, and we are not retreating.*

For Leah and Victor, this is their companion's move. Their resource, their opening. The e-pawn is the aggressive center-seeking energy that creates space for everything that follows. Leah recognizes it as the kind of move she'd make — bold, direct, committed. Victor recognizes it as necessary but exposed. A pawn in the center is a target. It's also the only way to start.

### What This Means (Internal)

**Leah:** The moment the squad decides to engage feels like relief. She's been holding the anger like a breath for months — the gala, the recordings, the NDA patterns she's been tracking. Action is her language. Waiting corrodes her. When the decision comes, her body unlocks. Finally. *Finally.* She doesn't interrogate the plan. She doesn't ask whether the center is safe. The center is where the fight is, so the center is where she wants to be.

**Victor:** Victor watches the commitment happen and feels the familiar tightness in his chest — the feeling he gets when a system is about to be tested and he can see the stress points before anyone else. The center is powerful. The center is also where you get surrounded. He doesn't voice the objection. The team needs momentum, and momentum requires someone willing to go first. The e-pawn goes first. That's its job. Victor just wishes it weren't so far ahead of everything else.

### Connection

This move establishes the posture that leads directly to M2. The center is claimed. Now the question becomes: who follows the pawn forward? The answer — Leah and Victor, deployed aggressively to c4 — is already taking shape. The e-pawn's advance creates the diagonal that the bishop will travel. Victor can see the line. He isn't sure yet whether it's an opportunity or a funnel.

## MOVE 2 (Bc4): AGGRESSIVE EARLY DEPLOYMENT — f1 → c4

*The bishop deploys from f1 to c4 — an aggressive diagonal push, reinforcing the center, eyeing Black's vulnerable squares. Leah and Victor enter the field early and forward.*

### What Happens (External)

Leah and Victor deploy to c4. This is Move 2 of the entire game — they are in the field before most of the squad. The position is strong on paper: they support the center pawn, they control a long diagonal, they threaten into Black's territory. But they are ahead of their own support structure. The knights haven't developed yet. The rooks are still on their starting squares. Leah and Victor are a bishop on c4 — powerful range, no protection.

This is an opening book move. Standard theory. Aggressive but sound. What makes it dangerous isn't the position itself — it's what happens when Black responds to it.

### What This Means (Internal)

**Leah:** This is where Leah is her best and most dangerous self. Forward deployment. First in. She transforms into Battlea with purpose — not the frantic rage of the police ambush in Book 1, but something more controlled. More directed. The MMA training has given her new architecture for her anger. She's not exploding. She's *striking.* The c4 position feels right. She can see the angles, feel the diagonal stretching into enemy territory. Victor is beside her, reading the field, and she trusts his reading even when she doesn't slow down enough to hear it.

What she doesn't feel — and this is the thing that will haunt the people who survive her — is doubt. Leah on c4 is Leah without hesitation. She earned this. The training, the pain, the nights at the heavy bag, the mornings at the gym when her body screamed and she went anyway. She *earned* the right to be here. This is the distinction that matters to her: earned strength vs. given strength. She doesn't have temporal powers. She doesn't teleport. She has bruised knuckles and a purple belt and the willingness to stand in the space others won't. That's enough. She believes that's enough.

**Victor:** Victor takes the c4 position and immediately starts cataloging. Lines of sight. Retreat paths. Adjacent squares. He's not being paranoid — this is what he does. He sees the board the way he sees a neighborhood: nodes, connections, resources, vulnerabilities. The c4 square is strong *if* the center holds. The c4 square is a coffin if the b-file opens.

He hasn't said this to Leah. He hasn't said this because Leah on the attack is a force that accomplishes things caution wouldn't, and because Victor has learned — from organizing, from marriage, from grief — that sometimes the right call is to support the charge rather than question it. Both/and. The position is strong *and* it's exposed. Leah is magnificent *and* she's ahead of her protection. Victor can hold both truths. What he can't do is hold both truths and also stop what's coming.

He touches his left ear. *Slow down, something's wrong.*

Leah cracks her knuckles. *I hear you, but I'm going anyway.*

Victor doesn't sigh this time. The absence of the sigh is the first thing that's different. The absence of the sigh means he's not resigned — he's *alert.*

### Connection

M2 places them on the square where they'll be captured. Everything between this moment and M4 is the space between arrival and ambush. Two moves. That's all they get. Leah will never advance beyond c4. She'll never see the middle game, the endgame, the checkmate. The board she knows is the board as it exists at Move 2: open, promising, aggressive, full of potential. The board she leaves behind will be unrecognizable.

## MOVE 3: THE SPACE BETWEEN

*M3 belongs to other pieces. Ruth and Tess develop their knight. The board is still taking shape. Leah and Victor hold c4.*

### What Happens (External)

Leah and Victor aren't moving this turn. They hold position at c4 while the game develops around them. Black responds to the opening. Other pieces come into play. The board is filling up.

From c4, Leah and Victor can see the knight deployment — Ruth and Tess entering the field. They can see the center being contested. They can see Black's pieces arranging. What they cannot see — what no one on the board can see at Move 3 — is that the b-file is being prepared. That c4 is not just a strong square. That c4 is also a *target.*

### What This Means (Internal)

**Leah:** Holding position is harder than advancing. Leah's body wants to move. The diagonal is right there — she can feel it stretching toward Black's king side, full of possibility. But the position requires patience, and patience is the discipline she's worst at and has worked hardest to develop. She holds. She watches Ruth and Tess take the field. She thinks about how different their partnership looks from hers and Victor's — Ruth leading, Tess following, the hierarchy clean and legible. Leah and Victor's dynamic isn't like that. It's messier. She pushes; he reads; sometimes she overrides his reading; sometimes his reading saves her. There's no clean hierarchy because Leah won't accept one and Victor won't impose one.

She thinks, briefly and without connecting it to anything larger, about Leta. About how Tess talks about Leta — the quick smile, the change in posture, the way Tess's voice drops half a register when she says Leta's name. Leah notices this the way she notices a lot of things about other people's relationships — from the outside, with a mix of curiosity and distance. She has not considered what Leta's world looks like from the inside. She has not wondered what it means to be a trans woman on Twitter with a loud voice and a growing list of people who want her silent. Leah is not cruel. She's just not paying attention. Not yet.

**Victor:** Victor uses the hold to map. He's watching Black's response to the opening. He's watching how the opponent's pieces arrange around the contested center. He's running the kind of quiet probability math that community organizers do when they're planning a campaign — *if this, then this, then this, and if that goes wrong, then what?*

Something is nagging him. It's not a thought yet — it's a sensation. The way the b-file is clearing. The way the opponent's pieces are orienting. He's seen this pattern before, not on a chessboard but in neighborhoods — the way a system targets a specific block for demolition. You don't see the demolition order first. You see the rezoning. The permit changes. The quiet, structural preparations that make the demolition look inevitable after the fact.

He can't name what he's seeing. He can only feel it — a wrongness in the geometry. The b-file clearing is the rezoning permit. Something is being prepared.

He touches his left ear. Leah doesn't crack her knuckles. She's looking at Ruth and Tess. She doesn't see his signal. He lowers his hand.

### Connection

M3 is the last moment of potential. After this, the trap springs. Everything Victor is sensing — the wrongness, the geometry, the quiet preparation — will crystallize into a half-second of recognition at M4. One beat too late to stop it. This is the space where the outline pauses to note what's about to be lost: two people holding a position together, one of them straining forward, one of them trying to read the danger, both of them doing exactly what they're built to do, and it not being enough.

## MOVE 4 (bxc4): REMOVAL — THE PREPARED TRAP

*Black's b-pawn captures on c4. Leah is incapacitated. Victor survives. The position at c4 was a prepared trap — systemic, not personal.*

### What Happens (External)

**Per Juncture Canon:**
- The position at c4 was a prepared trap. Kain's forces anticipated an aggressive push into that square. Leah and Victor walked into it.
- Leah is incapacitated — not killed.
- Victor is not incapacitated — he survives and migrates to Ben's unit.
- This was systemic, not personal. No individual villain targeted Leah specifically. The trap would have caught whoever pushed forward first.
- Victor saw it coming a half-second before it sprung. He could not stop it.
- Leah had some awareness of what was happening as it happened.

The b-pawn captures on c4. The trap was structural — built into the position, waiting for whoever was bold enough or unlucky enough to occupy that square. The squad pushed forward aggressively, and the response was already chambered. Leah takes the hit. Victor does not. The bishop is removed from the board at Move 4. The game is twenty-two moves from checkmate.

### What This Means (Internal)

**Victor — The Half-Second:**

Victor sees it. He sees it the way he saw the rezoning — the structural preparation, the b-file clearing, the geometry resolving into a shape he finally recognizes. *Oh.* The word doesn't make it out of his throat. His hand is moving toward Leah. His left hand, the one that touches his ear for the signal. But this isn't a signal. This is reaching.

A half-second. That's the gap between seeing and stopping. Half a second is enough time to understand completely and do nothing. Victor has lived inside gaps like this before. The gap between the phone call about his wife and arriving at the hospital. The gap between knowing the community center's funding was cut and watching the after-school kids find out. The gap between *I see what's happening* and *I cannot change what's happening.* He has built his entire life in these gaps — the space where understanding arrives too late to be useful and too early to be grief.

He reaches for Leah. His fingers touch nothing.

**Leah — The Awareness:**

Leah feels it as it happens. Not before — that's Victor's curse, not hers. She feels it *during.* The trap engages and her body knows before her mind does, because her body has always been the faster thinker. MMA trained her to read an incoming strike in the first three inches of motion. Roller derby trained her to feel the hit before it lands. Her body says: *you are being taken off the field.*

What she thinks, in the awareness: *this was the square I was supposed to be on.* Not regret. Not exactly. Something closer to bewilderment. She did the thing she was built to do — pushed forward, occupied the contested space, stood where others wouldn't. She *earned* this position. And the position was the trap. The thing she's proudest of — her willingness to be first — is the thing that caught her.

She had been building something. The folder on her laptop. The NDA patterns. The other women's names. The recording she hadn't listened to but hadn't deleted. She was going to do something with it. She didn't know what yet, but she was going to find out. She was going to learn what solidarity looked like from the inside instead of observing it from the outside. She was going to think about Leta. She was going to think about what it means to be loud on the internet when people want you quiet. She was going to connect the dots between her experience at the gala and the systems that made that experience routine and protected and repeatable.

She doesn't get to do any of that. She is incapacitated at Move 4. The recording stays on her phone. The folder stays on her laptop. The names stay half-researched. The thought she was about to have about Leta stays unthought.

Leah Turner is removed from the field before her arc completes. She never sees the middle game. She never sees the evidence fail. She never sees Leta die. She never releases the recording — someone else will have to decide what to do with what she gathered. She pushed forward first, and the system was ready for anyone who pushed forward first, and now she is gone and the game is twenty-two moves from something she'll never witness.

**The Incapacitation:**

Not death. The canon is clear. But removal from the field is its own kind of ending. Leah is *out.* Whatever happens from here — the investigations, the convergence, the manhunt, the election, the checkmate — happens without her. She earned the right to be on that square. The square was the trap. Both are true. Both/and.

### Connection

Leah's removal at M4 is the earliest capture in the game. It establishes the cost of aggression and the nature of the enemy — systemic, not personal, structural, not combative. The trap wasn't designed for Leah Turner. It was designed for the square. She happened to be standing on it.

What she leaves behind:

1. **The recording.** The red dress audio. Months of NDA research. A half-built case that someone else will need to complete. In Month 11, someone releases this recording publicly. Leah never sees the release, the brief polling dip, or the way the evidence is ultimately reframed and absorbed. She did the work. She doesn't get to see it matter.

2. **The unfinished thought about Leta.** Leah was beginning to see the connection between her own experience and a larger system. She was starting to turn from observer to participant. That turn doesn't complete. When Leta is killed in Month 11, Leah isn't there to process it, and the people who are there will carry the specific weight of knowing that Leah was building toward something she never reached. The style guide's description of Leah's Book 2 arc — "Learning solidarity (too late for Leta)" — takes on a crueler dimension: Leah didn't just learn too late. She was *removed* before she could learn at all.

3. **Victor.** She leaves behind Victor, who saw it coming and couldn't stop it. Victor, who now carries the half-second in his body like a scar. Victor, who migrates to Ben's unit not because he chooses to but because the piece he was partnered with has been taken off the board and the game continues and he is still alive and the game continues and he needs to be somewhere and the game continues.

## POST-M4: VICTOR'S MIGRATION

*Victor leaves c4 and joins Ben's unit. This beat is the hinge — the last moment that belongs to this steward before Victor's arc transfers to Steward 5.*

### What Happens (External)

Victor is alive. Leah is not on the field. The game is still in its opening phase. Victor needs to attach to another unit — he can't operate as a bishop alone, not without the piece that gave the bishop its forward energy. He migrates to Ben's unit (the other bishop). The migration is not instantaneous. There is a transit — a crossing of the board from c4 to wherever Ben is setting up.

### What This Means (Internal)

Victor walks away from c4. He walks away from the square where Leah stood. He walks away from the half-second.

He is thinking in systems because systems are what he has. The grief is there — it's enormous, it's the wedding photo all over again, it's the scratched-out face, it's another person he was standing next to who is now not standing next to him — but the grief flows into the systems the way water flows into infrastructure. Victor doesn't collapse. Victor *routes.* The community center taught him this: when a node goes down, you don't mourn the node during the crisis. You reroute. You find the next connection. You keep the network alive. You mourn later, in your office, with the lights off, when the building is quiet.

He thinks about what Leah would say if she could see him walking away from her position. She'd crack her knuckles. She'd say: *Go.* She'd say it with her jaw set and her shoulders squared and she wouldn't say anything else because Leah has never been good at soft words and she wouldn't start now. She'd say *Go* and she'd mean *I'm not done but you need to not be done too so go.*

He goes.

He carries three things with him to Ben's unit:

1. **The half-second.** The knowing-without-stopping. This will change how he operates — he will be earlier with his warnings, louder with his signals, more insistent when the geometry looks wrong. He will be a better analyst for Ben because Leah's fall taught him that seeing the danger means nothing if you don't force someone to hear you. He touched his ear. She didn't see. He will not rely on shorthand again.

2. **The both/and.** Leah was learning it. She'd started using "and" instead of "but" — once, quickly, self-corrected. She didn't finish learning. Victor will continue teaching, now to Ben and whoever is left. The framework survives the person he was teaching it to. This is how systems outlast nodes.

3. **The information.** Leah's research. The NDA patterns. The recording. The folder on her laptop. Victor knows it exists. He knows Leah was building something. He doesn't have the folder — it's on her machine, and she's not here to hand it over — but he knows the shape of what she was finding. He will tell someone. The information will find its way to the person who needs it, the way information always does in Victor's networks — not through clean handoffs but through the messy, human, word-of-mouth infrastructure of people who give a damn.

### What Victor Would Think of the Endgame (If He Could See It From Here)

He can't. That's the point. But:

The checkmate happens at M26. Ahdia alone on d7. One capture. The one that ends it. Victor — if he could see the full board from this moment at M4 — would see the pattern immediately. He'd see that Ahdia spent the entire game repositioning (Qa4, Qb5, Qc4, Qc3, Qc6) — constant movement, never settled, until one decisive strike. He'd recognize it as a system. He'd recognize that the aggressive early pushes — Leah's push, the e-pawn's push — created the space that Ahdia eventually moved through. The sacrifices weren't wasted. They were *structural.* They were the cleared center, the opened diagonals, the space that exists because someone was willing to occupy a dangerous square first.

He'd see that Leah was part of the architecture of the checkmate even though she was removed twenty-two moves before it happened. He'd see that both things are true: she was caught by a trap *and* her willingness to be caught created space that led to victory. Both/and.

He'd tell her. If he could. He'd say: *You were the first move that mattered.*

She'd crack her knuckles. She'd say: *Obviously.*

## ARC SUMMARY

**Leah Turner/Battlea:**
- Pre-game: A woman converting private trauma (the gala) into private investigation, training her body because her body is the thing she trusts, not yet connecting her own experience to the larger system of harm
- M1: Her companion pawn charges the center. She recognizes herself in the aggression.
- M2: She deploys to c4. Forward, first, earned. The best version of what she does.
- M3: She holds position. Watches others deploy. Almost thinks about Leta. Doesn't.
- M4: The square was the trap. She is incapacitated. Her arc ends before it completes. The recording, the research, the solidarity she was building toward — all left unfinished for others to carry.

**Victor Hernandez (Early):**
- Pre-game: A man who thinks in systems and grieves in empty rooms, teaching "both/and" to someone who was starting to hear it
- M1: He reads the center push as necessary and exposed. Holds both truths.
- M2: He deploys beside Leah. Sees the diagonal, sees the danger, supports the charge.
- M3: He senses the geometry shifting. The b-file clearing. He touches his ear. She doesn't see.
- M4: He sees it a half-second before. His hand reaches. His fingers touch nothing. He survives. She doesn't (on the field). He carries the half-second, the both/and, and the knowledge of her unfinished work to Ben's unit.

**Thematic Core:** Earned vs. given strength. The square was earned. The trap was structural. The removal was systemic. Leah did everything right and it wasn't enough because the system wasn't testing her skill — it was testing her position. Victor saw it and couldn't stop it because seeing is not the same as preventing. Both truths. Both/and. The anger and the love and the grief all flowing into the same infrastructure, keeping the network alive after the node goes dark.
