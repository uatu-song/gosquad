# STEWARD OUTPUT — KOREDE & LETA OWOLOWO
## Book 2B Outline | Run 1

**Triplet lens:** White g-pawn (Korede), White f-pawn (Leta), White Rg1 (the documentation)

**Reading:** Three pieces. One family. The f-pawn is Leta — f3 weakens the king's position in Move 2, the very first thing White does wrong. Not because f3 is aggressive. Because f3 is *honest*. Leta's activism is the f-pawn: she says what no one else will say, and it opens a diagonal straight to the king. When the f-pawn pushes to f4 in Move 9, it's overreach — not because the cause is wrong but because the position can't support it. exf4. Captured immediately. The pawn that weakens what it meant to protect disappears the moment it commits fully. Leta's resistance practice works. Her organizing works. Her willingness to stand in the open and say *this is happening to me* works — for everyone except her.

The g-pawn is Korede. g3 in Move 3 — quiet, positional, the kind of move that doesn't draw attention. He's fianchettoing. He's building a system. Color-coded spreadsheets, timestamps, documentation. He believes in the architecture of evidence. Then gxf4 in Move 10: he recaptures what his sister lost. He picks up her work after she can no longer do it safely. But the recapture opens the king — the g-file is a highway now, and Korede is standing on it. Move 26: Kxf4. The enemy king takes the g-pawn personally. Not a minor piece. Not a tactical exchange. The king walks over and removes him from the board. Webb doesn't send someone. Webb is there. And Korede doesn't die — he's taken off the board in a different way. He becomes something the game no longer recognizes.

The Rg1 rook is the documentation. The system. The spreadsheets and timestamps and evidence file that Korede has been building since before Book 2B. Rg1 in Move 12 — one move, one commitment, one blunder. The rook goes to g1 thinking the g-file will open favorably. It never moves again. It's forked at M25. It's still on g1 at checkmate. The documentation doesn't save anyone. It doesn't prevent anything. It sits in a folder on Korede's laptop at the moment his sister dies, and it is the most complete record of a murder that was coming and that no one with power chose to stop.

The relationship between the three: Leta opens the position by being visible. Korede tries to recapture what her visibility costs. The system they built together never moves from its square. The enemy king walks through all of it.

## M13 — a4?? Bxh3?!
*Missed opportunity; first piece taken*
**CONVERGENCE POINT — shared with Ahdia and Ryu**

**Chess:** White pushes a4, a queenside pawn advance that ignores the center. Black responds Bxh3 — the bishop takes the knight on h3, a piece White spent two moves developing. First capture of the game. The knight is gone. White got nothing for it.

**Convergence point.** Ahdia is fighting the narrative war. Ryu is hiding the math. Korede and Leta are in the room where neither of those things matter.

### The Reading

a4 is the wrong protection. Someone pushes a resource toward the queenside — the political side, the public side, the side with cameras — and leaves the kingside unguarded. Bxh3 is what happens when the guard is pulled: the thing that was holding the position together gets taken cleanly, without compensation, without recourse. First piece captured. Not the biggest piece. The piece that was closest to the king.

For Korede and Leta, M13 is the night the harassment goes physical.

### Outline Beat: THE FILE THAT ISN'T ENOUGH

**Setting:** Leta and Korede's apartment. Late evening. The windows are covered — not curtains, *covered*. Cardboard and tape. Korede did it three days ago after the laser pointer incident.

**What happens:**

Leta comes home late from a community organizing session at Victor's center. She's teaching the fear resistance practice to a group of eight — five trans women, two nonbinary teens, one mother whose son was killed by police. The practice is spreading. Victor says the demand is outpacing what they can offer. Leta says that's a good problem.

Korede is at the kitchen table. He has the laptop open. He's been updating the documentation file — today's entry is a new account that posted Leta's daily route with photographs. Time-stamped. The photos include the community center entrance and the bus stop she uses. He has flagged it, reported it to the platform, screenshotted everything before the account gets suspended and recreated under a new name tomorrow.

They have a ritual. Leta checks the file when she gets home. Not because she wants to — because Korede needs her to see it so she understands why he covered the windows. She reads tonight's entry. She doesn't react to the route photos. She reacts to one detail Korede buried in the notes: the account used a photo of her at the community center taken from *inside the building*.

Beat: the shift. Someone was in the room with her students. Someone was in Victor's center during a session where five trans women were practicing breathing exercises with their eyes closed. The harassment has been online, then adjacent, and now it's *inside*. Leta calls Victor. Victor confirms: the center's door was open during session, no sign-in sheet, anyone could walk in. He's shaken. He offers to cancel sessions. Leta says no. "If we close the door, they've already won. If we close the door, Mara and Destiny and Jo don't have anywhere to go Tuesday night."

Korede argues. Not loudly — Korede doesn't raise his voice yet, not in Book 2B, not until after — but with the spreadsheet. He turns the laptop toward her and sorts by escalation category. Green: online only. Yellow: adjacent/physical proximity. Red: direct contact. Three weeks ago the file was all green. Two weeks ago the first yellow appeared. Tonight is the first red. He has a trend line. He built a trend line for his sister's harassment. He is fifteen years old and he has built a projection model that says the red entries will outnumber the yellow ones within two weeks.

Leta looks at the trend line. She looks at her brother. She says: "Baby, I know what the numbers say." She closes the laptop. Not hard — gently, the way you close something you love and cannot use. "The numbers said the same thing about Dad's store. The numbers said the same thing about every Black business on Avalon Boulevard. The numbers are never wrong and they never help."

This is where Ahdia's M13 and Ryu's M13 intersect: somewhere across the city, Ahdia is fighting the terrorist label narrative, pushing a4, burning baseline on the wrong front. Ryu has the decay model and isn't sharing it. Neither of them is looking at this apartment. Neither of them knows that someone was inside the community center today. The Go Squad's attention is on the queenside. The knight just fell on the kingside.

Closing beat: Korede reopens the laptop after Leta goes to bed. He adds one line to the documentation file, a new column he hasn't shown her. Header: **"RESPONSE TIME."** He has been logging how long it takes the police to respond to Leta's reports. He has four data points. The average is 11 days. The most recent response was an automated email. He starts a new spreadsheet tab. Header: **"IF POLICE WON'T."** He doesn't fill it in yet. He just names it.

### What This Beat Leaves Room For

- **For Ahdia's steward:** Ahdia's a4 push (narrative war, terrorist label) is happening simultaneous to this. The missed protection is structural — she's not neglecting Leta out of callousness but out of triage. The guilt will come from the math: she chose the bigger fire and the smaller one killed someone.
- **For Ryu's steward:** Ryu's concealment of the decay model means the team doesn't know Ahdia can't sustain protective operations. If they knew, someone might reallocate. No one knows.
- **For Tess's steward:** Tess is Leta's girlfriend. Where is Tess tonight? If Tess is on a Gloom Girl operation — if Tess is doing vigilante work while someone was *inside the community center* — that's a parallel to Ahdia's misallocated attention. The people who love Leta are all fighting the wrong fires.
- **For Victor's steward:** Victor's center is the site of infiltration. His response (offer to cancel, defer to Leta) shows his pattern: he supports the person in front of him rather than overriding their autonomy. The Both/And frame may fail here. You can honor someone's choice AND that choice can get them killed.
- **For the Bellatrix/Geneva steward:** Is the harassment campaign organic or orchestrated? The infiltration of Victor's center suggests resources and intent beyond random trolls. If TRIOMF is amplifying or directing the targeting of Leta — not creating the hate, but *aiming* it — that's Eidolon's function applied to institutional violence.
- **For the Reader Proxy:** The reader now has a trend line. Korede gave it to them. The reader is doing the same math Korede is doing. The reader knows what red means.

## M26 — Rb5+ Kxf4
*Deflection; king takes bait*

**Chess:** White plays Rb5+, a rook check that accomplishes nothing — the king isn't trapped, the check doesn't lead to mate, it's a move made because the position is already lost and checks feel like doing something. Black responds Kxf4: the king simply takes the f4 pawn. The king itself. Not a knight, not a bishop — the king walks to f4 and removes the pawn personally. After this, Black's king is on f4, centralized, untouchable, and two moves from delivering checkmate.

The g-pawn was on f4. It started on g2, moved to g3, captured to f4 trying to recapture what the f-pawn lost. The enemy king took it. Personally.

### The Reading

The rook check is someone with power making a desperate gesture that changes nothing. Rb5+ is the system responding to a crisis with a procedural action — a check, a report filed, a statement released, an officer dispatched — that the recipient simply walks through. Kxf4 is the enemy king saying: *I will take this pawn myself, and the check you threw means nothing.*

For Korede and Leta, M26 is the murder and the moment after.

This beat contains two events that are one event: Leta dies, and Korede becomes someone else. The g-pawn is taken. The rook on g1 — the documentation, the system, the belief that records constitute protection — is still sitting on its square, untouched, useless, and will remain there through checkmate.

### Outline Beat: "Kxf4"

**Setting:** A street in the Ninth Ward during the citywide manhunt. The manhunt is for Go Squad — the team has been labeled terrorists, and the city is locked down. Ordinary people are being stopped, searched, brutalized under cover of the operation. Webb's unit is not searching for Go Squad. Webb's unit is using the manhunt as license.

**What happens:**

Leta and Korede are not at home. They are moving through the neighborhood on foot because Leta got a call: one of her students from the resistance practice sessions — Destiny, one of the five trans women from M13 — called to say she's trapped in her apartment, cops outside, she can hear them testing doors. Destiny doesn't have anyone else to call. Victor's center is closed — boarded up since the manhunt started. The phone tree Leta built reaches Leta.

Korede argues against going. This is the first time in Book 2B that he argues *loudly*. He has the documentation. He knows what the trend line says. He knows Webb's unit is out tonight. He says: "They're not looking for Destiny. They're looking for you." Leta knows this. She goes anyway. Korede follows. He was never going to let her go alone. He was always going to follow.

The street. Movement. They don't make it to Destiny's building. Webb's unit is three blocks from where Leta was told Destiny was sheltering. Whether Webb is there for Destiny or for Leta or for neither — whether this is targeted or the random intersection of a manhunt and a neighborhood — is never confirmed in the text. It doesn't matter. The system that produced Webb doesn't need to be aiming to hit.

Rb5+ — the check that changes nothing. In the moment before contact, something happens that looks like protection. This is for other stewards to define in their lanes — a warning, a call, a siren, an attempt by someone with authority to redirect Webb's unit. It doesn't work. The king walks through it.

Webb. Webb and Leta. This is not a long scene. Webb sees a Black trans woman on the street during a manhunt for super-powered terrorists. What Webb does next is what Webb has always done. The specific mechanism is the same one that killed Isaiah Bennett. Webb does not innovate. Webb does not need to.

Korede sees it. He is close enough to see it and not close enough to stop it. This distance — ten feet, fifteen feet, the distance between a brother's arm and his sister's body — is the distance he will carry into Book 3 and Book 4 and every book after.

Tess arrives. Not as Tess — as Gloom Girl. She's been running parallel to the manhunt, trying to protect civilians. She gets there in time for Leta to die in her arms. Not in time to prevent it. She removes her mask. Not a choice — a collapse. A refusal to let Leta die looking at a mask instead of a face.

Korede sees this too. He sees Tess's face under the mask. He knows Tess. He knows Tess is his sister's girlfriend. He now knows Tess is Gloom Girl. He processes this in the same three seconds he is processing his sister's murder. The two pieces of information fuse: *the person who could have saved her was right there the whole time and it wasn't enough.*

After. Korede doesn't scream. Korede doesn't run. Korede walks to where his laptop bag is — he brought it, because Korede always brings it, because the documentation is the rook on g1 and it goes everywhere he goes — and he opens it and he closes it. He doesn't look at the spreadsheet. He doesn't pull up the file. He closes the laptop and puts it back in the bag and the documentation is over.

The last image of M26: Korede standing. Tess on the ground holding Leta. Webb's unit moving on because the manhunt is still happening, because the city is still on fire, because a dead Black trans woman on the street during a crisis is not something that makes anyone stop. Korede standing with a closed laptop and an open future that has exactly one shape.

He does not say "I'm not sitting out anymore." Not yet. That line is for when someone asks him. Right now no one is asking. Right now he is standing in the specific silence of a boy who has just learned that documentation is a g1 rook and love is an f4 pawn and the king takes what it wants.

### What This Beat Leaves Room For

- **For Tess's steward:** The mask removal is Tess's moment as much as Korede's. Her steward defines what it costs Tess to show her face — the security implications, the identity exposure, the choice to let grief override operational discipline. This beat only describes what Korede *sees*. What Tess *feels* belongs to her steward.
- **For Ahdia's steward:** Where is Ahdia during the manhunt? If she's burning baseline on another front — if she's at 2% or 5% or whatever the number is by M26 — then Leta's death is the final proof that Ahdia's sacrifice model doesn't scale. You can't protect everyone by destroying yourself. You protect no one.
- **For the Bellatrix/Geneva steward:** The manhunt is the cover. Whether TRIOMF engineered the terrorist label specifically to create conditions like this — martial conditions where Webb can operate freely — or whether they merely exploited existing structures, the result is the same. Bellatrix didn't pull the trigger. Bellatrix built the room where triggers get pulled.
- **For Ben's steward:** Ben's faith collapse. A dead woman on the street and a boy with a laptop. If Ben sees the aftermath, or hears about it, or prays about it — his crisis of faith meets Korede's crisis of documentation. Two systems that promised to protect and didn't.
- **For the Reader Proxy:** The reader was given the trend line in M13. The reader knew. The reader had Korede's data. The reader did the same thing with that data that the police did: observed it, understood it, and could not reach through the page to change it. The reader is now Korede. The reader has a closed laptop.
- **For Book 3:** Korede enters Book 3 as a recruitable Go Squad member. His origin isn't anger — not yet. His origin is the closed laptop. The moment he stopped believing that watching and recording and reporting constitutes action. The rook never left g1. Korede does.

## STEWARD NOTES

**On the distance between M13 and M26:** Thirteen moves. In those thirteen moves, the game goes from "first piece lost" to "king takes pawn." I did not write what happens between them because other stewards own those moves. But the trajectory is: M13 is the night Korede built a column called "IF POLICE WON'T" and didn't fill it in. M26 is the night he stopped needing to. The spreadsheet's silence between those two entries is the loudest thing in the file.

**On the triplet convergence:** The f-pawn (Leta) is already gone by M26. She was captured at M9 — her overreach, her insistence on remaining visible, was punished the moment the position couldn't support it. She's been playing on borrowed time since M9. The g-pawn (Korede) recaptured at M10, stood on f4, and was taken by the king at M26. The rook (the documentation) never moved from g1. At checkmate — after everything — the rook is still there. Korede's laptop still has the file. The file is complete. The file is perfect. The file protected no one.

**On what I didn't write:** I didn't write Leta's internal experience of dying. That belongs to Tess's steward or to no one. I didn't write Korede's recruitment speech. That belongs to Book 3. I didn't write the specific mechanism of Webb's violence. The reader knows what American police do to Black trans women. The text doesn't need to perform it. Webb *does not innovate.* That sentence is the whole scene.

**On the rook:** Rg1 is the single cruelest piece in this triplet. The g-pawn and f-pawn at least moved. They at least *tried*. The rook sits on g1 from Move 12 to checkmate. Twenty-eight moves. It watches the whole game. It is forked at M25 and cannot escape. It is never captured — it is simply *irrelevant*. That's the documentation. That's every screenshot, every timestamp, every report filed, every automated email received eleven days late. The rook is still on the board when the game ends. It outlives both pawns. It is the last thing left standing and it did nothing.
