# Leah Turner — Steward Run 1 (Book 1)

**Steward:** Leah Turner (codename **Battlea**). White (Go Squad).
**Triplet (lens):** ♗ Bishop f1 — quick deployment (shared w/ Ruth) · ♟ d-pawn — first-swing aggression (shared w/ Ruth) · ♟ c-pawn — over-extension. **All three "fast and short-lived": immediate damage, immediate cost.**
**Owned:** M4, M7, M8, M20, reveal-aftermath (~M82+), CP-3 (M131–136).
**Canon held:** acts before analyzing; emotionally honest, cuts posturing; identity-crisis doubt turns INWARD, never cruel; real power = emotional safety not attitude; partner Victor; cries openly AND keeps swinging. Knows her strength was Ahdia's; does NOT know Ahdia is dying for it.
**Convergence:** read `convergence_M82_ahdia.md` + `convergence_M131_ahdia.md`. Diverged — wrote her tension, not Ahdia's.

## M4 — `4. Bg2` (the Bishop deploys)

**Chess reading (♗ quick deployment).** The f1 bishop fianchettoes to g2 — one fast move out of the corner, committed to the long diagonal in a single breath. This is the charge: she's *out*, she's *in it*, before anyone called for it. (Falls M20, no captures of her own — she'll have nothing on the scoresheet to show for the speed. That's the point: deployment isn't the same as landing.)

**Narrative meaning.** This is Leah at the start — already moving while the rest of the team is still talking strategy. She doesn't wait to be deployed; she deploys herself the second she smells injustice. She's the first one through the door, the first one to close distance, the first to put her body where the argument is. The room reads it as confidence, as the strong one being the strong one. It isn't confidence exactly — it's that hesitation genuinely isn't in her. She'd rather take a hit she didn't have to take than stand still one second too long while something's wrong. The bishop on the long diagonal looks unstoppable. Nobody, least of all Leah, is counting how few moves it's actually going to get.

## M7 — `7. dxc5 Bxc5` (first blood, taken back the same move)

**Chess reading (♟ first-swing).** The d-pawn lands the *game's first capture* — `dxc5`, first blood, before anyone else has touched anything. And it's gone the same move: `Bxc5`, taken back instantly. Decisive damage and immediate punishment, inside one breath. Her whole combat signature in two half-moves.

**Narrative meaning.** The first real fight, and Leah throws the first punch — lands it clean, drops the guy, *yes,* the feeling she joined up for, anger with somewhere to go at last. And she's hit back the same instant, because she over-committed to land it; she's bleeding before the high even fades. She gets up. That's the rhythm under all of it: she swings first, she pays first, she's on her feet first. To her this isn't recklessness, it's honesty — you don't get to hit the problem without the problem hitting back, and she'd rather feel both than feel neither. The team will remember she drew first blood. They won't clock that she's the one who got opened up to do it. (Shared piece w/ Ruth: Ruth reads d-pawn as CR-7, the treatment entering. Leah reads it as the punch. They never know they're the same pawn.)

## M8 — `8. c4 dxc4` (the one-move charge)

**Chess reading (♟ over-extension).** The c-pawn steps to c4 and is captured `dxc4` *the same move.* A one-move lifespan. The piece that exists purely to charge in and pay for it — over-extension as a born trait, not a mistake.

**Narrative meaning.** Right after first blood, she pushes too far — chases the next one, over-extends, gets caught out for it. This is the shadow side of the speed: the part of Leah that's already past where she should've stopped because stopping feels like flinching. It's not stupidity; it's that the cost lands a beat after the body's already decided. She charges, she's exposed, she eats it — and she'd do it again, because to her the alternative is standing safe while Tess or Victor takes the hit she could've taken instead. The c-pawn lives one move on purpose. So does this part of Leah: the over-extension *is* the protective ferocity, just pointed slightly past the target. She doesn't regret it. She just hurts.

## M20 — `20. Bxg2 Kxg2` (the Bishop falls)

**Chess reading (♗ taken out, no captures).** The fianchetto bishop is captured — `Bxg2` — and recaptured `Kxg2`. Removed with **zero captures to its name.** The charge-in-doesn't-land-taken-out pattern completes: she deployed fast and hard at M4 and comes off the board having scored nothing, traded away. Fast and short-lived, finished early.

**Narrative meaning.** The construction-site ambush (Ch11) — she charges in like always and this time it doesn't land. She's down, shot in the vest, the wind gone out of her, and the thing that's supposed to be true about her — *I'm the strong one, I hit and it works* — just... didn't. (This is also the move where the "super-strength" reads as real to her for the first time: she takes a round to the vest and gets back up, and she thinks *that's me, that's mine, I'm built for this.*) Her bishop comes off with nothing on the scoresheet — all that speed, no kill to show. She doesn't know yet how much that empty scoresheet is going to mean later. For now it just stings: she went in first and hardest and got taken out anyway, and the only thing she's sure of is that she'd go in first again. The grief here is small and seeded — a body that's supposed to be unstoppable, learning it's not. Save the big version for the reveal.

## Reveal-aftermath (~M82+) — *the identity crisis*

**Chess reading (all three pieces already spent).** Her triplet is *gone* — bishop off at M20, both pawns inside the first eight moves. Through the entire back half of the game she has no piece on the board. The fast-and-short-lived pattern finishes its sentence: she did all her damage early, paid for all of it early, and now she has to live the other hundred moves with nothing to move. Her absence from the late board IS the beat — the strength was front-loaded, and it was never hers.

**Narrative meaning.** The truth lands (~Ch25, after the Docks crack things open): the strength was Ahdia's. The vest she walked away from, the punches that landed, the door she went through first — all of it was Ahdia bending time around her. And it hits Leah exactly where she's proudest, knocks out the load-bearing wall: *was I ever actually strong? Or was I just standing where the strong thing happened?* This is the one she can't punch. There's no body to put herself in front of, no door to go through first — it's inside her, and her whole method is action-before-analysis, and there's nothing to act on. So she does the thing only Leah does: she doesn't go cold, doesn't go cruel, doesn't perform okay. She *feels* it, out loud, in front of the team — cries openly, furious and gutted at once, says the true ugly thing nobody else will say: *maybe I'm nothing. Maybe I never was.* The doubt turns **inward** — never at Ahdia, never weaponized. (Critical irony she carries: she knows the strength was Ahdia's, and it guts her pride. She does **not** know Ahdia is *dying* to give it — which would flip every flash of resentment into guilt. So the resentment stays clean and unresolved, pride-wound not guilt-wound, all book.) And here's the thing the reveal can't take from her, even if she can't see it yet: the person currently keeping the whole gutted team from spiraling — saying the scared thing so everyone else is allowed to be scared too, making the room safe to fall apart in — is *her.* That was never Ahdia's. That was always Leah. The redefinition starts here, but she doesn't believe it yet.

## CP-3 — M131–136 — THE SINGULARITY / THE FINISH (the human chain)

*Read `convergence_M131_ahdia.md`. Ahdia is leaving (e6) and being dragged back; she is not narrating the room. The room is Leah's. Diverged below — Leah's hands on the rope, not Ahdia's view of it.*

**Chess reading (no piece — only hands).** `133. e6 … 135. f5 … 136. Ng7 1-0.` Two pawns at the brink, neither queening; the knight plays the final move; Black resigns. Leah has **nothing on the board** — every piece she owned died in the first twenty moves. So she is not a piece in this position. She is one of the hands. The fast-and-short-lived triplet spent itself early precisely so that *this* — the part where strength isn't a capture at all — is all that's left of her, and it turns out to be the part that holds.

**Narrative meaning.** Ahdia's at the edge, going, transcending — and the team makes the chain, hands on hands on the rope, and Leah is in it. And this is where her whole book pays off in a way she'd never have scripted: the strongest she is all book is the moment she has *no* strength to use. There's no door to go through first, no punch to land, no body to throw in front of harm. Just a grip, and the refusal to let go, and the one thing that was always actually hers — she will not let this team shut down, she will not let go of the person, she keeps everyone holding when holding is all there is. She's crying openly and pulling at the same time; for Leah the feeling and the fight were never opposites, and right here that's the entire mechanism. She's pulling Ahdia back — the person whose time-bending made Leah "strong," the source of the wound that gutted her pride — and *none* of that resentment is in her hands now; only *come back, come back, you don't get to leave, you're worth the rope.* (Divergence per the convergence note — not everyone on the chain is pulling for the same reason. Some are saving a hero, some a weapon, some a stranger they fear. Leah is pulling for a *friend,* flat and total, no math, the way she throws every punch — and she'd be furious at anyone treating it as anything colder.) Then Firas dissolves and is gone, and the win they cheer (Ng7) she can barely feel through the loss. She holds the team in the white space after — the one who makes it okay to be a wreck. Her redefinition completes here without a single word about it: her real strength was never the punch. It was this. It was always this. (Irony she carries out the door: she still half-believes she was never strong. She just held a dying star to the ground with her bare hands and doesn't see that as the answer to her own question.)
