# Pacing Monitor — Book 1 Steward Run 1 Evaluation

**Agent:** Pacing Monitor (Production Crew, Role 8) · **Domain:** tension curves & rhythm
**Task:** Assess the collective tension curve of the 10 steward outputs against the 4-phase skeleton in `BOOK1_MOVE_MAP.md`.
**Date:** 2026-06-14 · **Confidence:** high (beat positions are explicit in the files)

---

## Domain & method

I read all 10 steward beat files plus the three convergence anchors and plotted **every owned beat by move number**, then measured density against the canon phase boundaries: **Opening M1–26, Imbalance M27–66, Grind M67–95, Climax M96–136.** I am assessing rhythm only — clustering, gaps, jams, variation, and whether the climax peak lands or rushes. Literal-time ordering (Timeline Keeper), whether a beat is thematically earned (Theme Guardian), what the reader knows when (Reader Proxy), and character-state availability (Status Tracker) are deferred below.

Method caveat: a steward "owns" a move; the actual chapter may stage it across a wider span. So a sparse move-stretch is a *risk flag*, not proof of a dead chapter — but the run as delivered is the raw material the Director blocks from, and where it clusters tells us where the prose will want to cluster.

## Findings

### F1 — The Grind doesn't grind. M67–95 is two moves of event in a 29-move phase. **[BLOCKER]**
The move map's thesis (sec. "Why this game fits") is **win by endurance, not fireworks** — the long attrition *is* the emotional arc. The run delivers the opposite: the Grind is the emptiest phase in the book, the M59→M80 stretch is a 21-move beat-vacuum, and all its energy is a single M80–82 fireworks spike (EMP trap + Docks reveal). As staged, the middle will read as "nothing, nothing, nothing, EXPLOSION" — the exact pacing failure the chosen chess game was meant to avoid. The endurance theme has no beats to be endured *in*.
**Fix:** Commission 3–4 low-amplitude "attrition" beats spread across **~M67, ~M72, ~M88, ~M92** — small costs, repeated patrols, the ledger ticking, the team winning-but-tiring. These should be deliberately *quiet* (see F5). Stewards with spent triplets by the Grind (Leah, Tess, Victor, Ben after M80) can carry presence-not-piece beats here. This is the single highest-priority pacing fix in the run.

### F2 — M59→M80 is a 21-move gap straddling the act seam. **[FIX]**
The largest single void in the book sits exactly where the Imbalance→Grind transition should be building dread toward the EMP trap. Ryu's M59 (system wakes late) and Ben's M80 (trap springs) are 21 moves apart with nothing between — so the trap springs with no on-ramp of mounting pressure. A trap needs the audience to feel the jaws closing.
**Fix:** Add a **~M66–72 "the noose tightens" beat** — Kain's machine visibly closing (he owns M80; a mid-stretch Kain beat seeding the institutional trap would pay off his own M17→M80 span and bridge the gap). Bridges the seam and gives M80 its build.

### F3 — Climax is back-loaded with an empty M96–107 entry ramp. **[FIX]**
Phase 4 opens with 12 dead moves (M96–107) before Ahdia's bidirectional-time discovery at ~M108. The climax *act* begins with a silence, then all five climax beats fire in its back two-fifths. The "march to the finish" has no opening stride.
**Fix:** Pull one beat into **~M100–104** — a quiet pre-climax positioning beat (a Ruth or Ryu "calm before" register works; both have late-game presence). Gives the climax a running start instead of a standing one.

### F4 — Endgame compression: three studs (6/7/8) + 10-steward convergence in ~20 moves; the peak risks landing rushed. **[FIX]**
Stud 6 (suit, M116–120), Stud 7 (autoinjector, M133–135), and Stud 8 (dissolution, M131–136) are stacked into a 20-move window, with Studs 7 and 8 *and* the full 10-hand human chain inside the same 6 moves (M131–136). The individual convergence beats are extraordinary (Firas's suit handoff, the autoinjector inversion, ten divergent why-we-pull readings) — but ten stewards' worth of climax in six moves is a genuine over-compression risk: the suit lull and the dissolution will fight for the same emotional oxygen, and any single beat (e.g. Leah's identity-crisis completion, Tess's first-loss) can get swallowed.
**What protects it:** the suit at M116–120 is explicitly framed as a *frozen-time lull* (Firas's file, move map Stud 6) — that's a built-in breath and it should be **protected and lengthened**, not trimmed, because it's the only decompression beat before the finish.
**Fix:** Do not add beats to M131–136 — it's already at capacity. Instead, **stage the all-hands chain sequentially** (Ruth organizes → individual grips land one at a time → Firas → dissolution → landing) so ten readings get discrete moments rather than a simultaneous wall. Treat M116–120 (suit) and a possible M124–127 quiet beat as the two breaths the peak needs to land instead of blur. Bourn's M128 missile is a good cold-procedural counter-beat *inside* the heat — keep it as a register-break.

### F5 — The run has almost no quiet/low-tension beats; it runs hot or empty, rarely soft. **[FIX]**
Variation is the weakest rhythmic dimension. Nearly every beat is a strike, a cost, a loss, or a reveal. The few genuine "quiet" beats — Ben M6 (setting the perimeter), Tess M30 (the Sertraline on the sink, maintenance), Victor M14 (holding ground) — are all in the front third. The back half (M59→136) has essentially **one** designed lull (Firas's suit). After M40 the curve only knows two settings: silent or spiking.
**Fix:** The attrition beats from F1 should be explicitly *low-amplitude* (a quiet patrol, a shared meal, a ledger entry, the team tired-but-okay). Tess's depression-as-weather register and Victor's community-presence register are the run's best quiet-beat engines and both go dark after the early game — bring them back as breaths in the Grind.

### F6 — Confirmed JAMS at M17, M26, M80, M82, M131–136 are load-bearing, not problems — but two sit adjacent. **[NOTE]**
The multi-steward jams are mostly well-placed act-pivots (M26 fakeout, M82 Docks, M131–136 finish). One structural note: **M80 (3 stewards) and M82/CP-2 (6 stewards) are two moves apart** — effectively a single mega-spike carrying the entire Grind. This is fine *if* F1/F2 give the Grind other beats so the spike has contrast; it becomes a problem only if the Grind stays empty (then M80–82 is the whole phase, and a phase with one note has no rhythm). Flagging the dependency: **F6 is benign only if F1 is fixed.**

## What's sound

- **The Opening (M1–26) paces beautifully** — dense but varied, with real quiet beats (Ben M6, Victor M14) braided into the action, building cleanly to the M26 fakeout. Best-paced phase in the run.
- **M27–M40 decompresses correctly** — the cadence-stretch after the Opening jam is exactly right; the book exhales before the costs mount.
- **The three convergence anchors are rhythmically well-positioned** — M26 (end of Act 1), M82 (mid-grind reversal), M131–136 (finish). The act-pivot jams land on the right moves.
- **Firas's suit beat (M116–120) is the one correctly-placed lull in the back half** — a frozen-time breath before the final march. The run's best instinct for pre-peak decompression. Protect it.
- **Per-beat tension *within* scenes is strong** — almost every individual beat has a clean build→cost shape (Leah's swing-and-pay, Tess's strike-and-comedown, Ben's precision-under-load). The problem is distribution between beats, not tension inside them.

## Verdict

**CONDITIONAL PASS — one BLOCKER, four FIXES.** The micro-rhythm is excellent and the act-pivots land on the right moves, but the macro-curve has a **structurally inverted middle**: the Grind (the phase the entire chess game was chosen to deliver "win by endurance") is the emptiest phase in the book, and the back half runs on two spikes (M80–82, M131–136) bracketing two long dead-zones. The finish is over-compressed but rescuable through sequential staging rather than cuts.

**Priority order for the Director:**
1. **F1 (BLOCKER)** — populate the Grind with 3–4 quiet attrition beats (~M67/72/88/92). Without this the book's core thesis has no rhythm to live in.
2. **F2 (FIX)** — bridge the M59→M80 trap with a ~M66–72 "noose tightens" beat.
3. **F4 (FIX)** — stage M131–136 sequentially; protect the M116–120 suit lull as the peak's breath.
4. **F3 (FIX)** — pull one beat into ~M100–104 to give the climax a running start.
5. **F5 (FIX)** — make the new Grind beats deliberately *quiet* to restore hot/soft variation.

Fix the Grind and the curve goes from "hot–empty–spike–empty–cram" to a genuine sustained-pressure middle with a landing finish. The material is there; it's the spacing that needs the Director's hand.
