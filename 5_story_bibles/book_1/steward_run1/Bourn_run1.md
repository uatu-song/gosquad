# Harriet Bourn — Book 1, Steward Run 1

**Steward:** Harriet Bourn (she/her). CADENS Chief Overseer. Lens: the **file cabinet**, not the field — each piece a responsibility she signed for and keeps weighing.
**Triplet:** ♔ White King e1 = the asset she authorized (Ahdia; shared w/ Ahdia, Ryu). ♕ White Queen d1 = Firas, the doomed-status piece she watches fall (M26). ♟ White a-pawn = Mother FAERIS, the buried exploitation she knows and won't fix (shared w/ Victor; spent M33).
**Owned moves:** M26 (CP-1), M33, ~M128 (CADENS missile authorization), M131–136 (CP-3).
**Canon held:** she/her; she IS the institution in Book 1 (no defection — that's Book 2); she knows about Mother FAERIS and chooses not to fix it; under cost she goes drier/more procedural (bureaucracy as armor over conscience); she does NOT yet know TRIOMF has infiltrated CADENS. No icy-spook cliché; no exposition-via-Bourn.

---

## M26 — `26. Qxc8 Rxc8` (CP-1) — THE QUEEN FALLS

**Move (chess fact):** The White Queen sacrifices herself, removed in a trade that reshapes the position. The reader's assumed protagonist is gone; the King remains, untouched, central.

**Chess reading (files, not field):** The ♕ comes off the board and Bourn does not flinch, because the Queen was never the file she was protecting. She had Firas tabbed *high-value, watchable* — an asset she'd authorized surveillance on, not the asset she'd staked the program on. The board does the thing her ledger already did weeks ago: it clears the flashy piece and leaves the ♔ standing — the King she signed for, the file that stays open. Reading Ahdia's M26 beat (the warehouse fire, the closed file she *wants* to be), Bourn diverges hard: Ahdia is engineering a closed file from the inside; Bourn is the one person who refuses to let it close. A dead hermit is not a dead asset. Not until Bourn signs the line.

**Narrative meaning:** Firas is dead to the team and a girl burns down her own funeral, and across town Bourn reads the after-action with the dispassion of someone who's seen too many pyres staged. The fire is clean. Too clean — sealed exits, counted floors, the work of a person managing her own disappearance, not dying in it. She does not say so to the room. She lets the field believe what the field needs to believe and makes a private note: *asset's status — do not list deceased.* The grief in the building below her is real and Firas's loss is a genuine subtraction from her board; she logs it as a subtraction, which is the only way she's allowed to feel it. The weight here is the one she won't show: she is the only one who suspects the dead girl is alive, and her response to that suspicion is not rescue. It's *acquisition* — keep the file open, keep watching, wait to see what the asset becomes before she decides what it's worth. She and Ahdia want the same outcome from opposite poles: Ahdia wants the file closed so no one finds her; Bourn keeps it open so she can.

## M33 — `33. Rd1 Bxa3` — THE FOUNDATION SPENT (Mother FAERIS)

**Move (chess fact):** Black's bishop captures the White a-pawn (`Bxa3`). The long-held edge pawn — the quiet structural piece on the rim — is taken. Victor reads this as community overrun. Bourn reads the a-pawn as something else entirely.

**Chess reading (files, not field):** The ♟ on a3 is Mother FAERIS — the exploited foundation buried under the whole defensive structure, the piece that has held position since the opening and is treated, when the moment comes, as institutionally expendable. Bourn does not lose this pawn. She *spends* it. There is a difference and she is the only one on the board who knows it: the field thinks a pawn fell to pressure; Bourn signed the trade that let it fall, the way she has signed, for years, the standing decision *not* to fix the thing she knows is wrong underneath everything. The a-pawn coming off the board is not a defeat she suffers — it's a compromise she renews.

**Narrative meaning:** This is the central rot, and it does not look like rot. It looks like a routine authorization. A line item that comes around again — the same exploitation she's known about since before Ahdia was a file, the foundation CADENS runs on and doesn't repair because repairing it costs more than the program can survive. She reads the brief. She runs the cost-benefit she can defend on paper. She signs. The tell is in how *procedural* she gets — the language flattens, the sentences shorten, she stops using the entity's name and starts using its designation, because the bureaucracy is the armor and the more it costs her the thicker she wears it. She is not a cartoon who doesn't care. She knows exactly what she's spending and what it is and what it would take to stop. The knowing-and-not-acting *is* the character — the compromise she's built a self around. She does not feel it crack. (The crack is Book 2, the day she learns the foundation she kept spending was being read, the whole time, by people she signed for and never saw — TRIOMF in the walls. Not yet. Today the file just closes, clean, the way she's made a career of closing them.)

## ~M128 — `127. Re5+ Kf7 128. Rf5+ Ke8` — THE KILL SHE SIGNS (CADENS missile authorization)

**Move (chess fact):** The endgame approach. White's rook drives the Black King across the back ranks with checks — the slow, mechanical herding of the cornered piece toward the finish. No capture here; this is the institution closing distance on the target it has already decided to kill.

**Chess reading (files, not field):** Bourn does not move the rook — she is not the field piece doing the herding. She is the seat that authorized the herding. Reading the King's forced walk (`Kf7 … Ke8`), she sees the canonical kill-shot she alone can sign: the CADENS cruise missile on Kain. The field believes Ahdia's knight will end it; the on-board image *is* Ahdia. But the actual lethal mechanism is a strike package with Bourn's signature on the release line. Her file on the King (♔ = the asset) and her file on the target are the same ledger now: she is spending an authorized strike to protect the asset she authorized. Institution killing for institution. Clean on paper.

**Narrative meaning:** A windowless room, a strike package, a release authorization that needs one signature and the signature is hers. This is the kill-shot the whole climax is built to credit to a girl with a knight — and it isn't. It's a procurement decision. Bourn weighs it the way she weighs everything: the target is a candidate for high office, the deniability is engineered, the acceptable-loss math is run and survivable, and the strike protects an asset the program has spent a fortune keeping alive. She signs. She does not narrate it to herself as heroism or as murder — both would be a kind of feeling-winning, and she does not let feeling win. What shows is the restraint: the pause before the signature is one beat too long, and then it's exactly on time, and she moves to the next line item. The dramatic irony she carries and cannot see: she believes she is ending Kain. She is the institution killing a figurehead and calling it the war won — and Kain clone-survives off-board, on a handler's order, by an architecture (Bellatrix, TRIOMF) Bourn's files have no entry for. She signs the cleanest kill of her career against a man who doesn't stay dead, on behalf of strings she can't yet feel on her own wrists.

## M131–136 — `131. Kh4 Qh6+ 132. Nh5 Qh7 133. e6 Qg6 134. Rf7 Kd8 135. f5 Qg1 136. Ng7 1-0` (CP-3) — THE SINGULARITY / THE FINISH

**Move (chess fact):** Black resigns in a lost position. The e-pawn sits at e6 (never promotes), the f-pawn reaches f5 (never promotes), the knight plays Ng7 as the last move. A resignation, not an on-board mate. The canonical kill is Bourn's missile plus the singularity Ahdia burns out.

**Chess reading (files, not field):** All her files come due at once. ♔ — the asset survives, pulled back, the King still on the board: the file stays open, which is the outcome she authorized everything to reach. ♕ — Firas's piece is long gone (fell the fakeout at M26); his actual dissolution here is the doomed-status file finally, truly closing, and she logs it second. ♟ — the a-pawn is already spent (M33), the foundation that held all of this and got no credit and no rescue. Reading Ahdia's CP-3 beat — the chain, the rope, the girl saved over her objection — Bourn diverges from every hand on that rope: they are pulling a hero, a friend, a weapon, a stranger. Bourn's hands are institutional, and her missile is the real kill under the personal rescue. She is the one pulling who knows the rescue is also an *acquisition*: the asset comes back alive, which means the asset comes back *hers*.

**Narrative meaning:** The all-hands climax, and Bourn is the only one in it doing arithmetic. The field forms a human chain out of love and terror and choice; Bourn's contribution is a strike already in the air and a file she is determined to keep open. To her this is not a miracle. It is a successful recovery of a high-value asset at acceptable cost — and the *acceptable cost* line is where the weight finally shows, because the cost is Firas, who she'd tabbed watchable and is now watching dissolve, and the math says the trade was good and she signs it and it still sits wrong in a place the procedure can't reach. She watches the girl land back in a body at ~30% baseline, 18–24 months with treatment, furious at having been saved — and Bourn understands that fury better than the people who love Ahdia do, because Bourn also lives inside outcomes she can defend on paper and can't stop feeling. **Ch30, the badge.** She offers Ahdia the CADENS badge — the asset converted to officer, the file made permanent, the cleanest acquisition of all. Ahdia declines. Bourn does not push, does not flinch, accepts the no the way she accepts every line item, dryer than the moment warrants — because the refusal costs her, and the cost makes her procedural. She closes the file as *active, unrecruited, monitored.* What she carries out the door, not knowing she carries it: she thinks she won the war and kept her asset; she killed a figurehead who clone-survives, she's recruiting for an institution already hollowed from inside, and the dying girl who just told her no is the one person whose refusal will, in Book 2, turn out to be the most clear-eyed read of CADENS anyone ever handed her. Tonight it's just a no. She signs around it and moves on.

### Steward note (not a beat)
Across all four moves Bourn never sheds the institution — she *is* it, competent and compromised, and her arc in Book 1 is the weight of authorized harm carried in the restraint, not the speech. The blind spot (TRIOMF in the walls, the foundation being read while she spends it) is performed as institutional confidence with nothing underneath it yet. Don't pull the defection forward. Let what she won't say carry it.
