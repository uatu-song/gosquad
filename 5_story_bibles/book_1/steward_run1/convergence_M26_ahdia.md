# Convergence M26 (CP-1) — THE QUEEN FALLS — Ahdia's beat

**Anchor steward:** Ahdia Bacchus (♔ King / ♘ Knight / ♟ e-pawn). Written first. Stewards present: Firas, Bourn, Kain. **Read this, then write tension — do not conform.**

**Chess fact:** `26. Qxc8 Rxc8` — the White Queen sacrifices herself, removed from the board in a trade that reshapes the position. The piece the reader assumed was the protagonist is gone. The King remains.

**Chess reading (through her triplet):**
The ♕ — Firas, the apparent hero — is removed from the center, and the board's whole center of gravity drops onto the ♔, which is to say onto *her*. She has spent 26 moves being the square nobody reads as the story. Now the Queen is gone and she is what's left, and she doesn't want to be. Her ♘ is between captures (M11 behind, M40 ahead); her ♟ is one move from finally waking at M29. The fakeout breaks here: the protagonist was never the flashy piece. It was the one being protected the whole time. She hates that it's her.

**Narrative meaning:**
The warehouse fire. The team believes Ahdia died in it — "her pyre" — and she lets them, because a dead hermit is a closed file and a closed file is safe, and being unfindable is the only kind of safe she trusts. She watches the building burn on a feed (intimacy at a safe distance, the way she does everything), counts the floors, notes which exits she left and which she sealed. Resistance active: **being known** — and she wins this round by erasing herself, which is the wound dressed up as strategy. The flat fact she won't soften: *they'll mourn the version of me that was already dead inside, so really I'm just making it official.* What it costs: nobody comes looking, which is the point, which is also the loneliest thing she's ever engineered. She doesn't know — can't know — that the person who'll eventually find her alive (Ruth, at the Docks) is the one who'll tell her she's worth the rope. Right now she's just a girl who faked her own funeral so she could keep working.

> **For co-located stewards:** Ahdia is *absent from the room* here — she's a feed, a fire, a closed file. Firas (the Queen falling) and Bourn (watching the fakeout, refocusing the institution on the King) and Kain (spending the Queen, reading the board) are reacting to a death that is a lie. Write the gravity of believing she's gone, and the divergence of what each of you does with that belief. Bourn may suspect the file isn't really closed. Kain may not care about her at all yet. Firas's grief is real and misplaced. Tension, not agreement.
