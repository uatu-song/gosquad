# Firas Bacchus — Steward Run 1 Beats

**Character:** Firas Bacchus | **Faction:** White (Go Squad)
**Triplet:** ♕ White Queen (d1) — the protagonist fakeout / how the world sees him · ♖ White Rook (a1) — the crime-fighting self / sidelined M80 · ♟ White f-pawn — the autoinjector / the sacrifice that never queens
**Arc through the triplet:** descending status → ascending meaning. Queen (perception) → Rook (action) → Pawn (sacrifice). He runs from stillness the whole book; the only stillness he accepts is the one that saves Ahdia.

---

## M1 — `1. d4` (the opening move; White claims the center first)

**Chess reading (♕):** The Queen's-pawn game opens, and the board arranges itself around the highest-status piece — the Queen, him. The reader's eye goes to d1 the way a crowd's eye goes to the guy doing the rooftop work. This is the fakeout being set: everything looks like it's about to be his story.

**Narrative meaning:** He's already moving before the scene starts — a vault, a drop, a three-point landing he didn't quite stick. He puts himself between a mugging and the kid getting mugged because that's not a decision, it's a reflex that happens to him faster than thought. He reaches for the cool line — *"This ain't Burger King, you don't get it your way"* — and it lands sideways, and he commits to it anyway, too loud, grinning. Then the address that matters: he texts Ahdia, *you up?*, the way he does every night, knocking on a door he doesn't understand is locked from the inside. He reads the whole city and misreads only her. He thinks if he can just get her out into the air, into motion, she'll come back. The opening nobody reads as the real story — he's certain it's his.

---

## M26 — `26. Qxc8 Rxc8` (CP-1 · THE QUEEN FALLS) (the White Queen is traded off; the apparent protagonist leaves the board)

*Read Ahdia's M26 beat — she is absent here, a fire on a feed, a closed file. This room is mine. I do not know she faked it. My grief is real and aimed at a lie.*

**Chess reading (♕):** The Queen is gone — not beaten in a duel, spent in a trade that the position needed. The flashy piece comes off and the board's gravity drops onto the King, the small protected square nobody was watching. The fakeout breaks exactly here: the hero was never the hero. But he doesn't feel it as structure. He feels it as a bullet.

**Narrative meaning:** The docks job goes wrong and a round goes through him — through the side, not clean, enough. He's down before the protector reflex can fire, which is the part that scares him more than the wound: he couldn't step between anything. And in the same stretch of bad hours, the warehouse burns, and they tell him Ahdia was inside. He doesn't get to disbelieve it — he's flat on a gurney with Ruth's hands in him, unable to *go,* unable to run at it, and the one thing motion was always for (keep anyone else from falling) has failed in both directions at once. He grieves the sister he thinks he just lost without ever knowing she's the reason he ever looked enhanced. The thoughtless thing surfaces, not aimed at anyone, just the dark water rising: *she was already gone, I just kept knocking.* He'd be horrified to hear himself. The Queen is off the board. He thinks the story is over and that he's the one who failed it.

## M80 — `80. Rxf7+ Kxf7` (♖ ROOK REMOVED) (the White Rook is captured by the Black King and leaves the board)

*Canon: this is the sidelining, NOT a Kain duel. The injury takes him off the field. The board enacts his removal from active duty — not a defeat in combat.*

**Chess reading (♖):** The Rook — the direct, charging, linear self, the most-captures piece in the game — comes off the board. Not in a brave exchange he chose. It's simply *removed,* taken out of the action while the rest of the side keeps grinding. The crime-fighting Firas is gone from the field and the game goes on without him for fifty more moves. That's the cruelty of it: the board doesn't even need him to win.

**Narrative meaning:** The midgame is the wound healing wrong and the stillness it forces on him, and the stillness is the most dangerous place he goes. He watches the team operate on a comms feed from a couch he can't stand up off of fast enough — and the team is *fine.* They're winning without him. Every patrol he misses is a patrol where someone might fall and he isn't there to step between, and that thought has teeth. He paces the apartment when he can pace, reorganizes gear he can't use, reaches for the next task and finds there isn't one, and underneath the boredom the dark water comes up to his chin. This is the resistance the book grinds hardest: *stillness equals helplessness equals the thing he's been outrunning since his parents died.* He covers it with bad jokes that come out brittle now, the brightness strained thin. He does not say he wants to stop existing. He just stops being able to think of a reason to be in the room. The Rook is off the board, and being unable to help is, for him, the closest thing to dying he experiences before the end.

## ~M116–120 — `116. Rd3 Kf8 ... 120. Re3` (THE SUIT) (quiet rook maneuvering; the position is being arranged for the final push)

**Chess reading (♖→♟ handoff):** Slow, patient repositioning — the Rook self stepping back, making room. His piece is winding down toward the f-pawn that will matter. This is him *handing over* the part of himself that charges and takes hits, because he's worked out, in some place he won't name, that the next move isn't his to make.

**Narrative meaning:** He gives her the suit. From his side it isn't a costume change and it isn't powers — he doesn't know about powers, he never will — it's his armor and the closest thing he has to a name. He's back on his feet for the heist, moving again, and the relief of motion is so total it's almost funny, and then he does the one thing his whole psyche resists: he takes the suit off his own body and holds it out to her. *"Built it for me. Fits you better."* He fumbles even this — reaches for something dignified, lands on a joke about the shoulders being too big, barefoot kid drowning in his tac gear. He thinks he's lending it. He thinks he's getting it back. The protector reflex has finally found a form he can't outrun: not stepping between her and the danger this time, but putting his shell on her and stepping aside. He does not understand that he's just dressed his sister for the role that was always hers and quietly retired from his own. He thinks they're going in together. He's already started leaving and doesn't know it.

## M133–135 — `133. e6 / 134. Rf7 / 135. f5` (THE AUTOINJECTOR) (Ahdia's e-pawn reaches the 6th; Firas's f-pawn reaches f5 — one square from queening)

*Canon line: he injects HER, not himself. "Oh hey. I knew you were in there somewhere."*

**Chess reading (♟):** The f-pawn — dormant fifty-eight moves, the smallest and truest piece, the one with no status at all — finally advances to f5. One square from queening. It will never queen. It moves *to enable the win,* and the move itself is the whole point: a pawn pushed into the position where its only remaining function is to be spent. Beside it her e-pawn sits at e6, also at the brink, also frozen. Two smallest selves at the edge of becoming, and neither crosses.

**Narrative meaning:** He finds the last dose. One autoinjector, and for half a second the old arithmetic runs — *use it, get back up, keep moving, there's more to do* — and then he looks at her. She's at the edge of herself, leaving, going somewhere bright and dimensionless he can't follow, and for the first time in the whole book he actually *sees* her — not the depressed sister he kept trying to fix, not the door he kept knocking on. Her. *"Oh hey,"* he says, like he just ran into someone he hadn't seen in years, because in a way he has. *"I knew you were in there somewhere."* And he puts the needle in her arm, not his. No speech. No swelling music. The man who never sat still, who outran the dark water for thirty years by refusing to stop — he stops. He chooses it. The resistance that held the whole book inverts in one quiet motion: he is not fighting helplessness anymore, he is *accepting* the one loss he swore he'd never accept, because the trade is her life, and that's the only trade he was ever going to take. He still thinks he's just out of doses. He has no idea he's not coming back.

## M131–136 — `131. Kh4 Qh6+ 132. Nh5 Qh7 133. e6 Qg6 134. Rf7 Kd8 135. f5 Qg1 136. Ng7 1-0` (CP-3 · THE SINGULARITY / THE FINISH) (Black resigns in a lost position; neither pawn promotes; the knight plays the last move)

*Read Ahdia's M131 beat — she is barely in her body, being saved over her objection, furious at the price. This is my dissolution. I do not know it's transcendence not death; I do not know I'm displaced not dead; I do not know I return Book 7. Perform the certainty that this is the end. And diverge: she did not want the rope. I am the one tying it.*

**Chess reading (♟ + ♖ resolved):** Everything I am converges at the brink and none of it crosses. The f-pawn at f5, the autoinjector, spent without transforming. The Rook long gone. The Queen long gone. All three of my pieces — the seen, the doer, the truest — emptied out by the climax, status fully descended, meaning fully arrived. The knight plays Ng7 and Black resigns; the team reads it as the kill. My piece isn't even on the board for the finish. I'm not in the picture of the win. I'm the move that made it possible, already off-square, already gone. Diminishing status into one act of total meaning — that's the whole arc landing in a single still beat.

**Narrative meaning:** He gives the needle, and then the singularity takes him. From inside it there's no terror — that's the thing he'd want someone to know if he could: he goes *serene.* The motion he ran on his whole life finally resolves into stillness and the stillness doesn't hurt, because for once it means he saved someone instead of failing to. He smiles. He thinks it's permanent. He thinks *I'm not losing you again* came true at last and the cost was the right cost and he'd pay it ten more times. He doesn't see the human chain hauling her back down the rope, doesn't hear them cheer Ng7, doesn't know Bourn's missile is the real kill or that Kain walks away in another body. He dissolves believing he is dead, believing it worked, believing his sister gets to live and that's the only sentence that matters. **And here is the divergence from her room:** she is being saved against her will, furious she didn't get to choose, certain she should have been the one to go. He chose. Freely, smiling, certain it was right. They are both at the brink in the same instant and they could not be more opposed — she'd have traded her life for his, and he just did the reverse, and got there first, and to her that will be the wound. He never learns she didn't want him to. He's simply gone, and as far as he or anyone in that room knows, gone is forever. (It isn't. He doesn't know that either.)
