## THE ENGINE (why this fight is the reason he does it)

Ch17 has Kain price **the blur** as an asset and buy two instruments to
neutralise it: an EMP and four Tanks. In this room **the EMP worked.** Powers
are off. Only Tess's teleport survives and it is degraded — *"a pause where no
pause used to live."*

And four unarmed people are taking twelve armed professionals apart anyway.

So the thing beating his men is not the asset he priced. **He did not misprice
the asset. He mispriced the category.** Training cannot be jammed, bought,
EMP'd, or acquired. It is the one thing in his house he has no instrument for.

The only unbuyable object in the room is on the plinth.

**Every beat below serves that.** The fight's virtuosity IS the motive: the
better they are, the less he can purchase, and the shorter the distance to the
Heart. His integration is not calculated. **It is a man discovering, at
seventy-odd seconds, that his entire method does not work — and reaching for
the one thing left.**

**Ch27's "He had priced that before he ever put it in his chest" STAYS** and is
now a lie he tells himself afterward. Do not reconcile it. The contradiction is
the character.

---

## THE STAGING GIFT: KAIN IS THE AUDIENCE

Leah wheels him into the middle of the floor as a bulletproof obstruction
(existing beat). That accidentally does three things and all of them should be
exploited:

1. He is at the **centre of a 360° room** and cannot look away.
2. He is **zip-tied** — the man whose whole self is the ability to act on a
   decision can only watch and price.
3. He **turns slowly under his own chandelier**, so the room revolves past him
   like a stage. The existing text already has this. It is the best thing in
   the chapter and nothing has been built on it.

Stage the fight so he sees it. His break is the second movement's payload.

# MOVEMENT 1 — SMOKE (he can only hear it)

**Story beats**
- Victor tips the champagne bucket; goggles out from under the catering linen
  (KEEP). A dozen men lose the room in one breath, and three do not.
- **Leah's heels come off first, and they come off as something** — kicked or
  thrown into the nearest man's face, not stepped out of. She fights the rest
  of the chapter barefoot in the gown, which is why she is barefoot in the
  alley later. *(Pays ch21: the slit "was clearly a tactical decision.")*
- Leah takes the wheeled chair apart for two armrests (KEEP), and hands one to
  Victor without looking, because she knew where his hand would be.
- **THE DERBY WHIP.** Ben braces and Leah slingshots off his forearm to cross
  the room in one move at a speed she cannot generate alone. *(Pays ch12 —
  jammer — and ch7 — Ben as "fleshy brick wall." Her sport, made physical for
  the first time in the book, and with no powers in it.)*
- The team makes **no sound**. Every noise in the room is the guards: furniture
  going over, men calling positions, men walking into each other. *(Pays ch9's
  spec: "making their footfalls as inaudible as possible.")*
- Ben works the door end, Victor the window end, they meet in the middle and
  stay there, back to back, turning like one slow animal (KEEP — and this
  should now read as the routine's known shape, not improvisation).

**Emotional beats**
- Tess: relief, then something colder. They are better without her than she
  expected, and the copy bar will not hurry.
- Kain: mild interest. He is still pricing. Smoke is a tactic; tactics have
  vendors. He assumes he is watching equipment.

**Consequences**
- The smoke advantage exists and is finite. Say so on the page.
- Kain has heard the fight but not seen it. He has an explanation for it.

**What could happen next**
- Someone breaks the window and takes the weather away.

# MOVEMENT 2 — LIGHT (this is the beat that breaks him)

**Story beats**
- **Ben's wall run** — three steps up the paneling, off it sideways, a guard
  through the west glass (KEEP, EXPAND). Four-foot hole. Smoke pours out and up
  the face of the house.
- *"Their advantage had been the weather. They had just broken the window it
  lived behind."* (KEEP VERBATIM — it is the movement's hinge.)
- **And the team keeps winning in the clear.** This is the whole point of the
  movement. In plain light, against men who can see them, with no enhancement
  and no cover:
  - **Bodies as apparatus** — one lifts another to a grip; a throw that is a
    transport, not an attack; someone using a teammate's back as the first step
    of a climb. *(Ch9: "lifting and tossing one another to reach grips.")*
  - **The room as weapon** — chandelier chain, drapes, the plinth's glass case,
    bookcases, the balcony rail, the oak desk. Nobody picks up a gun.
  - **Sequencing** — each of them opening the next one's line, read as one
    animal. *(Rhymes forward to ch27's "each of them opening the next one's
    line" — show it HERE first, without powers, so ch27's version reads as the
    same team plus Ahdia.)*
- **Kain watches it from the chair, and stops pricing.** One clean tell: the
  man who has assessed every object in the book **stops looking at the fight**
  and looks at the plinth. That is the decision, and it happens on the page.

**Emotional beats**
- Kain: not fear. **Category failure.** He bought the instrument for the thing
  he could see, and the thing beating him was never on the invoice. Krav maga
  and judo and four years in a converted warehouse are not for sale.
- Tess: pride that hurts. She is watching the thing Firas built work perfectly
  on the night Firas could not come.
- The guards: this is where they stop being an obstacle and become an audience
  too.

**Consequences**
- Kain's decision is made in this movement and the reader sees him make it.
  What the reader never sees is him touch it.

**What could happen next**
- Reinforcements who did not watch the first two movements.

**Consequences**

# THE OPEN DECISION — BOTH STAGED, NOT CHOSEN

## A) Tess watches movements 1–2 on the twelve-camera grid, enters for 3

**Buys.** The director's view — she reads the fight the way she reads comments,
and ch16 already gave her a film vocabulary ("a Hong Kong action movie somebody
had shot in one long take"). The choreography becomes legible *because* she is
reading it as footage, which licenses the Cirque/Chan staging without the prose
straining. The blackout becomes a deliberate cut. Her arrival in movement 3 is
an entrance. And it is the status quo — the existing line *"she read the fight
like you read a storm from indoors"* already does it, so nothing needs
re-motivating.

**Costs.** The best fight in the book happens **on a screen.** Kain's break
arrives at two removes — the reader watches Tess watch a camera watch Kain — and
that is the payload of movement 2. Tess is in no danger for two-thirds of the
chapter. The apron cams are established as "dark and jouncing," so the twelve
house cameras have to carry it, and house cameras do not cover a room the way
the scene needs.

## B) Tess on the molding early, watching in green and gray

**Buys.** Immediacy. Kain's face first-hand, which is where the movement-2
payload actually wants to land. She is inside the danger. The vertical position
gives the Cirque staging a natural camera and sets up *"the whole room a route
instead of a fight."* And the final choice is harder because she has been close
enough to intervene the whole time.

**Costs.** It breaks the jump numbering, which the brief protects — she is on
the molding at eight, and the breakers are five. **And it creates a character
problem the chapter cannot afford:** Tess perched above her team while they are
shot at, not helping, for two movements. Every good reason she might have
(carrying Jericho, the copy bar, the degraded jump) is already spent elsewhere
in the chapter. Fixing it means inventing a new constraint, and a new constraint
in the last third of Book 1 is expensive.

**Noted, not proposed:** a split — grid for movement 1, molding for movement 2
— would buy Kain's break first-hand while keeping her out of movement 1. It has
the same numbering cost as B. **Not choosing; flagging that the decision may not
be binary.**
