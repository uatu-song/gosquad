> **What blocking does:** decides, across the whole book, *which beats become which scenes, in what order, in whose POV, and how the convergence points choreograph and intercut.* It does NOT write prose — it produces the staged skeleton prose is generated against.

## Framework (conventions for all 4 acts)

### Scene unit
A **scene** = one continuous POV moment in one place/time, fed by one or more steward move-beats. A **chapter** = 1–3 scenes. The move→chapter scaffold from the move map holds (~30 chapters, ~4.5 moves/ch); blocking divides each chapter's move-range into scenes.

### POV approach — DECIDED 2026-06-14: HARD FAKEOUT (Ahdia withheld)
**Multi-POV, third-limited; Ahdia's interiority WITHHELD for all of Act 1.**
- **Firas opens** (Ch1) and carries the early foreground — the reader is *meant* to read the book as his story.
- **Ahdia appears only from the OUTSIDE in Act 1** — the weird shut-in sister (Firas's frustrated visits, Ruth's awkward read). Her saves are felt only as *the team's impossible luck/skill.* **No interiority.** The reader is fooled alongside the team.
- **The warehouse fire (M26) lands as her REAL death** — no cut to her watching, no reader complicity. The reader grieves with Firas.
- **The POV cracks open into Ahdia early in Act 2** (the reveal-alive pivot): we finally enter the "dead" sister's head, alive, and realize she's been the secret source all along. **That crack is the book's biggest structural turn**, and it detonates Act 1's irony *retroactively* — the reader re-reads every save.
- **Supporting POVs** surgical: Ruth (the Docks reveal), the team (powers landing from the outside), **Kain** for sealed antagonist beats.

### Convergence choreography (M26 / M82 / M131–136)
At each CP, multiple stewards wrote the same moment. Blocking decides intercut + POV + sequence:
- **Anchor POV** = whose head we're in for the spine of the scene.
- **Intercut** = which other stewards' beats cut in, and in what order.
- Ahdia's convergence notes drive this: she's *absent* at M26 (a feed — and under the hard fakeout, that feed is HELD from the reader), *unconscious by the end* of M82, *barely-present* at M131–136 — so those rooms belong to the co-located POVs, with Ahdia threaded in obliquely.

### Intercut philosophy
- **Sealed antagonist thread:** per the reader-irony locks, Kain's clone-survival and Bellatrix-watching stay sealed to the epilogue — so Kain's Act 1–3 beats intercut as *menace and machine*, never tipping the epilogue reveals.
- **The Grind:** the quiet attrition beats (M70/M72/M88) interleave to convey *sustained pressure* — short, low-amplitude cuts between the louder spikes, never stacked.

### Deferred-fix handling (from the eval)
- **CP-3 sequential staging (V6, quad-consensus):** the climax beats (entry ramp ~M100–104 → suit ~M116–120 → near-transcendence M133 → autoinjector M133–135 → Ng7/dissolution M131–136) run **in sequence with breathing room**, not crammed. The suit lull is protected as the peak's one breath.
- **Rescue out-weighs kill (Theme F2):** at the climax, the **human chain stays in foreground**; the Kain-kill (missile + singularity) plays in the *background register* so the felt climax is "she gets chosen," not "the monster dies."

### Output format (per chapter)

```
**Scenes:** N.1 — [POV] · [place/time] · feeds: [steward beats] · [what happens]

**Dramatic irony live:** [what the reader knows that characters don't]
```

## ACT 1 — *Origin* (M1–M26, Ch1–8) — the template

**Act function:** sell the fakeout (Firas's story), keep Ahdia a closed door (outside only), let the team look unstoppable while the *cost stays invisible*, and end on the warehouse fire as her apparent death (CP-1, M26) — the reader fully fooled. Tension: quiet opening → impossible saves → the "death."

### Ch1 — The Rooftop (M1–M3, Act 1)
**POV:** Firas.
**Scenes:** 1.1 — Firas · night patrol · feeds: Firas M1 · the vault, the mugging he steps between, the fumbled "This ain't Burger King," the *you up?* text to a sister who won't answer. He reads the whole city and misreads only her.
**Intercut / staging:** clean single-POV open. The reader is *meant* to lock onto Firas as the protagonist. (Tess M3 / Leah M4 piece-beats held to Ch2.)
**Dramatic irony live:** none — the fakeout depends on the reader having *no* idea it isn't his story.

### Ch2 — The Closed Door (M4–M6, Act 1)
**POV:** Firas.
**Scenes:**
- 2.1 — Firas · the parkour academy / team · feeds: Leah M4, Ben M6 · the Go Squad as Firas's crew; the team's shape (Leah charges, Ben holds structure). Reads as Firas's ensemble.
- 2.2 — Firas · the penthouse (visiting Ahdia) · feeds: Stud 2 (strained relationship, *from his side*) · he knocks; she barely answers. The depressed hermit sister seen entirely through his frustrated, loving outside — TV at 2 a.m., the dead-parents money, the door locked from the inside. **We never enter her head.**
**Intercut / staging:** the cut from Firas's bright motion to the still-dark apartment is the book's first thesis — but stays in HIS POV. The sister is a closed door, to him *and* the reader.
**Dramatic irony live:** none — the reader reads Ahdia as a supporting character (the shut-in sister), exactly as intended.

### Ch3 — First Blood (M7–M8, Act 1)
**POV:** Ruth (introduced).
**Scenes:** 3.1 — Ruth · Ahdia's bathroom · feeds: **Ruth M7 (the gunshot — Firas shot investigating Kain; CR-7 saves him)**, Leah M7/M8 (first-blood / over-extension as the team's combat signature, intercut from the field) · Ruth pushes her dismissed CR-7 into Firas's body; the prototype spends itself saving him. **Firas benched here** (the single canonical wounding).
**Intercut / staging:** Ahdia is present only as the silent owner of the bathroom — a shape in the doorway, still no interiority. Ruth introduced as competence-under-pressure.
**Dramatic irony live:** none.

### Ch4 — Impossible Luck (M9–M11, Act 1)
**POV:** the team (Firas recovering / Ruth on comms).
**Scenes:** 4.1 — team · the field · feeds: **Ahdia M11 (the first save — staged from the OUTSIDE)** · the impossible happens: a moment that shouldn't have been survivable is. The team credits luck, adrenaline, training. The reader sees only the *effect*, never the cause.
**Intercut / staging:** the engine starts and the reader can't see it. **Ahdia's M11 interiority is HELD** — it pays off at the Act-2 POV crack. The save is felt as the team's impossible luck.
**Dramatic irony live:** none yet — the reader is fooled with the team. (The hard-fakeout payoff: when the POV cracks in Act 2, the reader re-reads every one of these saves.)

### Ch5 — The Squad That Can't Lose (M12–M14, Act 1)
**POV:** Firas (recovering) + the team.
**Scenes:** 5.1 — team · the field · feeds: Victor M14 (community roots / both-and presence) · the team operating, looking *enhanced* (secretly Ahdia, unseen). Victor holds the line of normal; the squad seems unstoppable.
**Intercut / staging:** the "powers" land entirely from the outside — the reader credits skill alongside the team. The fakeout tightens.
**Dramatic irony live:** none — the reader is inside the team's illusion.

### Ch6 — The Trap's First Teeth (M15–M17, Act 1)
**POV:** Victor + Ben.
**Scenes:** 6.1 — Victor/Ben · a police confrontation · feeds: **Victor M17 (the ethical strike + the cross-faction lost-youth knight)**, Ben M17 (precision undone by a foot soldier) · Victor's one clean intervention, answered by the system's cheapest unit (police-ambush *seed*, Stud 3). His grief-lens (the lost kid) lands on the same beat as his action.
**Intercut / staging:** Kain's g-pawn (M17) intercut as cold machine — the antagonist spends a foot soldier and barely notices. First taste of the institutional threat that pays off at M80.
**Dramatic irony live:** none on the powers; the *institutional threat* is the live thread now.

### Ch7 — Earned (M18–M21, Act 1)
**POV:** the team + Ruth.
**Scenes:**
- 7.1 — Tess/Leah · the field · feeds: Tess M19 (the strike that costs itself), Leah M20 (charges, doesn't land, takes a round to the vest — "that's me, I'm built for this") · the "super-strength" reads as real to Leah for the first time.
- 7.2 — Ruth · the field/aftermath · feeds: **Ruth M20 (the bishop dies — science stops being enough; she steps out of the lab into command)** · Firas sidelined; Ruth starts leading without wanting to.
**Intercut / staging:** the team takes hits and wins anyway; the *cost* (Ahdia's depletion) is entirely off-page — the reader, like the team, has no idea anyone is paying for this. The wins feel *earned.* That unpaid-for quality is the fakeout's tension; it pays off when the reader learns the price at the Act-2 crack.
**Dramatic irony live:** none — the reader is inside the team's illusion.

### Ch8 — The Pyre (M22–M26, Act 1) — **CP-1: THE QUEEN FALLS**
**POV anchor:** Firas (grief, in-scene) — intercut Bourn (institutional) + Kain (machine).
**Scenes:**
- 8.1 — Firas · the hospital/safehouse · feeds: **Firas M26 (laid up from the M7 wound; the warehouse burns; they tell him Ahdia was inside; the grief — "she was already gone, I just kept knocking")** · the apparent-hero, already benched, gutted by the death of the sister.
- 8.2 — Bourn · CADENS · feeds: **Bourn M26 (the Queen falls; the institution keeps its eyes on the King — the asset)** + Kain's machine in the wider frame · the cold institutional read of a death the reader is grieving.
**Intercut / staging:** **the fakeout's death blow, played straight.** Ahdia's M26 "watches her own pyre on a feed" beat is **HELD** (hard-fakeout decision): we do NOT cut to her alive. The reader grieves with Firas, believing the sister is dead and the apparent protagonist has lost everything. Bourn's institutional frame is the only hint something larger is in play. **This is where the reader thinks the book just ended its real story.**
**Dramatic irony live:** none yet — *and that's the point.* The reader is fully fooled; the irony detonates retroactively at the Act-2 POV crack.
**→ Sets up the Act-2 pivot:** the POV cracks open into Ahdia (alive) early in Act 2 — the book's biggest structural turn, recasting all of Act 1.
