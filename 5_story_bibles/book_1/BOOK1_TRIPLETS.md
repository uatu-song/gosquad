## Policy Decisions

### Triplets are rebuilt per book
Each book's triplet assignments are derived fresh from the chess game selected for that book. Ahdia's Book 1 triplet (King + Knight + e-pawn) does not constrain her Book 2B triplet. The triplet is a lens onto *this game*, not a permanent character schema. Characters keep their identity, voice, and arc across books — but the chess-piece interpretive layer reboots when the game changes.

### Cross-faction lenses are authorized
A character's triplet may include pieces from the opposing faction when that piece *defines or shaped* the character. Victor's lens includes the Black Knight b8 because the cycle of gang violence that keeps taking kids from his community center is the lost-youth shape he reads into every action. The piece is not under his control; it is the shape of his grief. Cross-faction pieces in a triplet are interpretive only — the character does not move them — but they are part of how this character reads the board.

### Pieces may recur across multiple characters' triplets
Divergence is the point. The White King appears in Ahdia, Ryu, and Bourn's triplets. Each reads it differently: Ahdia as her passive identity, Ryu as the square he stands beside, Bourn as the asset she authorized. Same piece, three meanings, three valid simultaneous readings.

### Pawn arcs are first-class data
Several triplets lean on specific pawn movements (Ahdia's e-pawn march, Firas's f-pawn push, Ruth's d-pawn sacrifice). The Chess Chronicles tool excluded pawns, so pawn arcs are documented separately in `BOOK1_PAWN_ARCS.md`. Stewards reading these triplets must reference both files.

## Reading Note: Piece-Fate ≠ Character-Fate

**Critical interpretive rule.** Chess terminology uses "kills," "dies," and "captures" interchangeably for the same event: a piece being removed from the board. **In the triplet system these are chess events, not narrative deaths.**

When a character's chess piece is "captured" or "dies," the steward translates that into a narrative event consistent with that character's canon. **The character does not literally die unless canon says so.**

Book 1 canon constraints that override naive chess reading:

| Character | Survives Book 1? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ahdia | Yes | Nearly transcends at climax; pulled back |
| Firas | **Dissolves at climax (~M131-M136 only)** | Displaced into singularity, not conventionally dead. Returns Book 7. |
| Ruth, Ryu, Ben, Tess, Leah, Victor | Yes | All survive |
| Bourn | Yes | Defects in Book 2 |
| Kain | "Destroyed" then clone-survives | Team believes him dead; he isn't |

**Killing constraints on Go Squad characters:**
- **Tess does NOT kill** (canon: brutalizes Webb in Book 2 but leaves alive). The team's MO is incapacitate-not-kill.
- **Victor has NO dead wife** (partner is Leah). Any "lost loved one" reading must come from his community center work (kids lost to gang violence), not a deceased spouse.
- **Ben's wife Sarah** — cause of death UNSPECIFIED (never invent).

**Chess captures that happen "to" a character's piece** = narrative defeats, near-misses, losses of capability, witnessed deaths of others, or moments of confrontation. **Not the character's literal death.**

**Chess captures made "by" a character's piece** = takedowns, captures, incapacitations, confrontations the character wins. **Not necessarily lethal.**

Stewards must check character canon (`7_characters/profiles/`, `2_method_actor/stewards/`, BOOK1 topology and final-state files) before interpreting any piece-fate as character-fate.

## Character Triplets

### ♔ Ahdia Bacchus

| Piece | Reading |
|---|---|
| **♔ White King (e1)** | Her passive identity — the prize the team protects. The secret center. She IS this square; every White piece's job is to defend it. |
| **♘ White Knight (b1)** | Her active self. Active M10–M136 (survives), **51 threats survived** (cellular cost per action), 2 careful captures (surgical, not brutal — she removes obstacles, doesn't kill). **Plays Ng7 at M136 — the final blow, the act the team believes ends Kain (he clone-survives off-board; team doesn't know).** Note: `136. Ng7 1-0` is a *resignation in a lost position*, not on-board checkmate; the canonical kill-shot is Bourn's CADENS missile + the singularity Ahdia burns out. The Knight is the *image* of Ahdia ending it, not the literal mechanism. |
| **♟ White e-pawn** | Her smallest version. Dormant 28 moves, then e3 → e4 → e5 → e6. **Most-pressured pawn in the game (90 ply-states under threat).** Reaches the 6th rank at the climax but **does not promote** — frozen at the edge of transcendence. |

**How they relate:** Three scales of Ahdia. King = what others see. Knight = what she does. Pawn = what she could become. The e-pawn's arrested transformation (reaches e6, never queens) IS the dissolution-into-higher-dimensions stud — she nearly transcends, the team pulls her back.

His arc is the inversion of Ahdia's: she ascends in significance as he diminishes in stature. Both arcs peak at the climax. Neither piece promotes — Firas dissolves *as* he completes the enabling move.
