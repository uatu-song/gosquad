# Chapter 11: Coordinated

---

Ruth settled into position behind the HVAC unit, binoculars steady, watching the crowd gather three stories below. Environmental justice protest. Mining exploitation in the watershed. Victor's contacts had organized this one—families present, permits filed, everything by the book.

"Positions," she said into the comms.

"North corner, clear." Ben's voice, clipped and professional.

"East rooftop with Gloom Girl." Leah. Still getting used to the tactical comms, but steady.

"Ground level with organizers." Victor. "Crowd's about two hundred. Families, students, some union folks. Energy's good."

"West access secured." Tess, somewhere Ruth couldn't see. "Police presence is standard. No riot gear yet."

Ruth scanned the perimeter again. Standard deployment. Clear sightlines. Professional setup. This was what they'd trained for—months of drills paying off in smooth coordination.

Two hundred people chanting about clean water. Signs bobbing in the evening light. A few kids on parents' shoulders. The kind of protest that should end with speeches and dispersal, maybe a mention on the evening news.

Should.

Ruth adjusted her binoculars, tracking the police line. Standard formation. But something in their posture felt off. Shoulders too tense. Hands too close to batons.

"Anyone else feeling this?" she asked.

Pause on comms.

"Yeah." Ben, after a moment. "Can't name it. But yeah."

The crowd below kept chanting. Clean water. Clean air. Clean future.

Ruth watched. Waited. Professional. Prepared.

Something was wrong.

---

"Crowd's amped higher than it should be."

Victor's voice on comms, careful and measured. Not alarmed—observational. Ruth trusted his read on crowd energy more than anyone's.

"Organizers are saying the same thing," he continued. "People showed up angrier than expected. Speeches haven't even started yet."

A pause. Then Victor again, quieter. "Thing is—these people have real reasons to be angry. Mining company poisoned their water table. That's not manufactured. If Eidolon's here, it's not creating anything. It's just turning the volume up on something that was already loud."

Ruth tracked the crowd through her binoculars. He was right. The chanting had an edge to it. Not the rhythmic solidarity of a planned march—something sharper. Reactive.

"Police feel it too." Ben's voice. "Look at the line. They're shifting to containment posture. No reason for that yet."

Ruth panned to the police formation. Ben was right. Subtle repositioning. Hands moving to belts. The body language of people expecting violence.

But nothing had happened. The crowd was loud, not violent. Signs and chanting, not bottles and bricks.

"This doesn't track," Leah said. "I've been to protests. This feels... artificial. Like someone's pushing buttons we can't see."

"Eidolon?" Tess's voice was flat. Not a question—a recognition.

The name landed in Ruth's chest like a stone. They'd seen what Eidolon could do at the Isaiah riot. Fear amplified until people couldn't think straight. Anger magnified until it spilled over into violence.

"Can't confirm," Ruth said. "But assume hostile influence. Everyone reposition for rapid intervention."

No hesitation from the team. Ruth heard the soft sounds of movement through comms—Tess and Leah descending from their rooftop, Ben shifting closer to the police line, Victor moving deeper into the crowd.

"Night Knight, stay near the officers. If this goes sideways, we need someone between them and civilians."

"Copy."

"Crimson Sable, keep eyes on organizers. If anyone can calm this, it's them."

"Already on it."

"Gloom Girl, Battlea—street level. Cover the north exit. If we need to create dispersal corridors, that's your zone."

"Moving now."

Ruth stayed on overwatch. Four teammates positioned around a crowd of two hundred. Good coverage. Professional deployment.

The crowd kept chanting. The police kept tensing. And somewhere invisible, something kept pushing.

Ruth waited for the match to drop.

---

The first bottle came from the east side of the crowd.

Ruth tracked it through her binoculars—arc, trajectory, the way the thrower moved. Not a protester losing control. Deliberate. Trained movement through the crowd.

"Provocateur east side," she said. "Tactical movement. Not organic."

The bottle shattered against a police shield. The crowd flinched. The police line tightened.

Second bottle. Different section. Northwest.

"Another one," Ben confirmed. "Coordinated. Same movement pattern."

The crowd was surging now, fear and anger feeding on each other. People trying to back away but too packed together to move. Children crying. The chanting dissolving into shouting.

"Police calling dispersal," Victor reported. "But there's nowhere to go. Crowd's physically trapped."

Ruth made the call. "Split focus. Night Knight, Battlea—take the agitators. Everyone else, exit routes. Smoke deployment on my mark."

The team moved.

Ruth watched through binoculars as Tess and Victor deployed smoke grenades—calculated positions, dispersal corridors opening through the haze. Professional work. Clean lines.

Ben was tracking the first provocateur through the crowd. Military precision. The target didn't see him coming until Ben's tackle drove him into the pavement. Zip-ties secured in seconds.

Leah flanked the second agitator, cutting off his escape route. He tried to throw another bottle—she caught his arm mid-motion, twisted, disarmed. Down and secured before he could shout.

"Third one," Tess's voice. "I've got visual."

Ruth tracked Tess through the smoke. Acrobatic takedown—the kind of move that looked impossible until you'd trained for it. The third provocateur hit concrete, and Tess had him secured before he could roll.

Three down. Evidence secured. Earpieces, tactical vests, professional gear.

The crowd was dispersing now, flowing through the smoke corridors toward safe exits. Police holding position—no reason to charge when civilians were leaving peacefully.

"Site secured," Ruth said. "Check in. Injuries?"

"Clear." Ben.

"Clear." Leah.

"Clear." Tess.

"Organizers are good," Victor reported. "Families accounted for. Some scared folks but no casualties."

Ruth exhaled. Two hundred people safe. Three provocateurs neutralized. Evidence collected.

This was what competence looked like.

---

The smoke was clearing when Ben's voice came through comms, sharp with new tension.

"Ruth. We have a problem."

She found him in her binoculars—crouched over one of the secured provocateurs, holding something to his ear.

"Their earpiece is still active," Ben said. "I'm hearing coordinated chatter. Multiple teams."

Ruth went cold.

"How many sites?"

"At least three more. University plaza. Community center. Riverside park." Ben's voice was tight. "They're all happening now. Same coordinated provocateur attacks. Same timing."

Victor's voice cut through: "Those are all active protests tonight. My contacts organized two of them."

Ruth processed. Three additional sites. Same tactic. Same timing.

They'd saved two hundred people here.

But hundreds more were at the other sites. Right now. No Go Squad.

"This wasn't isolated," Tess said. The anger in her voice was controlled but present. "This was a coordinated multi-site operation. And we walked into exactly one piece of it."

Ruth looked down at the dispersing crowd. The smoke corridors she'd been proud of seconds ago. The clean operation that suddenly felt like a trap.

"How long until the other sites go critical?" she asked.

Ben listened to the earpiece. "They're already escalating. University's at bottle-throwing phase. Community center has fighting near the police line. Riverside—" He paused. "Riverside sounds bad. Someone mentioned a knife."

Two hundred people here. Safe.

Hundreds there. Burning.

Every second of deliberation was a second someone else got hurt.

"Options," Ruth said, already knowing the answer.

"We split up." Tess, immediate. "Only way to cover three sites."

"That spreads us thin. One person per site, maybe two."

"One person's better than zero."

Ruth closed her eyes. Opened them.

Made the call.

---

"We're splitting up."

Ruth's voice was steady. The decision was made.

"Three sites. University plaza, community center, riverside park. Night Knight—you take riverside. Reports of a knife. You've got the combat experience."

"Copy." Ben was already moving.

"Battlea, Gloom Girl—university. Largest crowd, most variables. Work together."

"On it." Leah and Tess moved toward the north access.

"Crimson Sable—community center. Your contacts organized that one. They'll trust you."

"I'll get there fast." Victor was running before he finished speaking.

"What about you?" Leah asked.

"Overwatch. Coordinating all three sites." Ruth was already climbing to a higher vantage point. "Check in every two minutes. No heroics. If a site is lost, you extract and we regroup."

Silence on comms. The team understood what she wasn't saying.

Some sites might be lost.

"Go," Ruth said.

They went.

Ruth watched her team scatter across Caledonia—four people trying to cover a city-wide coordinated attack. Ben heading east toward riverside. Tess and Leah moving north toward the university. Victor cutting south toward the community center.

She found her overwatch position. High enough to see nothing. Far enough to help no one. Laptop open, city map glowing, three sites marked in red.

The weight of command settled on her shoulders.

Comms crackled. Four voices heading toward four different disasters.

Ruth waited. Coordinated. Alone.

---

The first site to crack was university plaza.

"We've got multiple provocateurs." Leah's voice, breathing hard. "At least four. Crowd's already panicking."

Ruth tracked the update on her map. Red marker pulsing.

"Smoke deployment?"

"Deployed. But the crowd's too big. Corridors aren't forming—people are just running into each other."

Tess's voice cut through: "I've got two targets. Pursuing."

"I'm on a third," Leah reported. "But there's a fourth still active. We can't cover them all."

Ruth heard shouting through Leah's comms. Screaming. The sounds of a crowd losing cohesion.

"Police are charging," Tess said. Flat. Clinical. "They're not waiting for dispersal."

"Can you intercept?"

Pause. Then Tess: "There's a family in the charge path. Kids. I'm diverting."

Ruth understood. Mission compromised to save civilians. The right call. The costly call.

"I've got my target secured," Leah reported. "But the charge hit the crowd. People are down. I'm counting injuries—" Her voice tightened. "Six. At least six injured."

University: partially contained. Six civilian injuries.

The community center crackled next.

"Situation's complicated." Victor, his voice strained. "Provocateur was positioned near the police line. I intercepted, but he's trained. Fight's drawing attention."

"Can you disengage?"

"Not without letting him continue. He was about to throw something at the officers. Ruth, they're watching me now. Police think I'm the problem."

Ruth heard the sounds of struggle. Then Victor's grunt of effort. Then new voices—authoritative, aggressive.

"They're detaining me." Victor's voice was calm but urgent. "I've got the provocateur secured but they're not listening. Ruth, they think I started it."

"Can you explain—"

"They're not interested in explanations. I'm being cuffed."

Community center: Victor detained. Violence continuing. No Go Squad presence.

Ruth's hands were shaking. She steadied them against the laptop.

Riverside came through last.

"This one's bad." Ben's voice was tight. "Smallest crowd but someone's pushing fear hard. Eidolon influence is strong here."

"Provocateur status?"

"Took down one. Clean. But there's a second one and Ruth—" Ben's voice changed. "He's got a knife."

Ruth's blood went cold.

"The crowd's panicking," Ben continued. "Not just fear. Genuine terror. People are trampling each other trying to get away."

"Can you reach the knife?"

"Pursuing. But the crowd—" Sounds of shoving. Screaming. "Someone got between us. Civilian. Confused."

More sounds. Then Ben's voice, sharp: "He slashed someone. The knife provocateur just cut a protester."

"How bad?"

"Bleeding. Bad. Ruth, I—" Ben's voice cracked. "I can chase him or I can help the victim. I can't do both."

"What's your call?"

Silence. Then Ben: "I'm applying pressure to the wound. Knife's getting away. I'm sorry. I couldn't—I can't leave him bleeding."

Riverside: one civilian stabbed. Knife-wielding provocateur escaped. Ben pinned with victim.

Ruth stared at her laptop. Three red markers. Three disasters.

University: six injured, partially contained.
Community center: Victor arrested, violence ongoing.
Riverside: stabbing victim, armed provocateur loose.

Everyone was calling for guidance.

Leah's voice: "Ruth, we've got six injured here. What do we do?"

Victor's voice, muffled through interference: "Still detained. The situation's getting worse. What's the play?"

Ben's voice: "Victim's stabilizing but the knife's still out there. I can't leave him. Ruth, what do I do?"

Three voices. Three disasters. Three impossible choices.

Ruth's hand hovered over her earpiece.

The city was burning in three places and she was standing on a rooftop watching it happen.

She had to choose who to save.

The weight of triage.

---

## Chapter Close

Ruth stood alone on the rooftop.

Below her, Caledonia burned in three different places. The laptop screen glowed with red markers she couldn't reach. The comms crackled with voices she couldn't help.

"Six injured here, Ruth. What do we do?"

"Still detained. Violence getting worse."

"Victim's stable but knife's still out there. I can't leave him."

Everyone waiting for her call.

She had the training for this. Triage protocols. Resource allocation. The mathematics of impossible choices.

But training didn't prepare you for the weight of it. The voices in your ear asking what to do when there was no right answer.

Ruth's hand found her earpiece.

She had to choose.

University had injuries—but Tess and Leah were there, working the problem.

Community center had Victor detained—but he was alive, just constrained.

Riverside had a knife loose in a panicked crowd. Ben was saving one life, but the threat was still active.

Three sites. Three disasters. One of her.

The mathematics were simple. The answer was impossible.

Ruth opened her mouth to speak.

The words didn't come.

Her hand trembled on the earpiece.

The city kept burning.
