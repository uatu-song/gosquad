# Chapter 22: Triage
<!-- SOURCE: chapter_12.txt -->

---

Ruth breathed.

One second of paralysis. The laptop screen glowing with three red markers. The comms crackling with voices asking what to do.

Then clarity.

Triage thinking. The protocols she’d learned as a field medic, before Go Squad, before any of this. You don’t save everyone. You save who you can save.

University: Six injured, but Leah and Tess were there. EMTs would be arriving. Injuries were stable—not life-threatening.

Community center: Victor detained, but detained meant alive. Violence was burning itself out. Contained.

Riverside: Active knife. Bleeding victim. Ben pinned down providing first aid. Armed provocateur loose in a panicking crowd.

The math was ugly but simple.

Ruth keyed her comms.

“Battlea, Gloom Girl—leave university. Get to Riverside. Now.”

Leah’s voice came back immediately: “But we’ve got injured here—”

“I know.” Ruth’s voice didn’t waver. “We can’t save everyone.”

Silence on comms. The weight of that sentence settling across all frequencies.

Then Tess: “Copy. Moving.”

Leah hesitated one beat. Ruth counted it. Then: “Copy.”

Ruth switched channels. “Crimson Sable, hold tight. We’ll extract you after.”

Victor’s voice came through muffled, interference from wherever they were holding him: “Understood. Do what you need to.”

“Night Knight—reinforcements incoming. Hold position.”

Ben, breathing hard, pressure still on the wound: “Copy. Victim’s stable but needs hospital. Knife’s still in play.”

Ruth tracked her screen. Two markers converging on the third. Tess and Leah abandoning the university, running through streets toward Riverside. Behind them, injured people. EMTs arriving. It had to be enough.

She’d made the call.

Now she lived with it.

---

Ben’s arms were burning.

Twenty minutes of continuous pressure on the wound. The victim—a woman in her thirties, protest sign still clutched in one hand—had stopped screaming and started focusing on breathing. Good sign. Meant she wasn’t bleeding out.

“Help’s coming,” Ben told her. “You’re doing great. Keep breathing.”

Around them, chaos. Police riot control pushing the crowd toward exits. People running, falling, helping each other up. Somewhere in that mess, a knife-wielding provocateur was still loose.

Ben scanned constantly but couldn’t leave. The moment he lifted his hands, she started bleeding again.

Then—movement at the edge of his vision. Two figures cutting through the crowd, moving against the flow.

Tess arrived first, dropping to her knees beside him. “I’ve got first aid. Go.”

The handoff was seamless. Tess’s hands replacing his, pressure maintained, not a second of gap. Ben flexed his cramped fingers and stood.

Leah was beside him. “Where’s the knife?”

Ruth’s voice in his ear: “Six o’clock from your position. Moving toward east exit.”

Ben spotted him. Same tactical vest as the others. Moving fast but controlled. Professional.

“Visual confirmed. Pursuing.”

They ran.

The provocateur saw them coming. His head snapped around, tracking two masked figures closing from different angles. He broke into a sprint toward the east exit.

Leah was faster. She’d always been faster. Roller derby training translated into explosive acceleration that Ben couldn’t match. She cut left, angling to intercept his escape route.

Ben pushed harder, flanking from the right. The provocateur was cornered against a chain-link fence, crowd giving him space because of the knife glinting in his hand.

“Careful.” Ruth’s voice. “Non-lethal. We need him talking.”

The provocateur held the blade in a trained grip. Not panicking. Calculating.

Ben and Leah moved without speaking. Practiced coordination. Months of drills paying off.

Ben feinted left. The provocateur tracked him, blade arm extending.

Leah struck from the right. The provocateur tried to slash at her—but she was already moving, body low, roller derby reflexes reading his weight shift before he completed it. She dodged the blade and kicked his weapon hand.

The knife clattered across concrete.

Ben tackled before the provocateur could recover. Down hard. Zip-ties securing wrists in seconds.

“Knife secured.” Leah picked up the blade with gloved hands. Evidence.

Ben checked the provocateur’s gear. Same earpiece. Same tactical vest. Same professional equipment. Hired muscle. Pattern confirmed.

Behind them, Tess’s voice: “Victim stabilized. EMTs just arrived.”

Ruth from overwatch: “Riverside secure. Knife down. Victim getting help.”

Around them, the crowd was calming. Some people had stopped running. A few were watching Go Squad with something that might have been hope.

Ben hauled the provocateur to his feet. “You’re going to tell us who’s paying you.”

The provocateur spat.

Ben didn’t flinch. “That’s fine. Your gear will talk for you.”

---

The community center was quiet when they arrived.

Not peaceful quiet. Aftermath quiet. Broken windows. Scattered debris. A few people still being treated by EMTs. Police securing the perimeter with the weary efficiency of people who’d done this too many times.

Victor was in a holding area near the parking lot. Flex-cuffs on his wrists. Two officers watching him while a third processed the provocateur he’d tackled.

“Anonymous tip time,” Ruth said through comms. “Show them the gear.”

Tess pulled out her phone and dialed. Her voice shifted—concerned citizen, not masked vigilante. “Hi, I was at the community center protest? I saw someone get arrested, but I think there’s been a mistake. The guy in the vest with the earpiece? He was throwing things at police. The other guy—the one they’re holding—he was stopping him.”

She hung up.

They watched from across the street as the tip worked its way through channels. An officer walked over to examine the provocateur’s tactical vest. Pulled out the earpiece. Checked the professional-grade equipment hidden in the pockets.

The officer’s posture changed. He walked over to his colleagues, spoke quietly, gestured at Victor, then at the provocateur.

Five minutes later, Victor was being uncuffed.

“Don’t interfere with police operations,” the officer warned him. Standard warning. Covering themselves.

“I was trying to help,” Victor said.

“Next time, let us handle it.”

Victor bit back whatever he wanted to say. Not the fight for tonight.

He walked across the street to where the team waited in the shadows.

“Everyone okay?” Ruth’s voice in all their ears.

“We’re here,” Tess confirmed. “Victor’s out. Team complete.”

“Then let’s extract. Debrief at base.”

They moved into the night. Four figures in tactical gear, walking through streets that were still processing what had happened.

Behind them, the community center. Broken windows. Scattered debris. People hurt.

Ahead, the university. Similar damage. Six people who’d needed help they couldn’t give.

Riverside. A woman who almost bled out. A knife that almost killed more.

They’d saved some. Lost some.

Nobody spoke on the walk back.

---

The parkour academy’s back room had seen better nights.

Dim lighting. Folding chairs. The team collapsing into seats with the bonelessness of complete exhaustion. Tactical gear still on. Nobody had the energy to change.

Masks off. Just people now.

Ruth should lead the debrief. That was her job. But she sat in silence with everyone else, staring at the floor.

Leah broke first.

“We left injured people at university.” Her voice wasn’t accusatory. Just hollow. Processing. “Just . . . left them.”

Ruth looked up. Met Leah’s eyes. “I know. I made that call.”

“Six people hurt because we weren’t enough.” Tess, quiet.

“Seven.” Ben’s voice was flat. “Riverside stabbing victim makes seven.”

Silence. The weight of casualties settling into the room.

Victor spoke carefully: “But Riverside victim would’ve died if we hadn’t committed there. Ben was the only thing keeping her alive.”

“Knife provocateur would still be loose.” Ben, acknowledging. “Could’ve killed more.”

Leah processing out loud: “So Ruth’s call saved lives, even though it cost at university?”

Ruth shook her head slowly. “I don’t know. Maybe. Both things are true. We saved some and couldn’t save all.”

“Both/and,” Victor said.

“Both/and,” Ruth agreed.

Tess leaned back in her chair, staring at the ceiling. “I hated leaving those people. Physically hated it. But Ruth was right about the knife. Active threat beats stable injuries.”

Victor nodded. “Being detained sucked. But I trusted Ruth to handle it. That’s why we have leadership.”

“I left you there longer than I wanted.” Ruth’s voice cracked slightly. “Prioritized the knife over extracting you.”

“You made the triage call.” Victor’s tone was gentle. “That’s what leadership looks like.”

Ben shifted in his chair. “Military taught me something. No plan survives first contact. The measure isn’t whether your plan worked. It’s how you adapted when it didn’t.”

“We saved the first site completely,” Leah said. “That was perfect. Two hundred people, no injuries, three provocateurs caught.”

“Then we spread thin.” Ben’s jaw tightened. “I heard multiple sites and pushed to cover them all. That’s on me.”

“No.” Ruth’s voice was firm. “I made the call to split. I’m the one who said yes. I take responsibility for that.”

Silence. The team processing Ruth’s accountability.

Victor broke it: “We all chose to go. No one was forced. We knew the risks.”

“And we saved a lot of people.” Tess sat up straight. “Not everyone. But a lot. First site, clean. Riverside, salvaged. Even community center—Victor caught a provocateur before he could throw at the police line. That mattered.”

“Does it feel like it mattered?” Leah asked. Genuine question.

Nobody answered immediately.

---

Ruth straightened in her chair. Leadership mode engaging even through the exhaustion.

“What did we learn? Tactically.”

Ben responded first—always did when evidence was on the table. “Enemy can coordinate multiple simultaneous operations. Four sites tonight. Same tactics, same timing, same professional gear. That’s not random agitators. That’s training. Resources. Command structure.”

“TRIOMF,” Victor said. “Has to be. Professional provocateurs on payroll.”

“Same pattern as the Tank Kain investigation.” Ben nodded. “I’ve been tracking this. Tonight confirms it.”

Tess leaned forward. “But why? What’s the goal of manufacturing riots?”

“Footage.” Ben’s voice was bitter. “Every riot they manufacture becomes campaign material. Kain’s law-and-order narrative. ‘Look how dangerous these protests are. Look why you need strong leadership.’”

“So this is political.” Ruth connected the dots. “Election strategy.”

“Every broken window is a campaign ad,” Ben confirmed.

Leah processed: “We stopped it at one site perfectly. Proved it’s possible.”

“But we can’t be everywhere.” Victor spread his hands. “Need a different strategy. Early warning systems. Alert networks for organizers.”

“Can you build that?” Ruth asked.

“Already have most of it. Community connections. Just need to formalize it.”

Ben pulled out his phone, scrolling through notes. “I’m tracking TRIOMF operations. More intel means better positioning next time.”

Leah hesitated, then spoke: “What about the feeling? At all three sites, everything felt bigger than it should’ve been. Like emotions were amplified.”

“Yes.” Tess sat up straighter. “Anger was too hot. Fear was too strong. Didn’t match the actual threat level.”

Ruth nodded slowly. “I felt it from overwatch. The reports coming in—panic levels didn’t match what I was seeing on cameras. Something was pushing harder than the situation warranted.”

“Eidolon,” Ben said. The name landed heavy. “Same thing from the Isaiah riot. Fear manipulation at scale.”

Victor’s expression darkened. “If something’s artificially amplifying emotions at protests, our job gets exponentially harder. We’re not just fighting provocateurs. We’re fighting the atmosphere.”

“Something to investigate.” Ruth filed it away. “But tonight—we adapted. We survived. We learned.”

“First site crowd cheered when we stopped the provocateurs,” Ben said. “They saw us help. They knew we were on their side.”

“Riverside too,” Leah added. “When the knife went down, people cheered.”

Tess almost smiled. “People know we’re trying. Even when we can’t save everyone.”

---

Ruth stood up. The movement felt heavier than it should have.

“I need to say something.”

The team looked at her. Waiting.

“Those calls tonight. Leaving university. Delaying Victor’s extraction. Committing everything to Riverside before we knew it would work.” She paused. “I hated making them. I’m still not sure they were right.”

“But you made them anyway.” Leah’s voice was steady. “When we were paralyzed, you chose.”

Victor nodded. “Leadership isn’t always making right calls. Sometimes it’s just making calls when everyone else is frozen.”

“Indecision kills.” Ben, matter-of-fact. “You kept us moving.”

Tess met Ruth’s eyes. “I trust you, Ruth. Even when your calls hurt.”

Ruth felt something shift in her chest. She hadn’t expected this. Accountability, yes. Anger, maybe. Not . . . this.

“We’re not superheroes,” Victor said. “Just people making impossible choices with incomplete information.”

Leah stood up, stretching. “Tonight proved we can’t save everyone. That sucks. But we saved who we could. That has to count for something.”

“Seven injuries across four sites is better than dozens dead,” Ben said. “The math is ugly but it’s real.”

“And we got evidence.” Tess held up the knife, still in its evidence bag. “Provocateurs caught. Gear documented. Pattern established.”

Victor added: “My community contacts will hear about this. Police saw the professional equipment. Agent provocateurs are real, and now there’s proof.”

Ruth took a breath. Let it out. “So we learned. We adapted. We saved lives. We got evidence.”

The team nodded. Exhausted but united.

“Then we did our job. Not perfectly. But we did it.”

Beat of silence. Acknowledgment passing through the room without words.

“Go home,” Victor said. “Rest. Process. We earned it.”

The team stood. Masks stayed off. Just people now. Tired people who’d done something impossible and survived it.

Ruth stopped them. “Wait. Everyone . . . thank you. For trusting me tonight.”

Leah paused at the door. “Thank you for leading. Even when it sucked.”

Small gestures. Nods. Ben’s hand briefly on Ruth’s shoulder as he passed. Tess and Leah leaving together, supporting each other through proximity.

Ben left next. He needed to process alone—Ruth recognized the signs.

Victor lingered.

---

## Chapter Close

The back room felt larger with just two of them in it.

Victor leaned against the wall, watching Ruth. She sat in her chair, staring at her hands.

“You made the right calls,” he said.

Ruth looked up. “Did I?”

“You made calls. That’s what mattered.”

She turned her hands over. Examined them like they belonged to someone else. “Seven people hurt tonight. Maybe more we don’t know about.”

“And probably dozens saved. First site alone.”

“Both/and.”

“Both/and.”

Ruth stood slowly. The weight was still there—she could feel it pressing on her shoulders. But it felt shareable now. Distributed across the team instead of crushing her alone.

“Next time they coordinate,” she said, “we’ll be ready.”

Victor nodded. “We will.”

He flipped the light switch on his way out. The back room went dark.

Ruth stood in the darkness for another moment. Then she followed him out.

Night over.
