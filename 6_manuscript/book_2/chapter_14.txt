# Chapter 14: The Escalation

The light came through Ahdia's penthouse windows at an angle that said she'd been up too long. Leah had stopped noticing hours ago—somewhere around 2 AM, when the names in the database started blurring together.

She blinked at her laptop, refocusing. Coffee cold on the nightstand. Guest room that still felt borrowed after months. But the work was hers now.

The encrypted drive Tess had cracked: NDAs, settlement amounts, dates, names. Some full names, some just initials and years. Ages at time of incident.

Some of those ages were—

Leah's stomach turned, but she kept scrolling. That was the work. Looking at the thing that made you want to look away.

*Incident: 2019. Age: 17. Settlement: $340,000. Confidentiality: Permanent.*

The settlement amounts were clinical. Clean numbers for dirty secrets. Three hundred forty thousand dollars to make a teenager disappear from memory. The math of buying silence.

Leah had done her own math: fifteen to twenty women so far. Probably more she hadn't found. The ones who couldn't afford lawyers, who signed whatever was put in front of them, who took the money because what else were they going to do?

She knew that math too. Had almost done it herself.

The red dress heist kept surfacing in her memory like a splinter she couldn't quite grip. Kain's hand on her arm, her waist, sliding lower while she smiled and made him feel clever. The wire recording everything. The shower afterward that didn't help.

*I'm not special,* she thought, scrolling through another page of initials and dates. *I'm just the one who had a wire.*

---

Pattern emerging: incidents clustered around campaign events. Galas, fundraisers, private parties. The same venues appearing again and again. Kain had a type (young, disposable) and a system (access, isolation, leverage).

Her own experience joining a longer list.

Cross-referencing now. Settlement payments routed through shell companies—she'd been mapping these for weeks. Synergy Solutions. Titan Holdings. Names that meant nothing on their own, meant everything together.

Wait.

Leah sat up straighter, reaching for the stack of notes on the coffee table. Family dinner residue: Ben's financial documents, Victor's mining research, her own scattered pages.

TRIOMF. Ben had mentioned TRIOMF. The shell company funding the riots AND the fusion project.

She flipped through settlement records. Synergy Solutions was a subsidiary of...

TRIOMF.

Same company paying hush money to Kain's victims was funding political violence. Same money, different purposes. Or maybe the same purpose, seen from different angles.

She wrote it down, hand shaking slightly:

*TRIOMF → Synergy Solutions → NDA payments*
*TRIOMF → Riot financing*
*TRIOMF → Fusion reactor*

The connection almost there but she couldn't quite see the shape of it. Why would a company need all three? What did sexual assault settlements have to do with energy infrastructure?

Phone buzzing. Victor.

*You okay? Been quiet since dinner.*

She typed back: *Working on something. Can I show you later?*

*Of course. Dinner tonight? We can talk.*

She agreed, returned to her screen. The TV was on mute in the living room—habit now, background noise while she worked. News cycling through: campaign coverage, weather, some story about leadership changes in Southeast Asia.

She ignored it. Another autocrat vanishing wasn't her problem.

---

Another name in the database caught her eye: *Incident occurred at Caledonia fundraiser.*

Same city. Same event circuit. Kain had been doing this here, for years. Local victims, local silence.

The weight of it settling on her chest like a hand.

She screenshotted everything. Backed up to encrypted drive. Then backed up the backup, because she'd learned that lesson the hard way.

This had to matter. She'd make it matter.

She just didn't know when.

---

Across town, Tess woke to empty sheets.

The bed still warm on Leta's side, but empty. Sound from the kitchen: coffee percolating, the clink of a mug against granite.

Tess reached for her phone before she was fully conscious—habit now, the first thing she did every morning. Not texts. Not email. The monitoring app.

Forty-seven new accounts overnight. All created within a three-hour window. All targeting Leta.

She scrolled through, stomach dropping. The same phrases repeated across accounts, the same coordinated talking points:

*Traitor bitch*
*We know where you are*
*Your family can't protect you*

Professional. Not random trolls—network, coordination, intent.

She locked the phone, lay there for a moment staring at the ceiling. Then she got up.

---

Leta was at the counter, coffee in hand, looking at her own phone. Her face was neutral in a way that meant it wasn't.

"Morning, baby." Tess crossed to her, hand on the small of her back.

"Morning." Leta's smile didn't quite land.

Tess noticed the phone face-down on the counter. Noticed Leta's hands not quite steady around the mug.

"Everything okay?"

"Fine. Just..." Leta shrugged. "Same stuff. More of it."

The dismissal that wasn't. Tess had learned to read it.

"You want to talk about it?"

"What's to talk about?" Leta turned to face her fully. "They want me scared. I won't be."

But her hands were shaking slightly as she poured Tess a cup. Tess saw it. Said nothing.

"I could look into it," Tess offered. "My work contacts—"

"Your 'security consulting' work?"

The lie sitting between them. The lie Tess refreshed every day like a password she couldn't change.

"Yeah. We track these networks sometimes."

"It's fine." Leta's voice softened. "Korede's been helping me document. Evidence chain."

Tess's stomach dropped for a different reason. "Your brother? He's seventeen."

"He wanted to help. And he's good at it."

"He shouldn't be exposed to—"

"To what?" Leta's eyes sharpened. "To my life? The threats against me?"

Tess backed off. "That's not what I meant."

"I know what you meant." Softer now. Leta crossed to her, hand on her face. "I'm being careful with him."

Tess wanted to tell her everything. The Go Squad, the powers, the ways she could actually help. The truth that sat in her throat like something she couldn't swallow.

"I just worry about you. Both of you."

"I know." Leta kissed her forehead. "But this is the work."

The work. Always the work.

Tess held her a moment too long. "Text me if anything feels different. Anything at all."

"I will. Promise."

---

Victor's organizing space smelled like old coffee and determination. Flyers from three campaigns stacked on folding tables. Whiteboards covered in strategy. The work made visible.

Leah cleared space, set up her laptop, tried to organize her thoughts. She'd driven here straight from the penthouse, research still burning in her head.

Victor appeared with actual coffee—fresh, which meant he'd noticed she looked like hell.

"Show me," he said.

She walked him through it: NDAs, settlements, Synergy Solutions. The pattern she'd been tracing for weeks finally taking shape.

Victor's face went still as he listened. "This is the same company from Ben's riot investigation?"

"That's what I can't figure out. Why would they fund both?"

"Both/and." His voice was patient, teaching. "Remember what I taught you?"

Leah thought. "They're not separate operations. They're the same machine."

"Keep going."

"Hush money keeps victims quiet. Riots create chaos, distract from... this?"

Victor shook his head slightly. "Or prepare the ground. Make people afraid, then offer a strongman."

"Kain."

Victor nodded. "Fear makes people accept authoritarians."

Leah sat back, overwhelmed. "So the harassment, the riots, the silence—"

"All serving the same goal. Kain's election."

"How do you fight something this big?"

Victor's smile was tired but real. "Same way you eat an elephant. One bite at a time."

"That's a horrible metaphor."

"But accurate."

Leah laughed despite herself. The weight on her chest easing slightly, just from being understood.

"You have audio," Victor said. "You have documentation. That matters."

"But when do I use it?"

"When it can't be ignored. When the timing makes it impossible to bury."

"That feels like waiting while he hurts more people."

Victor's face was serious now. "I know. That's the cost of strategy."

"Both/and again?"

"Always. Wanting to act now AND knowing timing matters."

"I hate this."

"Good. You should."

His phone buzzed. He checked it, expression shifting.

"What?" Leah asked.

"Tess just texted. Leta got another threat."

"Another one? How bad?"

Victor was already standing. "Bad enough that Tess is asking for help."

Leah grabbed her things. "Let's go."

---

Tess opened the door like she'd been waiting on the other side. Her face was controlled—the control that came from long practice, from performing normalcy while everything burned.

"Thanks for coming," she said, stepping back to let them in.

Victor and Leah entered. The apartment was warm, lived-in. Photos on the walls: Tess and Leta at some protest, laughing. A younger Korede making a face at the camera.

Leta was on the couch, phone in her hand, unnaturally still.

"It came through an hour ago," Tess said.

Victor held out his hand. "Show me."

Tess handed over her phone—her phone, not Leta's. She'd been monitoring. Protecting. Lying about it.

The message filled the screen:

*1847 Oakwood Drive, Apt 4B. Nice place. We know where you sleep. Traitor.*

Photo attached: their building. Taken from across the street.

Leah's breath caught. This was different. This was real.

"This isn't random trolling," Victor said quietly.

"They have her address." Tess's voice was tight. "Our address."

Leta finally spoke: "I'm not running."

Everyone looked at her.

"That's what they want," she continued. Voice steady, hands less so. "To make me disappear. Stop organizing. Stop showing up. I won't."

Tess stepped toward her. "Baby, this isn't about running—"

"Isn't it? Hide, stay quiet, stop organizing. That's their goal."

Victor, careful: "She's not wrong."

Tess shot him a look that could cut glass. "Whose side are you on?"

"Both. Yours and hers. That's the problem."

Leah stayed quiet. Watching. Learning.

Leta stood, and Leah saw the resistance practice in how she held herself—the deliberate breath, the centering. "I've been doing this for months. I know how to manage fear. I won't let them win."

"This isn't fear management." Tess's voice cracked. "This is a death threat."

The word landed in the room: *death.*

Silence.

Leta didn't flinch. "Then we document it. Report it. Keep going."

"And if they act on it?"

"Then at least I didn't let them stop me before they did."

Tess's face crumbled for just a moment. Just a crack. Then the control was back, but Leah had seen it.

Victor touched Tess's arm. "Kitchen? Can I talk to you?"

They went. Leah stayed with Leta.

---

In the kitchen, voices low:

"She's right, you know." Victor, calm as always.

"She's going to get herself killed." Tess, barely holding it together.

"Maybe. Or she's going to live exactly as she chooses."

"That's not comforting."

"It's not meant to be." Victor's voice softened. "What are your options?"

"I could tell her. About Go Squad. About what we can actually do—"

"And put a bigger target on her. On all of us. If they know she's connected to powered individuals, what happens then?"

Tess didn't answer.

"So I just watch?" she finally said.

"You protect her the ways you can. And respect her choices."

Silence from the kitchen. Leah pretended not to hear.

---

In the living room, alone with Leta, Leah found herself at a loss.

"I'm sorry," she said. "This is... I'm sorry."

Leta looked at her. Really looked. "You're being harassed too?"

Leah hesitated. Then: "Yeah. Different kind, but yeah."

"I didn't know."

"I don't talk about it much. Working through it."

Something passed between them—recognition. The specific understanding of people who know what it feels like to be hunted.

"Victor's been helping me," Leah continued. "Teaching me to see the system."

"The both/and stuff?"

"Yeah. It's helped. With my own stuff."

Leta nodded slowly. "It's good. Hard, but good."

"You invented resistance practice?"

"Not invented. Adapted. From therapy, mostly. Somatic stuff. You can't think your way out of fear, but you can choose what you do with it after."

"Does it work?"

Leta's smile was tired. "Sixty percent of the time, it works every time."

Leah laughed, surprised. Leta laughed too.

Something settling between them. The beginnings of solidarity.

---

Victor and Tess returned. Tess's eyes red but her face composed.

"So," Victor said. "What's the plan?"

"I keep organizing." Leta's voice was firm. "We document everything. We don't hide."

Tess: "And security?"

"I'm not getting a bodyguard." Before Tess could even suggest it.

Victor: "No. But we can create support networks. Check-ins. Buddy system. Never alone at protests."

Leah surprised herself: "I can help with documentation. I've been learning."

Leta looked at her with something like respect. "Thank you."

Tess, quietly: "And if things escalate further?"

"Then we adapt. But we don't stop."

Tess wanted to argue. Leah could see it. But she just nodded.

"The gala is in a few days," Victor said, shifting the conversation. "Big police event."

Tess's expression changed. "My father's gala. He wants me there."

"Could be an intelligence opportunity."

Tess's mind was already spinning—Leah could see the calculation. "I'll go. See what I can find out."

Leta frowned. "Your dad's gala? You sure?"

"Someone needs to know what they're planning."

The lie embedded in truth. Tess did need intel. About Isaiah Bennett, about her father's complicity, about the system that protected killers and targeted activists.

"Be careful," Leta said. "Those people—"

"I know who they are." Tess's voice was cold now. "I grew up with them."

Victor: "We should coordinate. Team meeting before the gala."

Agreement all around.

The group was smaller than Go Squad. But this was solidarity too.

---

Night fell across Caledonia. Leah drove back to the penthouse alone, Victor staying to help Leta and Tess with security protocols.

In the guest room, she opened her laptop again. All her research spread across the screen. Kain's face on muted news—campaign ad, smiling.

The same smile from the red dress heist.

She had the recording. She had the evidence. She just had to find the right moment.

*When it can't be ignored.* Victor's voice in her head.

She backed up everything again. Triple redundancy. Then she stared at the ceiling and didn't sleep.

---

Across town, in the apartment with the death threat and the home address, Leta practiced breathing.

Centering. Choosing.

Fear is a weapon. Don't let them load it.

But her hands were still shaking.

Tess was asleep beside her—finally, after hours of watching, of trying to stay awake. Exhaustion had won.

Leta didn't tell her about the shaking. Some fears you carry alone.

She looked at her phone one more time. The message still there. The photo of their building. The address they'd found.

They knew where she slept.

She put the phone away and closed her eyes. Tomorrow she'd organize. Tomorrow she'd keep going. Tomorrow she'd be exactly who she'd chosen to be.

Tonight she just lay in the dark and tried to believe her own resistance practice.

Sixty percent of the time, it worked every time.

Tonight wasn't one of those times.

---

In the same bed, pretending to sleep, Tess listened.

Leta's breathing—irregular, scared, trying to steady itself. The small sounds of someone practicing courage in the dark.

Tess calculated. What could she do from the gala? What intel might protect Leta? What might expose the network that was hunting her?

Her father's gala. Her father's people. The men who killed Isaiah Bennett.

She'd walk among them, smile, and remember everything.

And maybe—maybe—she'd find something that could stop what was coming.

The clock on the nightstand ticked toward morning.

They were running out of time.
