# Chapter 19: Exile and Exhaustion

---

CNN Breaking: Colombian cartel leader Miguel Santos missing from fortified compound. No sign of forced entry. Third cartel chief this month.

BBC World Service: Russian oligarch Dmitri Volkov vanishes mid-flight. Private jet lands at Heathrow on autopilot. Cabin empty. Champagne still cold.

Al Jazeera: Syrian secret police commander Tariq al-Najjar disappears from secure military installation. Guards report nothing. Cameras show nothing. He was there, then he wasn't.

Fox News: "—what some are calling the most baffling series of disappearances in modern history—"

MSNBC: "—the pattern is undeniable. Seventeen senior officials across three continents, every single one with documented human rights violations—"

#TheVanishings trending worldwide. 4.2 million posts.

@truthseeker2041: "Notice how it's ONLY war criminals and traffickers disappearing? Not a single innocent person. Whatever this is, it has a list."

@conspiracydaily: "Name one who didn't deserve it. We'll wait."

Reddit, r/worldnews, gilded 47 times: "Comprehensive list of all confirmed vanishings. 100% correlation with documented atrocities. Zero correlation with anything else. Whatever methodology is being used has perfect targeting."

Pentagon briefing, leaked transcript: "We cannot identify the method of extraction. No signatures consistent with known special operations. No state actor has claimed responsibility. Analysts are describing the operational capability as, quote, 'impossible.'"

CIA internal memo: "Perpetrator unknown. Method unknown. Motive consistent: removal of high-value human rights violators from operational theaters. Recommend monitoring, not intervention."

UN Security Council emergency session, day three: Unable to act. Delegates privately acknowledge the targets are indefensible. The Vatican condemns vigilantism and acknowledges the results in the same paragraph.

Amnesty International: "Justice requires transparency and accountability, not disappearance."

@sleepingbetter_now: "I don't care who's doing it. Global trafficking incidents down 34% in six weeks. Thirty-four percent. Sleep on that."

Conflict zones quieter. Key commanders missing. Power vacuums forming in three regions. Some destabilizing. Most not.

Undeniable fact, buried in a UN statistical report nobody reads: fewer people are dying from state-sponsored violence than at any point in the last forty years.

Theories: AI. Aliens. Secret government program. Rogue powered individual. Divine intervention. Time travel.

No one suspects a twenty-four-year-old in Caledonia.

---

Eleven forty-seven PM. Friday.

The shimmer lasted two seconds. Ahdia materialized in the penthouse living room and caught herself on the back of the couch. Missed the couch. Hit the floor.

She stayed there. Breathing. Pulled one arm free of the tactical jacket and left the rest.

She got up. Made it to the bedroom. Face-first onto the bed, boots still on, jacket half-off and pinned under her shoulder. The remote was on the nightstand. She found it without looking.

The screen lit up. Overdramatic music. Aerial drone footage.

She was asleep before the opening montage finished.

---

Ruth parked in the garage at quarter past midnight. She sat in the car for two minutes, hands on the wheel, running the calculation she'd been running since Thursday. Ahdia's baseline after treatment: 56.2 percent. Number of days since treatment: six. Expected decline rate without Seed activation: 0.3 percent per day. Expected decline rate with Seed activation: unknown, because Ahdia lied about frequency and Ryu reported what Ahdia told him.

She took the elevator up. Had the access code from the second week—Ahdia's birthday, which Ahdia had set because she couldn't remember anything more complicated and couldn't be bothered to change it.

The elevator opened on the penthouse floor. Ryu was standing in the hallway.

Ruth's heart slammed sideways. She grabbed the elevator frame.

Ryu dropped his equipment case. It hit the floor with a crack that echoed off the marble.

Two seconds of silence. Both catching their breath.

"Jesus," Ruth said.

"Sorry—I'm sorry, I was just—I didn't expect—" Ryu was picking up the case, hands shaking, the contents rattling. Diagnostic kit. Treatment supplies. The nervous energy of a man who'd driven across the city at midnight because the remote monitoring numbers looked wrong.

"You're here for her," Ruth said.

"Remote readings spiked at eleven-fifty. Three distinct Seed activation signatures in the last eighteen hours." He straightened up. The case was dented. "She's been out."

"How many?"

"Three. At minimum."

Ruth closed her eyes. Six days since she'd sat in that penthouse and drawn the line. Track 4. Seventy-two hours. Tell me the truth.

"She's not answering calls," Ryu said. "I tried four times."

"She's probably asleep."

"Or worse."

Ruth keyed the access code. The door unlocked.

---

The living room lights were on. Tactical gear on the floor by the couch—jacket, boots kicked half under the coffee table, a utility belt with equipment Ruth didn't recognize. The TV in the living room was off. A glass of water on the counter, untouched.

Ruth looked at the gear. Ryu looked at the gear.

"She went out tonight," Ruth said.

"The third signature was eleven forty-seven."

"Thirteen minutes ago."

Ryu set his case on the counter. Opened it. Pulled the portable baseline scanner. His hands were doing their thing—efficient, practiced, tremoring at the edges.

"She might need immediate treatment," he said. "If she did three in eighteen hours at this baseline—"

"Let's find her first."

They walked toward the bedroom. Voices low. Not whispering—the register of people who didn't want to wake someone but needed to know she was alive.

TV sounds from behind the closed door. Music. Something with production value—sweeping aerial shots, a dramatic voice-over.

"She's watching something," Ruth said.

Ryu tilted his head. "Is that... reality TV?"

"At midnight?"

"It's Ahdia."

Fair point.

They were three steps from the bedroom door when Ryu stopped.

Not slowed down. Stopped. Mid-step, left foot still raised, body locked in place like someone had pressed pause on him. His face went blank. Eyes wide. Mouth slightly open. The scanner in his right hand started to slip and he didn't catch it.

Ruth caught it. "Ryu?"

Nothing.

"Ryu." She put her hand on his arm. His pulse was rapid. Pupils dilated. He was breathing, conscious, upright—but somewhere else.

The brain receiver. She'd read about it in his file—FAERIS-integrated neural interface, allowed direct data downloads as internal sensation. Ryu had been using it for years to receive diagnostic telemetry. It auto-synced whenever he was in range of Ahdia's HUD. Normal. Routine.

Whatever was coming through right now was not routine.

"Ryu, talk to me. What are you getting?"

His mouth moved. No sound. Then: "AR-Ryu's syncing. It's—" He swallowed. "Four months. She's had it running for four months. Twenty-eight—" His eyes were tracking something she couldn't see. Processing at the speed the data was arriving. "Twenty-eight exiles. Forty-seven episodes. It's all coming in at once."

"What is?"

"Everything AR-Ryu's been doing. My system. She gave it a job and it's been running it for four months and it's uploading all of it right now." He made a sound that was halfway between a laugh and a choke. "Forty-seven episodes of reality television. Without me."

Ruth stared at him.

Ryu stared at nothing.

Three seconds of this.

Then Ruth's training kicked in—not the therapy training, the medical training—and she was past Ryu and through the bedroom door before the thought fully formed.

---

Ahdia was face-down on the bed. Fully clothed. One arm free of the tactical jacket, the other still tangled in the sleeve. Her breathing was slow and even. Asleep. Not unconscious—asleep. The specific respiratory pattern of deep exhaustion, not medical distress.

Ruth's hands were already moving. Pulse at the wrist. Skin temperature. Checked the eyes—responsive, no hemorrhaging. The portable scanner was still in her other hand from catching it when Ryu froze. She ran it. Baseline reading: 53.1 percent. Down three points in six days. Consistent with three Seed activations. Survivable.

She sat back on her heels.

Ahdia was fine. Depleted, dehydrated, running on cellular fumes. But fine.

The TV was playing.

Ruth looked up.

The screen showed a volcanic island. Lush, green, surrounded by water that was too blue to be real except it obviously was. FAERIS drones—she recognized the flight pattern from CADENS documentation—circled overhead in professional formation. Cinematic angles. The footage quality was broadcast-grade.

A man was sitting on a rock near the shoreline. Late fifties. Military bearing gone soft. He was holding a fishing rod made from a branch and what looked like paracord. His face filled the frame in a testimonial close-up.

"—I ordered the execution of fourteen journalists," the man said. His voice was flat. Not rehearsed—emptied. "Between 2019 and 2022. Fourteen. I told myself it was national security. I told myself they were agitators."

Cut to: the same man arguing with another man—younger, heavier, tattoos visible on both forearms—about a fishing spot.

"That's MY side of the reef—"

"Your side? You've been here three weeks, I've been here since Episode 12—"

The music swelled. Freeze-frame on both men pointing at each other. Text overlay: DAY 47 — EXILE ISLAND.

Lower corner: AR-Ryu Production.

Ruth watched. Couldn't stop watching. A third man appeared in the background—slight, nervous, definitely not military—trying to mediate the fishing dispute. A FAERIS drone swooped past at head level and all three ducked. Cut to an aerial wide shot: twenty-some figures scattered across the island. Shelters built from debris. A communal fire pit. What appeared to be a rudimentary volleyball court.

A title card: 28 SUBJECTS — 47 EPISODES — 0 ESCAPES.

"Ruth."

Ryu was in the doorway. Still pale. His hands had stopped shaking, which was somehow worse—Ryu's hands always shook.

"It finished syncing," he said. "I have everything. Four months of operational data."

"Tell me."

"Twenty-eight subjects. Dictators, cartel leaders, war criminals, traffickers. She freezes time around the target, extracts them, and drops them on an uncharted volcanic island in the South Pacific. FAERIS drones maintain surveillance and film continuously. She gave AR-Ryu the footage—" He paused. "My system's been processing everything into episodic reality television. Weekly testimonials. Confession format. Four months of output I never authorized and never saw."

Ruth looked at the screen. A cartel boss was crying. Genuine tears. Talking about a village he'd ordered burned.

"She watches it," Ruth said.

"She reviews it. Targeting verification. She checks that every subject on that island belongs there. Cross-references their testimonials against documentation she has from—I don't know, intelligence databases, Bourn's files, whatever she's been accessing."

"So it's not entertainment."

"It's accountability." He said it carefully. Like he was testing whether the word could hold the weight. "She's built a system that removes the worst people on earth from power, contains them on an island where they can't hurt anyone, and forces them to confront what they've done. On camera. In the format she knows best."

They both looked at the screen. The fishing argument had escalated. A former warlord was now involved, claiming territorial rights over the entire northern reef because he'd been there since Episode 3. AR-Ryu's editing had added dramatic zoom effects and a bass drop.

"It's a reality show," Ruth said.

"It's a reality show."

"She put twenty-eight war criminals on an island and made a reality show."

"With my own system running it. Without telling me."

They looked at each other.

Ryu's mouth did something complicated. Ruth felt her own face doing the same thing.

"The fishing argument," Ruth said.

"I saw."

"They're arguing about a fishing spot."

"A man who executed fourteen journalists is arguing about reef territory with a cartel enforcer who burned a village."

Ruth sat on the edge of the bed. Ahdia didn't stir. The exhaustion of three Seed activations in eighteen hours—she could sleep through a building collapse.

"Trafficking down thirty-four percent," Ruth said.

"I saw the numbers."

"Six weeks. Thirty-four percent."

"Yes."

"State-sponsored violence at a forty-year low."

"Also yes."

On screen, the nervous mediator had brokered a truce involving a shared fishing schedule written in the sand. AR-Ryu freeze-framed on the schedule, added a graphic overlay comparing it to the Geneva Conventions, and played a laugh track that was clearly modeled on mid-2000s sitcoms.

Ruth put her face in her hands. She was laughing. She didn't want to be laughing. A cartel enforcer was negotiating reef access with a war criminal on a volcanic island filmed by AI drones and edited by the system Ryu built and didn't know was running, and the woman responsible for all of it was unconscious three feet away with a cellular baseline of 53 percent, and global trafficking was down a third, and none of this should be funny and all of it was.

"She used your system," Ruth said through her hands.

"She gave AR-Ryu the footage and let it run." He leaned against the doorframe. "Everything it does is mine—the dramatic zooms, the freeze-frames, the editorial pacing. Except the bass drops. I've never used bass drops."

"Ahdia added those."

"Ahdia's been directing my system for forty-seven episodes. Adjusting the output. Teaching it things I never taught it."

"From a technical standpoint."

"From every other standpoint, I don't know what it is."

Ruth straightened up. Looked at Ahdia. The rise and fall of her breathing. The tactical jacket still half-on. The remote loose in her fingers.

"She's been doing this for months," Ruth said. "The news seeds—the dictators vanishing, the cartel leaders, the generals. We talked about it in the meeting. Ben said it was background noise. I thought Patterson might be behind it. It was her. It was always her."

"Four months. Since she started working with Bourn."

"Bourn knows?"

"Bourn provides intelligence on who Patterson wants silenced—witnesses, analysts, operatives. Ahdia's mandate was relocation. Protection. Instead she—" He gestured at the screen. "She ran the protection operations AND independently identified and removed the threats those people needed protection from. Both operations simultaneously. Track 4 and whatever this is."

"This isn't Track 4."

"No. This is whatever Ahdia does when you give her a constraint and she agrees to it and then immediately builds something larger around it."

Ruth looked at the screen. Episode 47 had moved on from the fishing dispute. A testimonial: a former trafficking coordinator, mid-forties, describing the logistics of moving people across borders. His voice cracked on the word *children*. The FAERIS drone pulled in close. AR-Ryu held the shot. No dramatic music. No freeze-frame. Just a man sitting on a rock on an island he couldn't leave, saying out loud what he'd done.

Ruth turned it off.

---

They sat in the living room. Left Ahdia sleeping. Ryu made coffee because his hands needed something to do and because Ruth hadn't eaten.

"Fifty-three percent," Ruth said.

"Down from fifty-six point two after treatment."

"Six days."

"Three Seed activations will do that. Each one costs roughly one to one-point-five percent at this baseline—more if she's holding a freeze long enough to extract someone from a fortified compound. The math—" He stopped. Recalculated. "The math says she has capacity for maybe eight more activations at that intensity before she hits the critical threshold. Below forty-five percent, the cellular degradation becomes—"

"I know what it becomes."

Ryu set the coffee down. Sat across from her. The penthouse was quiet. Ahdia's breathing audible from the bedroom if they listened for it.

"What do we do with this?" Ruth said.

"I don't know."

"She's saving people. The numbers are real. Thirty-four percent reduction in trafficking. That's thousands of lives."

"At the cost of her own."

"Which she's decided is an acceptable trade."

"Which she's always decided is an acceptable trade. Since I started—since the beginning." He caught himself. Rubbed his face. "She processes sacrifice as function."

"The reality show part."

"The reality show part is—" He exhaled. "It's her. It's entirely her. She grew up on television. She processes the world through television. Of course she'd take my system and point it at an island full of war criminals and let it run without telling the person who—" Another stop. Another redirect. "Without telling the person who built it."

Ruth watched him not finish the sentence. Filed it where she filed all the things Ryu almost said about Ahdia.

"It's working," Ruth said.

"It's working."

"And it's killing her."

"And it's killing her."

"Both things."

"Both things."

On the counter, the portable scanner displayed Ahdia's numbers. 53.1 percent.

"We confront her Sunday," Ruth said. "At treatment. When she's sitting still. When Ryu has the scanner running and I have the baseline in front of her and she can't deflect."

"She'll deflect."

"She'll try."

"She took my own system and pointed it at a volcanic island full of war criminals without telling me. Deflection is her art form."

"Sunday," Ruth said.

Ryu nodded. Picked up his coffee. Set it down without drinking.

"The fishing argument," he said.

"What about it?"

"The man who executed fourteen journalists was arguing about reef territory. With a cartel enforcer. On an island they can't leave. Filmed by drones. Edited by my own system." He looked at his hands. Still not shaking. "It was funny."

"It was."

"Is it allowed to be funny?"

"I think it has to be," Ruth said.

Ryu picked up the coffee again. This time he drank it.

They sat with it. Two people in a penthouse at one in the morning, the woman they were both trying to save asleep in the next room, her impossible project playing to an empty screen, and the world outside measurably safer because of something that shouldn't exist and couldn't be defended and was working.

Sunday. They'd deal with it Sunday.

Ruth washed the cups. Ryu packed his equipment. Neither of them checked on Ahdia again—she was asleep, her vitals were stable, and the truth they'd discovered could wait forty hours for a conversation none of them were ready to have.

They left together. Took the elevator down. Parked next to each other in the garage, which was either coincidence or the universe's idea of a joke.

"Ryu."

"Yeah."

"The bass drops."

He almost smiled. "I don't use bass drops."

"AR-Ryu does."

"AR-Ryu has questionable taste."

"AR-Ryu has Ahdia's taste."

He got in his car. Ruth got in hers. The garage was quiet. The city was asleep. Twenty-eight people on an island in the South Pacific were not.
