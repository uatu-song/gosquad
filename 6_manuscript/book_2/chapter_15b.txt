# Chapter 15B: Solo Operation

---

The ballroom was the same room. Different gravity.

Without Korede, the social mechanics simplified. No one to shield. No hand to squeeze. Just Tess Whitford, Chief's daughter, making the rounds at daddy's event. The role she'd been rehearsing since the first time he brought her here at twelve, white dress, patent leather shoes, learning to shake hands with men whose names she'd forget and whose faces she wouldn't.

She accepted a champagne flute from a passing server. Didn't drink. Prop.

"Tess! Sorry about your boyfriend." An officer she half-recognized from holiday parties.

"Weak stomach." She smiled. "He'll survive."

"Good kid. Seems like one of the—"

"He is," she said, and moved on before the sentence finished.

Her father materialized at her shoulder. Arm around her, possessive and warm. "Proud of you, sweetheart. Staying to support me."

"Of course, Dad. It's your night."

His hand squeezed her shoulder. She catalogued the weight of it—the specific pressure of a man who believed his daughter loved him without complication. He had no idea who she was. He'd never had any idea.

She circulated.

---

Patterson found her at the bar.

Not an accident. He'd been tracking her movement since she'd walked back in alone—she'd felt the attention like a change in air pressure, the particular weight of being watched by someone who watched for a living.

"Miss Whitford. Your young man didn't last long."

"Oysters." She kept her face easy. "Bad luck."

"Mm." He signaled the bartender. Bourbon. Neat. He didn't look at her while he ordered. "You know, your father is an admirable man. Loyal. Protective. The kind of officer who understands that some situations require... discretion."

She waited. The bartender set the glass down. Patterson turned the crystal slowly.

"Discretion is a vanishing skill," he continued. "Everyone wants to talk. Journalists, bloggers, people with cameras in their phones. Everyone wants to be the one who uncovers something." He took a sip. "The smart ones understand that some things stay covered for good reason."

He looked at her then. Direct. No pretense.

"The Bennett matter is settled, Miss Whitford. The investigation concluded. The officers were cleared. Anyone revisiting that conclusion now—" He paused. "Well. They'd be working against the interests of people who take institutional stability very seriously."

The marble floor was cold through her shoes. The string quartet was playing something by Dvořák. Three hundred people in formal wear behind her, and the Secretary of Defense was telling her to stop.

"I understand, sir."

"I hope you do." He set the glass down. "Your father's career depends on cooperative relationships. So does his daughter's future. I'd hate for anything to disrupt that."

He left the glass half-finished on the bar and walked away.

Tess picked up her champagne. Her hand was steady. She took a sip she didn't taste and moved toward the buffet table.

---

The intel came in pieces.

She didn't hunt for it. She positioned herself near conversations and let the room do the work. Chief's daughter refreshing a plate. Chief's daughter finding a seat near the stage. Chief's daughter moving through a party where everyone knew her name and no one saw her clearly.

Two senior officers near the carving station, ties loosened, third drinks in hand.

"Thank God for the federal assist. Local forensics would've been a disaster."

"SecDef came through. Owed Whitford a favor from the golf thing."

"CADENS has tech we can't touch. Enhanced imaging, digital reconstruction—"

"All public record." The other officer's voice dropped. "Investigation cleared our guys. That's the story."

Tess moved on with a plate she wouldn't eat from.

At the buffet, back turned, she caught a conversation three feet behind her. Officers who didn't know she was Chief's daughter—she'd been introduced to the room with Korede, and without him she was just another woman in a dress.

"Still can't believe they turned it around in forty-eight hours."

"That's CADENS for you. Brought in their team, re-examined everything."

"Re-examined? They rewrote the whole thing."

Silence. Then, quieter: "Corrected inconsistencies. That's different."

"Either way—bodycam footage suddenly showed justified force."

"I heard the original analyst quit. Refused to sign."

"Who told you that?"

"Doesn't matter. CADENS analyst signed instead. Case closed."

Tess set the plate down and walked to the bar. Her stomach was doing something she'd need to process later. Not now.

Near the bar. Civilian donors, not officers. Different vocabulary, same architecture.

"Chief handled that crisis beautifully. Barely a ripple in the polls."

"CADENS consultation isn't cheap. Where'd the funding come from?"

"Defense budget. SecDef authorized personally."

"Unusual. CADENS bills through Homeland Security."

"Special circumstances. Wanted it quiet. Buried in classified line items—who audits Defense?"

Tess thought: *Ben could find this.*

Three pieces. CADENS provided falsified forensics. The original analyst refused to sign. SecDef paid from Defense budget, bypassing normal channels.

One piece left.

---

She found her father in the alcove near the stage doors, half-hidden by a curtain, talking to Patterson. Their posture said private conversation. Their voices said they didn't expect anyone to hear.

She approached at an angle that let them see her coming. Nonthreatening. Daughter looking for her dad.

"Tess! Come here, sweetheart." Her father extended his arm. She stepped into the space beside him. Patterson's eyes moved over her face and found nothing.

"SecDef was saying what a lovely young woman you've become."

"Thank you, sir. Dad's always made sure I had opportunities."

Patterson's smile didn't move. "Your father is very protective of those he cares about."

"Family first," her father said. "Always."

"That's why our arrangement worked so well. We both understand protecting what matters."

Her father's eyes flicked to Patterson. Warning.

"Arrangement, sir?" Tess asked. Open face. Innocent curiosity.

"Just inter-agency cooperation, honey." Her father's hand tightened on her shoulder.

Patterson ignored the warning. "Your father needed help managing a public perception problem. I needed CADENS Director Bourn to understand the value of cooperative relationships."

*Bourn.* The name landed in her chest and stayed there. CADENS Director. The person who ordered it.

Her father, uncomfortable now: "We should discuss this another—"

"Why? Miss Whitford seems sharp." Patterson was watching Tess the way he'd watched her at the bar. Testing. "She understands how the world works."

"Sometimes doing the right thing means protecting institutions," Patterson said. To Tess directly now. Not performing. Teaching.

"Protecting the department's reputation," Tess said.

"Exactly. One unfortunate incident shouldn't destroy public trust."

Her father: "The officers acted in good faith. Situation was ambiguous—"

"Ambiguous situations require clear narratives." Patterson's voice was smooth, certain. "That's what CADENS provided."

They knew. Both of them, standing in this alcove, knew it wasn't justified force. They'd built a machine to say it was, and the machine worked, and they were proud of it.

"Director Bourn understood the bigger picture," her father said. "Political stability requires—"

"People like us making hard choices." Patterson finished the sentence for him.

He checked his watch. "I should make remarks. Chief, shall we?"

Before turning, Patterson looked at Tess one more time. "Bourn's cooperation didn't come free, of course. Budget appropriations. Facility access. The usual currency."

Quid pro quo. CADENS covered up murder for funding.

They walked toward the stage. Tess stood in the alcove alone.

Four pieces. Who. What. How. Why.

She had everything she'd come for. The CADENS plans, the institutional arrangements, the forward-looking conversation about how these men intended to use a federal agency as their personal cleanup crew. And underneath all of it, the thing she hadn't come for at all: proof that her father had coordinated the cover-up of a teenager's murder and called it proper procedure.

She needed to leave.

---

Her father was near the stage when she reached him. Adjusting his notes. The speech was next—gratitude, service, the usual language that meant nothing and meant everything.

"Dad?"

He looked up. Smiled. "Sweetheart. Everything okay?"

"Just wanted to say goodbye. Not feeling great."

"Of course. Come here."

He opened his arms.

Tess stepped into the embrace. She was ready this time—had built the wall between her face and her feelings in the thirty seconds it took to cross the room. But his arms closed around her and he smelled like the same aftershave he'd worn when she was ten, the same cologne from every birthday morning, every school drop-off, every night he tucked her in before driving to a precinct where he made problems disappear. The wall cracked.

One second. Her face showed it. Not disgust this time—something worse. Grief for the version of him she'd needed to be real.

He felt her stiffen. Pulled back. Caught the tail end of it.

"Tess." His voice changed. "Did I do something wrong?"

The question sat between them, enormous.

Patterson's attention from across the room. She could feel it.

"You looked at me like—" Her father searched her face. "What's going on?"

"Dad. Can we talk? Not here."

He went into protection mode immediately. Hand on her back, guiding her off the stage, through the service corridor, into his borrowed office. Door closed. Quiet.

"What happened? Did someone say something to your boyfriend?"

"Korede." She used the name deliberately. "Your officers—one of them said 'play stupid games, win stupid prizes' about Isaiah Bennett. Right in front of him. And the table laughed."

Her father's expression darkened. "Who?"

"That's not the point."

"Give me a name. I'll handle it."

"You'll *handle* it." The words came out sharper than she'd planned. "The way you handle everything? Quietly? So nobody has to feel uncomfortable?"

He went still. "What does that mean?"

"It means I brought someone I care about to your event, and your colleagues treated him like entertainment."

"I can't control what every officer—"

"You're the *Chief*. You control everything."

Too much. She'd pushed too far. His eyes narrowed—not the warm father now. The cop.

"This is about more than your boyfriend."

"I'm upset. About Korede. That's all."

"You looked at me with *disgust*, Tess."

"Because I'm *embarrassed*." Real tears now—real, but redirected. "I brought him into a room full of people who see him as—"

"As what? Careful."

The knife's edge. One more word of truth and the architecture of this conversation shifted from father-daughter to interrogation.

"People who don't see him as equal."

Her father's face moved through something she couldn't read. Anger, maybe. Or recognition. Or the particular discomfort of a man who'd spent his career building systems that did exactly what his daughter was describing and had never once been forced to watch them work on someone he could see.

"I'm sorry," he said. Quieter now. "I should have prepared you."

"You shouldn't have to."

He opened his arms again. She stepped in. Expected it this time. Controlled.

"I love you," he said, close to her ear. "You know that, right?"

"I know, Dad."

She held him for exactly three seconds. Then she pulled away, and she walked out of his office, and she walked through the ballroom where the string quartet was playing and the officers were laughing and a news ticker on the muted TV above the bar read *MASS DISAPPEARANCE OF MILITARY JUNTA LEADERSHIP — 8 GENERALS MISSING* and nobody looked at it twice.

She walked out the front entrance and into the cold.

---

Korede was across the street. Engine off. Lights off. Sitting in the dark the way she'd left him.

She got in. Closed the door. The car was quiet.

"What happened?" he said. "You were in there forever."

"Not here. Drive."

He drove. Three blocks in silence before she spoke.

"I almost blew it. My face—when I hugged him. He saw."

"What did you tell him?"

"The truth. About the racism. About how they treated you."

"And that worked?"

"He believed what he wanted to believe."

More silence. Street lights sliding across the windshield.

"What did you learn?"

"CADENS Director Bourn ordered the forensics altered. SecDef paid for it from classified Defense budget. My father coordinated locally. Original analyst refused to sign the revised report—CADENS replaced him with their own."

Korede processed. His hands on the steering wheel at ten and two. Seventeen years old, driving carefully because his sister's girlfriend had just dismantled the world in four sentences.

"They know it was murder."

"They know. They said it to my face and didn't care."

Quiet.

"What do we do?"

"I need to tell people who can use this."

"What people?"

"People I work with."

"Can I meet them?"

"No."

"You don't trust me?"

"I'm protecting you."

He pulled up to Leta's building. Idled. Neither of them moved.

"Will you tell Leta I helped?"

"I'll tell her you were brave."

"Tess."

"Yeah."

"Be careful. These people killed someone and covered it up. They'll do it again."

"I know."

He got out. Paused at the car door. "The officer's name."

"I didn't forget."

He nodded once. Walked inside. She waited until the light went on in the third-floor window, then pulled out her phone.

Text to Ruth: *Need to meet. Tonight. Bring everyone.*

She sat in the car for a moment. The dashboard clock read 11:47. Her hands had started shaking sometime in the last three blocks. She let them.

Then she drove.

*End of Chapter 15B*
