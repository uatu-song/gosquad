**POV: HARDING KAIN.** This is the villain's chapter and the register changes
with it (§7g: register follows POV). No jokes, no warmth, no pop culture.
Kain prices things. He is a man who has always thought of himself as an asset
he happens to be wearing, and the correct voice here is **cold assessment
that never raises itself.** He is not gloating. He is watching an investment
mature.

**THE CHAPTER'S REAL ASSIGNMENT — the trap.**

1. **The team's greatest victory is the trap closing.** They fight beautifully,
   in coordination, and every blow lands. Then the Tamois light comes out of
   the four bodies and goes home to Kain, and he grows. **He distributed the
   fragments to the Tanks on purpose. They were bait.** The reader should be
   allowed to cheer the fight and then have the cheer curdle.
2. **The team believes they killed four police officers.** Mechanically Kain
   reclaimed the fragments and left husks; morally they will never accept that
   distinction. Span 5 — "We killed them." — is unattributed on purpose and
   **must stay unattributed:** Kain cannot tell which of them said it, and
   does not care enough to work it out. That indifference is the point.
3. **Ahdia is winning and paying for it in the same seconds.** She is holding
   five people accelerated through a fall and a fight, on a body at thirty
   percent, in a suit built for somebody else. She never lands a blow herself.
   She sways in the middle of her own victory.

## BEATS (order fixed; texture yours; no scene breaks)

1. **The drop.** They come off the cornice, the fire escape, the alley mouth
   and the wreck of the bus, and they answer the cry in the air. Kain watches
   five people fall at him and reads it, at first, as suicide.
2. **DO NOT RESTAGE THE SUIT.** Ch26 already staged it — she held it up, swung
   it down to slam it, and was already wearing it. **That beat is spent.** She
   is in the oversized suit when this chapter opens: too big in the shoulders,
   sleeves long, still barefoot, blood soaked through the collar. Kain may
   register the suit as a fact he did not see her put on. He does not dwell.
3. **The acceleration, seen from outside.** The falling fighters speed up.
   **Kain can see the cost on her this time** — the sway, the shaking, the
   breathing — where before it was invisible. Weakened, and fast enough
   anyway. The Go Squad become blurs.
4. **The four takedowns, in order and in character.** Battlea into Ogden like
   a car crash. Crimson Sable into Philips, the street shattering. Edgar tries
   to dodge; Night Knight corrects mid-strike at an impossible angle and
   catches him centre mass. Orlansky raises a guard and Nightingale goes
   straight through it. **Gloom Girl does not strike anybody** — she blinks
   out mid-fall, reappears beside Firas, flips off the entire intersection and
   takes them both out of it. Four down; five standing in a ring around the
   girl in the centre.
5. **"What are you?"** and she does not answer, because she has nothing left
   to answer with.
6. **The finishing work.** Ogden claws up out of his crater and Crimson
   Sable's staff takes his temple. Philips crawls for the nearest fighter and
   goes down to a low sweep and Gloom Girl from above. Edgar surges at
   Nightingale and **the team intercepts before he makes three steps —
   protect the medic** — Night Knight into a restraint hold, Crimson Sable
   following. Orlansky is last, the young one, and he has watched all of it;
   he swings wild and all five converge, each opening the next one's line.
   **Written as competence, not slaughter.** They are taking people down.
7. **The victory, and the smell of it.** Standing, breathing hard, injured,
   alive. Ozone and copper and something burnt, pavement dust hanging.
8. **THE TURN.** Sickly green light — the shade of the Heart — comes up out of
   Ogden. Not steam, not smoke: **extraction.** His back arches, then slack,
   and the husk is smaller than the man was. The light crosses the pavement
   like iron to a magnet. It hits Kain wet, and it is **organic**, and it goes
   in. Then Philips, Edgar, Orlansky — four streams converging on one body.
   The air goes cold as it passes.
9. **Kain's understanding, delivered flat.** He distributed the fragments. He
   made them weapons. **He made them bait.** No monologue, no relish — the
   satisfaction of a line item resolving. The Heart is whole again.
10. **Nightingale sees it first** and screams spans 3–4, and there is nothing
    to stop; the light is already moving.
11. **"We killed them."** Unattributed. Then the four husks, and what they were
    before he twisted them: officers, with names and families. **Kain notes
    that this is what has broken them, and finds it inefficient of them.**
12. **His thanks.** Spans 6–7, in a voice that has become thunder. The most
    frightening thing in the chapter is that he is being sincere and polite.
13. **The growth does not stop.** Twenty-five feet, twenty-eight, thirty-one,
    thirty-five, thirty-eight, forty. Integration accelerating past the point
    of usefulness — **the Heart is consuming him and he has not noticed yet,
    or has priced it and accepted.** Skin translucent, bone visible beneath.
14. **END ON AHDIA, seen from Kain's height.** Swaying in the centre of her
    team, drowning in a suit built for her brother, blood through the collar.
    She turned five people into weapons. She saved them. **And she fed the
    monster.** Do not soften it and do not let anyone say it out loud.
