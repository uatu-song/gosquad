The world ran backward. People walked in reverse along the sidewalk, heels first and easy and wrong, and the rain lifted off the pavement and climbed smoothly back into the sky. A car rolled backward past her, and somebody’s spilled coffee jumped out of the gutter and folded itself neatly back into a cup and then into a waiting hand.

Ahdia reached for the Seed and nothing happened. Blue light pulsed under her skin with her heartbeat, and every single pulse patiently rewound the street by another second. She was doing this and she had absolutely no idea how to stop.

The feeling arrived without words, which was the Seed’s whole grammar.

Wrong direction.

She tried to argue with it, but you could not argue with a feeling.

You only ever pull. Slow. Stop. Backward. What if you pushed?

The world inverted, and she fell down through layers of frozen moments stacked like photographs. The warehouse mid-explosion with its orange bloom held at full width, Gloom Girl hanging above the bridge with her coat spread wide, the police ambush with its bullets parked politely in the air. Each layer dissolved as she dropped through it and she felt every dissolve as a small theft she had no way to report.

You can’t freeze moments. Only delay them.

She landed in a hallway. It ran ahead of her farther than she could see, blue doors on the left, red doors on the right. She had only ever opened blue ones and she knew that the way you know things in dreams, completely and without evidence.

One blue door stood open on Montana, on the campground and its pine and woodsmoke and a girl of eight sitting on a log with her shoes untied. Herself, obviously.

“You’ve been stuck here. We all have.” Little Ahdia said it without looking up from the log.

“You keep looking back. What if you looked forward instead?”

Behind adult Ahdia, a red door swung open. Warm light spilled out and everything inside the light moved forward. Forward was the one direction she had spent years refusing.

“I’m scared,” she admitted, because lying inside your own dream takes effort.

“Good. That means you’re moving.”

Then something else came down the hallway. It was far too big for the hallway and it came anyway, the way weather comes, curious and unhurried, observing her the whole time it passed and never once stopping for her. The red door pulsed and she reached for it.

Everything cracked into red light, hot and forward and fast. Something touched her mind, neither hostile nor kind, and she saw herself spread across infinite moments, every possible Ahdia moving forward and every one of them reaching for the red door. Then it pulled back and was gone. A voice followed her out, cold and final, and it did not belong to the Seed.

“Wake up.”

She jolted upright, gasping. White ceiling and screaming monitors, CADENS medical, and the IV torn out of her arm though she had no memory of tearing it.

“Hey, hey—” Ryu was right there with his hands raised. “You’re okay. You’re—”

She grabbed his wrist before her brain caught up with her hands.

“Whoa, easy.” He clearly hadn’t slept since the docks. His glasses sat crooked and he made no move to fix them. “You’ve been out for three hours.”

“What happened?” she asked, once her breathing let her.

“You passed out. Ruth said—” He stopped himself and adjusted his glasses, his tell, the thing he did while choosing a safer sentence. “You were in pretty bad shape when they brought you in.”

“Where’s Ruth?”

He hesitated a beat too long for it to mean nothing.

“Where’s Ruth?” she repeated, watching him decide what to leave out.

“She left. With the others.”

“Left where?”

Instead of answering that one, he simply pointed at the wall monitor.

Downtown was burning in high definition. Smoke poured steadily from buildings with whole pieces missing, and in the intersection stood Kain, twenty feet of him and climbing. He was still person-shaped and he had too many joints in every limb, and his skin had gone translucent, and the Tamois Heart pulsed in his chest like a second sun. It was framed like every kaiju shot she had ever watched from a couch, half a lifetime of giant monsters in miniature, and her brain served that up now as if it helped. He grabbed a car and threw it through a storefront.

“What the hell is that?” she demanded of the wall monitor.

“Kain.” Ryu let the name sit there for a long second. “He used the Heart. The Tamois Heart. He’s been growing for the past hour.”

“Ruth and the team went after him,” he said, watching her and not the screen. “About thirty minutes ago.”

“They don’t have powers,” Ahdia said, already doing the arithmetic on thirty minutes and losing.

“I know.”

“They’re going to die.”

She swung her legs off the bed and the floor was cold and her feet were bare and neither fact slowed her down at all.

“Ahdia—” Ryu said, and she ignored him.

“I need the treatment.” She stood and her knees held, barely. “Now.”

“You can’t. The treatment isn’t stable yet.” He put himself squarely between her and the door. “Using your powers will—”

“Give me the treatment.”

“You don’t understand,” Bourn said from the doorway. She came through in full tactical gear, which answered several questions nobody had asked yet. “You need eight more doses. Three weeks between each one. That’s six months.”

“I don’t have six months,” Ahdia replied, and Bourn didn’t blink.

“You collapsed at the docks. Cellular degradation. You’ve been unconscious for eighteen hours.” Ryu gestured at the IV port still taped to her arm. “Ruth developed a treatment. We administered the first dose. You’re stabilized, but still severely damaged. Your cells need time to integrate each dose. If you take them too soon—”

“We’re out of time,” Bourn said with her eyes on the monitor.

Out of time. The phrase hung in the room while the monitor showed Kain pulling another car off the street, and the team was somewhere between here and there on foot, unarmed in every sense that counted.

Her hand went to the Seed without asking her first. It sat warm under her sternum where it always sat, waiting. She shoved past Ryu to the supply cabinet and took all eight autoinjectors. She lined them up on the bed, sat down beside them and closed her eyes.

The Seed flared red.

Red, where it had only ever been blue. She was not slowing her own time anymore. She was accelerating it, spending the three weeks between doses in seconds, and her body understood the price before her mind finished the math. Every monitor in the room screamed at once.

“She’s accelerating!” Ryu yelled at the monitors. “She’s giving herself the three weeks!”

The first dose went in, and the whole room outside her slowed to a crawl only she could see. Ryu counted on fingers that moved glacially, and he tapped a pen for a metronome she heard faintly as if underwater, then through a wall and then through an ocean somewhere.

He typed on a tablet and held it up to her.

RATIO = 1:2000

Second dose, then third, and heat crawled through her at a depth no hand could reach.

“Energy expenditure is exceeding regeneration. It’s a net loss.” Ryu read the numbers off the monitor as fast as they changed.

TOO FAST = CELLS BURN

“It’s not like there’s a dial I can turn!” she shouted from inside the red. She looked at her hands, shaking at the wrong frame rate. “Or a needle gauge to monitor!”

TRY 1:1500

The fifth and sixth went in while her nose bled, and she let it bleed. The ratio kept slipping away from her dose by dose, and the Seed flickered red-blue-red under the gown while her body screamed at every scale she had a word for.

“Even at perfect ratio, she’s only getting fifty percent max,” Ryu told Bourn behind her. “It’s a zero-sum problem.”

He waved the tablet back and forth through her red haze.

SLOW DOWN

The seventh and the eighth went in, and she finally let go of the red and collapsed forward.

Ryu and Bourn caught her with one arm each, and the room came back at normal speed, which felt obscene.

“Did it work?” she asked, hoarse, with all her weight in their arms.

“First few integrated. Last ones barely worked. Maybe thirty percent total regeneration.” Ryu checked the monitors twice as though a second look might raise the number.

“Is that enough?” Bourn asked in the tone of someone recalculating.

“I don’t know.”

On the feeds, the team was making contact with Kain. Thirty percent. She had run on less and she had never told anyone how much less.

“It has to be.” Ahdia stood in the hospital gown, barefoot, barely upright. She reached for the Seed, and it answered red, and the pull took her.

The med bay stood empty behind her, monitors flatlined over a stripped cabinet. Ryu and Bourn turned to the feeds and settled in to wait.

The light came first. A FAERIS drone materialized over the intersection and its beam swept down through the smoke, scanning. Then came the pull, air folding silently in on itself, and Ahdia stood in the middle of the ruined street.

Hospital gown. Bare feet on broken glass, hair loose and hands spread wide, and the Seed flaring blue through the thin fabric.

Kain froze mid-step, fist raised. His other hand stopped around the teenagers he had been holding, and they hung inside his grip, terrified and alive. The Tank cops froze with him, Edgar with an arm locked around Sable, Ogden’s fist inches from Battlea’s face, Philips and Orlansky caught mid-charge.

Everything else in the intersection kept moving. Smoke rolled on, a siren ran somewhere south, and a traffic light cycled to green over a street nobody would drive down again.

For three full seconds nobody moved at all. Then the civilians ran and the Go Squad didn’t. Knight pulled Battlea up without looking away from the girl in the gown. Sable slid down the wall and stayed where he landed. Gloom Girl stayed on her knees, and Ruth stood with a bruise spreading across her throat.

“Is that—” Knight said, still holding Battlea’s arm.

“Ahdia,” Ruth whispered, and the name barely cleared her throat.

Ahdia held all five of them in the freeze, Kain and his four, while the rest of the world went on moving around the stillness. Then she opened her eyes and counted her team, one injury at a time: Knight favoring one leg, Sable’s split knuckles, Gloom Girl shaking badly, the bruise on Ruth’s throat going purple in real time. They stared at the girl who should still have been unconscious.
