**THE CHAPTER'S REAL ASSIGNMENT.** Three jobs, and the third is the book's
title theme:

1. **The withheld name is now standing in the street.** Ch23 was the team
   interrogating Ruth about a person she would not name. This is not a
   repeat of that scene — it is the escalation, because now they can see
   her, and the withholding has a face and a nosebleed. Play the difference.
2. **Ruth's authority survives on competence, not forgiveness.** Nobody
   absolves her. They turn to her at the end because there is no one else
   and because she is the one who can build the plan. She gives them a plan
   instead of an apology. That is the correct instinct AND another
   withdrawal from the same account.
3. **Ahdia's speech is the thesis of the book.** "I didn't want you to know
   that someone like me had powers when you didn't . . . Like I didn't
   deserve it," answered by "even without me, you guys are, well . . .
   super." She is arguing that worth is not distributed by merit, while
   visibly dying of the cost of hers. Do not let the narration explain this.
   Let the blood do it.

**THE HARD PART — comedy and terror in the same sentence.** Ahdia deflects
with jokes the entire chapter, and the jokes are genuinely funny. She is also
bleeding freely, shaking, and failing. The narration must never choose. It
must not undercut a joke by explaining that it is a coping mechanism, and it
must not let a joke soften the physical facts. Ruth is a doctor: she can hear
a punchline and read a symptom in the same instant, and that is the register.

**REGISTER:** Ruth POV throughout, clinical and losing altitude. The chapter
is people talking in a frozen street, so the narration's job is pressure and
diagnosis, not scenery. Kain and the four Tanks are motionless furniture the
whole time — a horizon, not an event.

## BEATS (order fixed; texture yours; no scene breaks in this chapter)

1. **The drone.** Ahdia hip-bumps the FAERIS unit that carried her and tells
   it to go on to its mama. **It moves, and then it does not leave** — it
   settles over Tess's shoulder and stays, close, as though waiting for
   something. **Plant this and never explain it.** Do not attribute intent
   in the narration beyond what Ruth can see.
2. **The silence, and who breaks it.** Four people who buried this woman are
   looking at her. She breaks first, because she cannot stand being looked
   at, and she opens with a radio-caller bit. Leah's panic is real (span 4),
   Victor's deadpan is how he handles fear (5–8). This is the chapter's only
   easy stretch; let it be funny.
3. **Ruth goes clinical.** She crosses the distance in counted steps and
   runs the assessment she cannot help running — nosebleed, tremor, sweat,
   bare feet, gown, and a woman who should not be vertical. She asks about
   the treatment and gets a joke back (10–13). She lets the joke pass and
   keeps reading the patient.
4. **The correction — the chapter's engine turn.** Ahdia reveals she is not
   freezing anything. **She is accelerating everyone else.** Ch24 played it
   from outside as five giants held still; that reading was wrong and Ruth
   has to feel it be wrong. **Tess gets there first** (16) and Ahdia rewards
   her by name. Then Ruth's "So why haven't you?" and the answer: she found
   out fifteen minutes ago, and had to go find a drone to hitch a ride on.
5. **Silence, then Tess turns on Ruth.** Four accusations in a row (21–24)
   and Ruth opens her mouth and gets one syllable out. Then Victor's two
   (26–27), and his hands on the staff. **This is not ch23's scene.** In
   ch23 they were owed a name; now they are looking at her.
6. **Ahdia defends Ruth and gets the number wrong.** Span 29 says two weeks,
   maybe three. **THE TRUTH IS ONE DAY** (§7d: the docks were last night;
   Ruth has been at CADENS one day). Ahdia is not lying — she has lost
   eighteen hours, she has just run her own cells through three weeks in
   fifteen seconds, and time is genuinely broken for her. **Ruth knows the
   real number and lets it stand**, and the narration must register both
   that she noticed and why she says nothing: correcting it would help
   nobody, and the honest answer is worse. One day was enough.
7. **The speech.** Spans 34–36, uninterrupted, with the physical decline
   running underneath it — blood reaching her lip, the sway, the wipe with
   the back of the hand. This is the book's thesis spoken by the person
   least able to believe it. Give it air. Do not gloss it afterward.
8. **The fracture.** Ben's "We trusted you" — from the man who built four
   years of tactics on it. Ruth's half-sentence. Leah's two, sharp. Ruth
   feels the team coming apart and the narration should stay in her ribs
   for it, not in commentary.
9. **Victor cuts it off — pointing at Ahdia, not at them.** She is failing:
   bleeding freely, knees going, the Seed pulsing wrong through the gown.
   Her line is a request for a plan, delivered as a joke, and it is the most
   frightening thing in the chapter.
10. **They all look at Ruth.** Their tactician; their leader; the one who
    has been lying to them — and the honest span of that lie is a single
    day, which turns out to have been long enough. She is the one who still
    has to call it. **Nobody forgives her; they use her.**
11. **The plan.** Ruth works the mechanism out in public: if everyone is
    accelerated and Kain is not, then the thing they have been treating as
    a wall is a window. Her confirming question (45), Ahdia's nod. **Then
    the team gets it, each in character** — Ben the tactician, Leah despite
    her ribs, Victor's grip shifting on the staff, and Tess going last,
    because Tess has to move from fury to understanding and that costs her
    something. **END on "Then let's run."** Nothing after it.
