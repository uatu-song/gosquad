## BEATS (order fixed; texture yours; # between scenes)

1. **The fist that stops.** Ruth's eyes closed, the whistle, the wind — then
   nothing. She opens them into a frozen world: the fist inches away, the
   snarl locked, the man not blinking. Twenty feet off, a small figure with
   one hand out. "Ruth knew that posture." (Spans 1–2.)
2. **(#) Inside the bubble.** SIX METERS — the bible's radius, and Ruth can
   see its edge: "the difference between being underwater and surfacing."
   Two realities in one space; she stands at the seam. Ahdia crosses to her
   — each step costing, nose bleeding, left arm limp, tactical gear torn.
   The instruction not to move. Then the surgical work: Ahdia circles the
   Tank and places fingers at knee, shoulder blade, solar plexus, knee, and
   RUTH RECOGNIZES IT — a doctor marking incision points. The bubble
   flickers once (she can't hold it as steady as she used to); half a second
   of the world snapping back, then frozen again. The count to three, Ruth
   moved ten feet without moving, the four strikes landing at one instant,
   the gentle push that throws an enhanced man through a container wall.
   Every effect must obey the bible: dilation, stored force released on
   unfreeze, gentleness amplified. (Spans 3–13 or thereabouts, per the list.)
3. **The collapse and the diagnosis.** Ahdia drops; Ruth catches her in
   doctor mode — pulse racing, skin burning, pupils blown, breathing shallow.
   Then the sentence that ends the book's first half: the powers were her.
   Ruth's differential runs backward through every impossible thing — Tess's
   teleportation, Victor's strength, Ben's jumps, Leah's speed — and each
   one resolves into what it actually was. The bitter laugh. "He said I was
   already dead inside" (ch12's overheard line, paying off). She stops
   breathing. Ruth's kit has nothing for this: "This wasn't an injury. This
   was collapse."
4. **The extraction.** The radio: Ryu, by name, the word ASSET and Ruth's
   correction of it. A non-FAERIS drone with visible lights and a stretcher.
   Three minutes to CCPD. The choice offered flat. Ruth refuses to leave her
   side, and that is the whole character in one line. Then translocation —
   and Ruth experiences from outside what Ahdia experienced in 14a: light,
   inversion, the docks folding like origami, the vomit on arrival. She is
   the second person in the book to be moved this way, and the first to have
   no idea it was coming.
5. **The medical bay.** Impossible equipment; Ahdia lifted onto the table by
   automated arms. BOURN — "Director Overseer Bourn," which the reader (not
   Ruth) will connect to ch17's Codename: Overseer. Do NOT have Ruth or the
   narration make that connection; she has never heard the name. Bourn knows
   Ruth's whole file, including the conference that laughed at her.
   The four readouts, then the arithmetic that lands: eighteen months if she
   stops entirely, weeks at her current rate, four percent spent tonight
   saving Ruth specifically. Ruth's professional awe at telomere analysis in
   seconds is a luxury she sets aside — that's the character.
6. **(#) The offer.** An hour later, Ruth at the observation window. The
   broad strokes delivered flat (the Seed, the bonding, FAERIS amplification,
   the monitoring since the warehouse). Then the reveal that reframes Ruth
   herself: CADENS has watched HER for two years, since her first paper —
   the sidelined researcher was never invisible. Bourn's pitch, the lab, the
   isotopes. Ruth naming it exactly: "You want me to manage the lie."
   Bourn's correction. Ruth's terms: see the lab, full disclosure, and — the
   line that starts the book's back half — she tells her team nothing.
   The text she sends. The last look through the glass.
