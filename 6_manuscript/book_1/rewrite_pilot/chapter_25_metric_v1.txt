The drone came down through the smoke to Ahdia’s shoulder. “Go,” she said, and bumped it off with her hip. It drifted a foot and hung there, patiently. “Go on to your mama.”

It crossed the intersection and did not leave. It settled over Tess’s shoulder, close enough to touch. Tess went rigid.

Nobody spoke. Four people who had buried this woman stood in the wreck of Main Street and looked at her. Nobody looked away. She broke first. Ruth watched it happen: shoulders coming up, chin sideways, one hand rising in a wave.

“Uh . . . hi, guys, first-time caller, long-time fan, I’m Ahdia, Firas’s big sister.”

Leah’s fist tightened in Ben’s sleeve. Ruth read the panic there. “What the hell is going on? Are we dead? We’re dead, right?”

“Definitely not,” Victor said.

“Oh yeah? How do you know?”

“Because if we were dead, this would be a tropical beach and you’d be wearing . . .” He let his hand finish it slowly, a pass at the scorched armor. “. . . not that.”

Ruth crossed to her in eleven counted steps, and every step gave less of the girl and more of the patient: bare feet on broken glass, sweat through the gown at the spine, the blue light beating under it faster than any heart. Blood ran freely from one nostril. Both hands shook badly. The woman underneath should not have been vertical.

“We’re not dead,” Ruth said. Three words out of a bruised throat, and they cost more than three should. “How are you feeling? You took the rest of the treatment, didn’t you?”

“Yeah, what gave it away?”

“Well, you’re here at all for one.” Ruth watched the blood and not the face. “But couple that with this demonstration of this whole new ability . . .”

“Oh, about that, yeah, turns out I could do it all along.” She tipped her head at the giants without looking. “Fun fact, I’m not actually freezing Marky Mark and the Funky Bunch over there. I’m doing the other thing. Who knew?”

Ruth had let the jokes past and kept reading the patient. Now she understood what she had been standing inside. Kain was not stopped, and Edgar and Ogden and Philips and Orlansky were not stopped, and nothing over there was touched, and the difference was here, in her own arms and lungs and the four people beside her.

“You’re speeding us up?” Tess said.

“Oh, yeah, look at you, Gloom Girl, penny for the pretty lady,” Ahdia said.

“So why haven’t you?”

“To be fair, I just figured out that I could about fifteen minutes ago.” She rocked back on her heels and made it look deliberate. “Would have gotten here sooner but I had to find one of those guys to hitch a ride. They do a really good job of cleaning up their toys when they’re not using them, over there at CADENS HQ.”

The word landed in a street where nobody had ever heard it, and Tess picked it out of the air like a stone and came off her knees with it, and she did not stop.

“You knew about her?”

“This whole time?”

“You’ve been working with CADENS.”

“You knew. You’ve been lying.”

“I—”

“How long?” Victor said. His hand slid down the rebar until it found the balance. The knuckles went pale. “How long have you been lying to us?”

“Okay, whoa, hold on.” Ahdia put both palms up, and the shake in them was plainly visible. “This is not on Ruth. She didn’t know about me until, like, two weeks ago? Maybe three? Time’s been weird.”

One day. Ruth ran the span and it came out at one day, the docks last night and the labs this morning. She let the wrong number lie where it fell. Correcting it would buy nobody anything. The honest answer was worse, because one day had been enough.

“And she’s been trying to help me not die, so maybe we could table the whole trust-issues intervention until after we deal with Godzilla over there?”

“This isn’t—”

“It’s not her fault,” Ahdia said. Blood had reached her lip. She wiped it with the back of her hand and looked at the hand. “CADENS approached her. She didn’t ask for this. I’m the one who’s been—”

She stopped. Then she picked it up again, evenly, and gave it to all four of them.

“Look, I know what you’re thinking. Why didn’t she tell you? Why all the secrets? And the answer is . . . me. I asked her not to. Because I’m Firas’s sister, the one who couldn’t even leave her apartment for dinner, and you guys—you’re out here actually doing things. Saving people. Being, like, actual heroes.”

The sway went through her shoulders and out again and she rode it standing, barely.

“And I didn’t want you to know that someone like me had powers when you didn’t. That felt . . . wrong. Like the universe screwed up the distribution or something. Like I didn’t deserve it.”

The nosebleed had found her chin, and nothing in the street moved except her mouth and the smoke.

“But you guys? You’ve been doing this with no powers at all. Just . . . guts and gear and giving a damn. So yeah, I kept it secret. And yeah, Ruth helped me do that. At first it was about being painfully, audaciously, anti-social and my anger towards Firas but then it became about something else. I started wanting you guys to look all enhanced and amazing because . . . because even without me, you guys are, well . . . super.”

“We trusted you.” Ben said it. Four years of tactics rested on that sentence, and Ruth felt the floor tilt.

“You still can—”

“Can we?” Leah was upright now and holding her ribs, and the second one came sharper. “Because it seems like you’ve been keeping a lot of secrets.”

“We don’t have time for this!” Victor said. He was pointing at Ahdia, not at Ruth. Her knees were traveling, and the blood came steadily onto the gown, and the light in her chest pulsed twice out of sequence and skipped. She smiled at them brightly, with red teeth.

“I can hold this,” she said. “But not forever. So if someone wants to, I don’t know, make a plan? That would be great.”

All four of them turned to Ruth. Nobody offered her anything. They looked at her because she built the plans, because no one else was standing, and because a liar with a plan beat four honest people without one.

“Wait.” They had been treating the stillness as a wall. It was not a wall. Kain stood inside his own second like a photograph of a man, fist up, and they stood outside it for as long as she could give them. “So you’re saying we’re all moving at unimaginable speed while they stand still in normal time?”

Ahdia nodded briefly, and the nod nearly took her down. Ben went to the giants and read the gaps. Leah let go of her ribs. Victor’s grip shifted on the rebar until the weight sat right. Tess came last, because Tess had to carry her fury the whole distance to the same place, and it cost her.

“Then let’s run.”
