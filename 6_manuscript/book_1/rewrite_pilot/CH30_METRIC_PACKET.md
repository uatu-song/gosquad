## THE FIVE THINGS THIS CHAPTER MUST GET RIGHT

**1. SHE DECLINES THE BADGE.** She picks it up, feels the weight, puts it back.
**She does not get fixed and she does not get recruited.** The steward run:
she walks out furious about the price, certain she would rather it had been
her. Do not let the chapter resolve her.

**2. RUTH ENDS IT AND THEN DOESN'T.** She is trying to disband the team because
she cannot lose another one, and **she is the only reason any of them are
alive.** Leah names it — *you've been carrying us* — and Ruth's grief shows
ONLY as function. **She never breaks, never cries it out, and gets no
catharsis.** She resists leadership and ends the book holding it.

**3. THE ENDPOINT RULE, STILL (§5b).** In sections 1–5, **every character
believes Firas is dead and believes Ahdia was rescued from dying.** No one
speculates otherwise. **The epilogue is the ONLY place the truth leaks**, and
only because the woman in the tank room already knows it — and even she does
not know he survives. Ahdia tells NOBODY about the hallway.

**4. THE LIE IS THE CHAPTER'S SPINE (Director-ruled).** Ahdia faked the eighth
dose in ch24. **Section 3 is where CADENS finds out** — the scan says she left
at a third of cellular recovery and walked into it anyway. Ryu's face falls.
**Bourn calls it reckless and effective.** Nobody ever confronts Ahdia about
it, and she never mentions it. She keeps that, like she keeps the hallway.

**5. THE EPILOGUE IS THE BOOK'S REAL ENDING.** Kain is alive. Tank 47 was a
body, not a man. The corridor, the tanks, dozens of his own face at different
ages. **The woman knows about the hallway and names the thing that looked at
Ahdia**, and gives the order that changes the series: *plans change; let me
worry about the girl.* **Play it cold.** He is a man being told his opinion is
not required.

## BEATS (order fixed; texture yours; `#` between the six sections)

**SECTION 1 — Ahdia.** Cold glass on her forehead in the command centre,
twenty-four hours out. Fog, and watching it go. **Ryu** behind her — she has
dropped the *Dr.* somewhere in twelve hours of debriefing, and that is the only
comment made on it. The team checked out clean; Victor's arm is the pull, not
the forces. **Bourn's office**: same desk, same chair, the woman who sent her
in without backup. The condolence, and Ahdia taking it flat. The correction —
**"I had the Go Squad"** — and the warning that it should not give anyone
ideas. **The badge on the desk.** She picks it up, feels the weight of a life
that is not hers, puts it back. The handshake. Then Bourn's last question about
how close she came, and **Ahdia considers telling her about the hallway and
does not.** She smiles instead and leaves.

**SECTION 2 — Ruth.** The penthouse. Carl Tucker on the television calling it a
gas leak and a prank. The suits are gone. **Ruth ends the Go Squad**, and her
reasons are all correct: Kain is untouched, already rebuilding, the campaign
rolling; the drives will land on the PAC and his hands stay clean; Ahdia has
vanished and left only a key and a bank account; **and they are not the Go
Squad without Firas, because it was always his.** Then Leah tells her what she
has been doing — holding them together, organising the chain, carrying them —
and Ruth's voice cracks once on *we just lost Firas* and she keeps going.
**That is the entire visible grief. Do not give her more.** Then Ahdia in the
doorway, hands in her coat, **and a duffel bag at her feet.**

**SECTION 3 — Ryu.** Bourn's office. He fixes his tie and lies about being
anxious. The scans. **She lied to us. The eighth treatment.** He works out what
that means and his face goes. A third of cellular recovery, walking into a
Terminus-level event. **Reckless and effective.** Then Kain, alive, on a
podium, spinning it as an impersonation and a bioweapon — and CADENS cannot
touch him, because he has lawyers and a story that does not require anyone to
believe in cosmic artifacts. **The authorization.** Requested six hours before;
arrived forty-seven minutes ago, fourteen hours after it was over, thanking
them for their patience. **They wanted plausible deniability.** Bourn's answer:
document everything, build the file, wait — and hope that next time a depressed
girl with time powers and a death wish is not the only line of defence.

**SECTION 4 — Ruth.** Ahdia's thanks, and the team talking over it. Then the
speech, which is the book's thesis returned to the people who taught it to her:
they saved her, they pulled her out of a collapsing dimension, **"You're
super."** She got off the couch because of them. This city needs them, and so
does everything else. **"You're the only family I have and my family is
unbeatable."** Then Tess, Victor, Leah and Ben each take it up, and they all
look at Ruth. **"This doesn't work without you, boss."** Ruth's terms: start
over, from the bottom, ready for cataclysms and cops and Kain. Leah's *oh my*
and Victor's headlock. **Ruth bites her lip and thinks about it — and the
chapter does not make her say yes out loud.**

**SECTION 5 — Ruth on the tower, then Ahdia.** Five figures at the highest
point of the city at sunset, in **their own gear, not CADENS armour.** Ahdia on
comms from the **MCC on the penthouse roof** — cloaked, humming, a base and not
a vehicle. Ruth's speech at the ledge: yesterday they lost someone, today they
keep going, because people still need help, **because they go when nobody else
will.** *No better place.* / *No better time.* **Ruth dives. The others
follow.** Wingsuits, patrol zones, splitting off. Ruth levels out over the
avenue, tucks, lands on a rooftop across from a robbery — **and then levitates,
just slightly.** The gift, still working, running through stolen equipment.
**She says thank you into the radio.** And Ahdia, surrounded by monitors she is
not authorised to touch, in a machine she took: Bourn wanted her to wait and
document and build files, **and people still needed help tonight.**

**SECTION 6 — EPILOGUE, Kain.** The press conference, the smile of a man who
has already won, the choreography of aides. **The elevator goes down** — past

singularity took. *An acceptable loss.* Then the woman: white coat, ageless,
proportions slightly wrong. Their exchange about redundancy and vessels. Then

thought. **"Plans change."** He is told to focus on the election and let her
worry about the girl. **She leaves. He stands alone among sleeping versions of
himself.** END THERE. Nothing after it.
