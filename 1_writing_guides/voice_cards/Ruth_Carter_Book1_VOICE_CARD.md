## 1 · Five things her sentences DO

1. **Sort before they describe.** Any scene enters her as a triage list — what
   can be saved, in what order, assigned to whom. *"Three down, all breathing."*
   The sorting IS the sentence structure, not a thing the sentence reports.
2. **Price things clinically, unprompted.** Bodies, water, time, risk. She
   cannot look at harm without costing it. *"Ten minutes to hypothermia in a
   healthy adult; she'd spent tonight's adrenaline already, so call it seven."*
3. **Give orders in the imperative, short, with the reason attached only if it
   speeds compliance.** *"Ben, you're walking, so you've got Leah."* Assignment
   by capacity — the triage habit applied to people she loves.
4. **Reach for the medical analogy when something is hard to say directly.**
   Enhancement as transfused blood; loss as pressure off a cuffed artery;
   panic managed in *"the tone she used on interns at a crashing bedside."*
   The metaphor is her compartmentalization, visible.
5. **Register competence and cost in the same breath.** *"Her diagnosis and her
   fear arrived in the same breath."* She never gets to feel one without
   the other, and the sentence shouldn't let her.

## 2 · Five things her sentences NEVER do

1. **Never explain a mechanism to the reader.** She is not the exposition
   engine. If the plot needs explaining, someone else explains it or it stays
   clipped. (Her embodiment names this the plot-explainer trap.)
2. **Never reach for metaphor outside her domains.** Medicine, ER logistics,
   residency, drills. No weather, no poetry, no cosmic register. If the image
   isn't something a trauma doctor would reach for, it isn't hers.
3. **Never narrate her own feeling by naming it.** No "she felt afraid." The
   fear appears as the thing she does next, or as a clinical observation
   arriving alongside it. **Book 1 has no Ruth catharsis** — the seal leaks; it
   does not break.
4. **Never soften an order or apologize for one.** *"That's an order."* No
   hedging, no "I think we should." She negotiates as an equal; she never
   supplicates.
5. **Never be frictionless.** She is sleep-deprived, running a triple life, and
   her body must show it. Competence without cost reads as a different
   character.

## 3 · Three sample lines (the target register)

> "Then we walk away. Nobody bleeds for inventory."
> — command + ethic in nine words, no explanation appended

> "Screaming meant breathing. Ribs, scapula, survivable."
> — the medical eye running under terror; assessment as reflex

> "You were trained before you were ever enhanced, and the training is still in
> your hands. Regroup at rally two. Keep moving. Now."
> — bedside cadence weaponized into a field order

## 4 · The one thing she can't say in this book

**That the powers are Ahdia's.** Before Ch19 she doesn't know — so no
Ruth sentence may hint, foreshadow, or be too curious about the mechanism.
After Ch19 she knows and is carrying it as a co-conspirator — so every scene
with the team has an unspoken load, and she manages the truth rather than
telling it. *"She distrusted every piece of the arrangement and used it
anyway"* is the pre-reveal ceiling: suspicion filed, never pursued.

She also cannot grieve Firas out loud. The ring stays unprocessed. Her grief
shows only as **over-function** — she does more, never less.
