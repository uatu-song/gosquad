## 1 · Five things her sentences DO  *(all examples ch05–10, clean source)*

1. **Sort before she describes.** A scene enters her as a triage list — what can
   be saved, in what order, assigned to whom. She names the priority before she
   names the condition: *"Everyone on comms."* (ch10) · *"Stand down, Gloomy."*
   (ch09) · *"Put eyes on, Gloomy."* (ch09)

   **Measurement note (2026-07-27):** this is a behavior, not a syntax licence.
   ed1 Ruth-POV action prose measures **10.4 burst/1K against mixed-POV action
   at 10.2** — statistically identical. Her fragmentation is **not** elevated in
   the author's own writing. **§1.1 may not be cited to justify a fragment.**

2. **Price a body in time and prognosis.** She answers questions about harm with
   a duration and a restriction: *"For full recovery? Weeks minimum. You won't
   be cleared for patrol for at least a month. Maybe longer."* (ch05) ·
   *"Your recovery rate is slowing."* (ch05)

3. **Give orders bare.** Imperative, three to six words, no preamble and no
   softener: *"Go for audio."* (ch09) · *"Confirmed, Gloom Girl."* (ch10) ·
   *"Copy, Battlea."* (ch10) The order is the whole sentence.

4. **Use clinical vocabulary literally, in dialogue as much as narration.**
   *"Tactical body armor was my idea because broken ribs aren't very treatable,
   so avoiding that is huge."* (ch05) The terminology is her job, not a figure
   of speech.

   ⚠️ **Do not write medical metaphor.** ed1 uses medical language figuratively
   **twice in 15,735 words of Ruth narration (0.13/1K)**; CH18 v5 uses it three
   times in 2,014 words (**1.49/1K**, ~11×). *"Enhancement as transfused
   blood," "pressure off a cuffed artery"* are **cold-agent inventions** that
   this card previously codified as her voice. Ration to ~one per chapter, or cut.

5. **Delegate the work she cannot do herself, without ceremony.** *"You built a
   good team, Firas. Good enough to run without you while you heal."* (ch05) —
   assessment, decision, and reassurance in two sentences, none of them about
   her own feelings.

## 1b · Dialogue vs narration — what actually separates them

Tested against ed1 (12 Ruth-POV chapters), 2026-07-26. **The expected split did
not hold and is not in this card.** Clinical vocabulary appears at essentially
the same density in both registers — dialogue 3.8/1K, narration 4.1/1K, a ratio
of 1.07. She talks like a doctor out loud as much as she thinks like one.

What separates them is **compression, not vocabulary**:

| | ed1 measure | rule |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue | mean **6.6 words/sentence** | clipped to the bone; orders, confirmations, one-line questions |
| Narration | full sentences | carries the sorting, the assessment, the cost |

So: medical words go in her mouth freely. What never goes in her mouth is
**length**. If a Ruth line runs past about a dozen words, it is either doing
someone else's exposition or it is not Ruth.

## 2 · Five things her sentences NEVER do

1. **Never explain a mechanism to the reader.** She is not the exposition
   engine. If the plot needs explaining, someone else explains it or it stays
   clipped. (Her embodiment names this the plot-explainer trap.)
2. **Never reach for metaphor outside her domains.** Medicine, ER logistics,
   residency, drills. No weather, no poetry, no cosmic register. If the image
   isn't something a trauma doctor would reach for, it isn't hers.
3. **Never narrate her own feeling by naming it.** No "she felt afraid." The
   fear appears as the thing she does next, or as a clinical observation
   arriving alongside it. **Book 1 has no Ruth catharsis** — the seal leaks; it
   does not break.
4. **Never soften an order or apologize for one.** *"I said no body armor."*
   (ch05) — a repeated order, with the repetition carrying the whole rebuke.
   No hedging, no "I think we should." She negotiates as an equal; she never
   supplicates. *(Earlier drafts of this card cited "That's an order." — that
   line is ch23/CH18-draft, not clean-window.)*
5. **Never be frictionless.** She is sleep-deprived, running a triple life, and
   her body must show it. Competence without cost reads as a different
   character.

> **"Everyone on comms."** (ch10) · **"Stand down, Gloomy."** (ch09) ·

> *Command register.* Three to five words. No preamble, no justification, no

> **"For full recovery? Weeks minimum. You won't be cleared for patrol for at
> least a month. Maybe longer."** (ch05)
> *Clinical pricing.* She restates the question as a duration, then attaches

> **"I said no body armor."** (ch05) · **"Clearly not!"** (ch06)
> *Off-duty register.* Clipped to the point of dryness. Her warmth arrives as
> brevity and irritation, never as elaboration.

## 3b · Rejected lines — what got thrown out of the CH18 drafts, and why

Every one of the pipeline's craft constraints was earned by a discarded line.
These are failures the rules in §1–2 would NOT already catch.

> ❌ **"Alone, then."** / **"Triage, then."** — v3
> Not wrong about Ruth; wrong as habit. Two identical syntactic frames within a
> page. The epigram lands the paragraph's meaning FOR the reader, and at this
> density it stops reading as her cognition and starts reading as generation
> tic. A paragraph may end on the concrete image with nothing appended.

> ❌ **"Different product line."** — v3, the Tank Cop
> A quip. He does not quip anywhere else, so the line belongs to a screenwriter
> rather than to a contractor. Applies to Ruth too: **she has no punchlines.**

> ❌ **"He drew back his fist."** — v3 ending
> Off-register for the character performing it. A tradesman adjusts his grip;
> he does not wind up. The final gesture must belong to whoever makes it, and
> pulp gestures survive in prose that has otherwise outgrown pulp.

> ❌ **"...grief with nowhere to send itself goes to the place where it happened."** — v4
> A *reconstruction* of a better line ("left at the only address grief could
> find: the place where it happened"). Rebuilt from a description of itself, it
> came back duller — "because" announces an explanation where the colon had
> performed a reveal. Preserve exact text; never re-derive a good line.

## 4 · The one thing she can't say in this book

**That the powers are Ahdia's.** Before Ch19 she doesn't know — so no
Ruth sentence may hint, foreshadow, or be too curious about the mechanism.
After Ch19 she knows and is carrying it as a co-conspirator — so every scene
with the team has an unspoken load, and she manages the truth rather than
telling it. *"She distrusted every piece of the arrangement and used it
anyway"* is the pre-reveal ceiling: suspicion filed, never pursued.

She also cannot grieve Firas out loud. The ring stays unprocessed. Her grief
shows only as **over-function** — she does more, never less.

## 5 · Register gap — a direction, not a target

> **Register gap (v6, as of 2026-07-27).** v6 sits above the author's
> clean-window short-sentence rate. Five independent measurements this day
> placed the gap between 1.28× and 1.71×; the interval did not narrow under
> refinement and no point estimate survived scrutiny. Treat the gap as real,
> modest, and unquantified. **This is a direction, not a target. Do not
> optimize toward a figure.**

A sixth measurement — blind labelling by an uninvolved agent, corpus identity
stripped — returned 1.53×, inside the same interval and no narrower. The
interval is the finding.

**Why no number is printed here:** a cold agent optimizes toward whatever
figure appears in this card, regardless of what is known about it. The five
values (1.28 / 1.61 / 1.71 / 1.44 / 1.31, plus the blind 1.53) moved twice in
opposite directions, and the last two shifts were labelling-judgment errors
rather than tool errors — the residual uncertainty is in whoever decides what
counts, not in the instrument. Publishing a point estimate would hand an agent
false precision to chase.

**Directional guidance that IS safe to act on:** shorter is not better. The
author's action prose is *less* fragmented than his domestic prose. If a draft
reads as clipped, that is the disease direction, not the author's.

**Other measures** (also directional): em-dash — author runs ~1.3/1K against
the infected chapters' ~16; keep it rare. Ellipsis — the author uses it in
*dialogue* (~11/1K of dialogue) where speakers hesitate or trail off; drafts
have used it at zero. Zero-instance markers (R103 "in her/his chest", R104
"X's voice was") — the author never uses these; neither should a draft.

## 6 · Maintenance rule (the one that governs this card)

**Nothing enters a voice card unless it was measured against ed1 ch1–11 first.**

Every refuted premise in this card's history was a claim about the author's
voice inferred from the *drafts*. The drafts are the contaminated artifact. The
card's own §1.4 briefly codified "reach for the medical analogy" — a
cold-agent invention running at 11× the author's rate — and one generation
later it would have been laundering an AI artifact as canon. That is precisely
what `GOSQUAD_PROSE_VOICE.md` has been doing since it was written.

This rule applies to Director notes, crew analysis, and agent output equally.
Measure first. Cite the chapter. Then write the rule.

### `GOSQUAD_PROSE_VOICE.md` — a doctrine problem, not a register problem

It was never mainly about the document's own prose style. It issues **live
instructions that countermand `canon/series/RULES.yaml` inside the same context
window**, and those instructions win, because they read as permission and are
more specific than a lint rule:

- *"Fragments land as beats, not errors. **Don't 'fix' them.**"*
- an entire section headed **"Em-Dashes for Pivots"**
- *"Period-Separated Lists … staccato inventory"*
- **Named style exemplars: ch01, ch22, ch27.** ch22 = 88.0 burst / 16.4
  em-dash; ch27 = 104.3 / 20.5. Both post-ch12. Only ch01 is the author's.
- *"66% dialogue"* — **unfounded at every level of measurement.** Author
  ch1–11: 24.4% of words. AI-era ch12–30: 34.6%. Whole book: 31.2%. Sentences
  containing dialogue: 30.9%. No reading of the manuscript yields 66%.
  CH18 v5 shipped at 21% — *correct*, against a guide demanding nearly 3× that,
  and no gate measured it either way.
- *"12-word average sentence"* — **this one checks out** (author ch1–11: 12.3
  mean, 9 median). Not everything in the document is invented, which is why it
  needs rebuilding rather than deleting.

**Likely history:** the infection begins around ch12; someone later writes a
style guide by observing the book's most distinctive-feeling chapters; those
chapters are ch22 and ch27. The guide did not catch the infection. **It
canonized it, and has been transmitting it ever since** — as load-slot #4 in
every packet. A cold agent told to imitate ch27 reproduces the disease no
matter how many tic gates it passes.

**Status: pulled. Rebuild required, not edits.** Before it returns: strike
ch22/ch27 (keep ch01), delete the fragments-are-not-errors line and the em-dash
