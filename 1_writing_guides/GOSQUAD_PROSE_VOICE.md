# Go Squad Series - Prose Voice Guide

**Purpose:** Capture the specific syntactic signatures and voice patterns of Go Squad prose. Load before any drafting or revision work. This complements the metrics-based `GO_SQUAD_WRITING_STYLE_GUIDE.md`.

**Philosophy:** Voice trumps technical polish. When grammar and ear conflict, trust the ear.

---

## Core Voice Principles

1. **Density is a feature** - Every scene is load-bearing. Compressed prose, nothing wasted.
2. **Embody, don't articulate** - Characters live the theme; they don't explain it.
3. **Trust the reader** - No over-explanation of emotional beats. Show through action.
4. **Fast pacing is the default** - 66% dialogue, 12-word average sentences, fragments for impact.

## Syntactic Signatures (Series-Wide)

### Fragments for Impact

Fragments land as beats, not errors. Don't "fix" them.

```
Perfect.

He moved. Fast. Faster than human.

Not a question. A statement.
```

Use fragments for:
- Emotional beats
- Realizations
- Pacing punctuation
- Action climaxes

---

### Dialogue-Heavy Rhythm

66% of Book 1 is dialogue. This is intentional.

```
"What are you wearing?" she finally managed to ask.
"Can we…" Firas let out as he breathed heavily, wincing. "… talk about that later? Can you please get Ruth?"
"Ruth? No, baby bro, we need to get you to the hospital," she protested.
"No!" he yelled, followed by a distressed groan. "No… Ruth. No hospital."
```

Rules:
- Most lines under 150 characters
- Contractions always ("won't", "she'd", "they're")
- Interruptions with ellipses or em-dashes
- Action beats over dialogue tags ("He ducked" not "he said, ducking")

---

### Period-Separated Lists

Short declarative lists creating staccato inventory. Each object gets weight.

```
Three exits. Two visible weapons. One guy who kept touching his waistband.

On the table: a utility blade. A bottle of gin. A hand mirror.
```

Period-separated. Not comma-separated.

---

### Em-Dashes for Pivots

Em-dashes interrupt thought, pivot mid-sentence, or insert commentary.

```
—great time to appreciate the square footage, Ahdia—

—which, okay, wasn't technically a question, but the subtext was pretty clear—
```

Em-dashes are interruptions. Commas are pauses. Know the difference.

---

### Action Scene Rhythm

From Book 1's strongest action sequences:

```
They fell. Second story. Thirty feet of empty air. Garden approaching fast.
Tess teleported mid-fall. They appeared back in the office for a split
second—she grabbed Sable's arm, surrounded by guards—then teleported again.
Now four of them falling. Ground rushing up. Tess teleported again.
```

Structure:
1. Setup in 1-2 sentences
2. Rapid action beats (fragments OK)
3. Character reactions mid-action
4. Sensory details (sight, sound, impact)
5. Consequences immediate
6. **No long descriptions during action**

---

### Internal Monologue Patterns

**Self-Interruption:**
```
Her brain did that thing where it cycled through every movie death scene
she'd ever watched. This is fine. Totally fine. Just like that Grey's
Anatomy episode. Except real. And my brother.
```

**Repetition Under Load:**
```
"Okay. Okay okay okay."
"This is fine. Totally fine."
"Oh god, oh god, oh god!"
```

**Both/And Contradictions:**
```
"Oh shit!" But also—"Yes!"
```

Two reactions at once. Neither cancelled. Don't resolve into one feeling.

## Tense Architecture

- **Present tense** for immediate action and high tension
- **Past tense** for normal narrative flow
- **Past perfect** for backstory/flashback within scenes

Consistency within scenes. Intentional shifts between scenes.

---

## POV Rules (Close Third)

Everything filtered through POV character's perception.

**Not this (neutral camera):**
> The office was sparse. A desk, two chairs, a filing cabinet.

**This (POV-filtered):**
> Three objects. Desk, two chairs, filing cabinet. She categorized the room
> in the time it took to cross the threshold.

What the character notices reveals character. What they miss also reveals character.

## What NOT to Write

### Over-Explained Emotion
```
❌ She felt guilty about what happened. The weight of it pressed down on her.
✓  Twenty-three people. She'd saved twenty-three people. [The count IS the guilt]
```

### Thesis Statements
```
❌ "The math didn't balance. The math was never going to balance."
✓  [Just show the math not balancing. Trust the reader.]
```

### Softened Dark Statements
```
❌ "parents who passed away"
✓  "dead parents"
```

### Triple-Adjective Staccato (overused)
```
❌ "His voice was flat. Professional. Wrong."
[Works once per scene. More than that becomes mechanical.]
```

### Lecture Mode
```
❌ Characters explaining themes in dialogue
✓  Characters LIVING the themes through action
```

## Voice Checklist

Before submitting Ahdia POV prose:

- [ ] Loaded `Ahdia_voice_sample.md`?
- [ ] At least one tangent or self-interruption?
- [ ] Pop culture references varied in structure (not all similes)?
- [ ] Repetition loop during high-stress moment?
- [ ] Dark statement without softening?
- [ ] Both/and contradiction if emotionally complex?

Before submitting any POV prose:

- [ ] Could this have been written about any protagonist? (If yes, voice is missing)
- [ ] Is there a thesis statement explaining what the scene showed? (Cut it)
- [ ] More than 2 triple-adjective staccatos? (Vary rhythms)
- [ ] Dialogue percentage above 50% in action scenes?
- [ ] Fragments used for impact moments?
